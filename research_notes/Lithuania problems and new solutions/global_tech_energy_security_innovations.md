## Q1. Energy security, seasonal heat storage and district-heating (DH) decarbonisation: which emerging solutions have results?

### Takeaway
Nordic DH systems are moving fast toward electrified, storage-heavy heat. The main pieces are sand/rock thermal batteries (Polar Night Energy), pit and cavern seasonal storage (Høje Taastrup PTES, Vantaa's 90 GWh Varanto), 60–110 MW wastewater and seawater heat pumps (Esbjerg, Tallinn, Hamburg), data-centre waste heat (Espoo, about 40% of the network) and heat-only SMRs (Steady Energy LDR-50 pilot under construction). These technologies turn cheap, volatile wind and solar power into storable heat, which directly addresses Lithuania's exposure to price spikes. The best-evidenced items are PTES (89% annual efficiency) and large heat pumps. The sand battery and SMR still rest mostly on company data.

### Cited Findings

**1. Polar Night Energy "sand battery", Pornainen, Finland (operating since 2025)**
- What/where/when: the world's largest sand battery began operating in Pornainen, Finland, in 2025 (reported September 2025) — [Gigazine](https://gigazine.net/gsc_news/en/20250901-finland-sand-battery). Trade press reports the rating as about 1 MW of heat and 100 MWh of thermal storage. One outlet gave 1,000 MWh, which appears to be an error — [Interesting Engineering](https://interestingengineering.com/energy/why-finland-is-betting-on-sand-to-solve-renewable-energy-storage); [TechEBlog](https://www.techeblog.com/finland-largest-sand-battery/).
- How it works: off-peak or surplus electricity heats a silo of sand or crushed rock. The stored heat is later discharged into the municipal DH network — [ioplus](https://ioplus.nl/en/posts/the-sand-battery-that-could-stop-wasted-wind-and-solar).
- Results: the CEO says it kept the town warm through a very cold, high-price winter in which Finnish power prices swung between €3 and €373/MWh within a single week — [bne IntelliNews](https://www.intellinews.com/world-s-largest-sand-battery-survives-its-worst-winter-ready-for-roll-out-440627/). A company engineer said it "actually exceeded the promised values" and ran at the upper end of its simulated range — [Interesting Engineering](https://interestingengineering.com/energy/why-finland-is-betting-on-sand-to-solve-renewable-energy-storage). Secondary reporting claims 90% round-trip efficiency (heat-in to heat-out) and a 70% cut in network emissions from ending oil use. Neither figure has been independently audited — [TechEBlog](https://www.techeblog.com/finland-largest-sand-battery/).
- Commercial status: the company raised a Series A in June 2025 — [Axios Pro](https://www.axios.com/pro/climate-deals/2025/06/13/sand-battery-maker-series-a). Coverage from May 2026 says a larger system is under construction — [bne IntelliNews](https://www.intellinews.com/world-s-largest-sand-battery-survives-its-worst-winter-ready-for-roll-out-440627/). The company won TEK's 2026 Breakthrough Award (€15,000) — [TEK](https://www.tek.fi/en/news-blogs/thermal-energy-from-hot-sand-polar-night-energy-wins-this-years-breakthrough-award).
- [Moderately known / early commercial, roughly TRL 8 / weak-moderate: no audited operating data found]

**2. Pit thermal energy storage (PTES), Denmark and Germany**
- Høje Taastrup, Denmark: 70,000 m³ and about 3,300 MWh, operating since February 2023. It is the first large PTES run as short-term storage with cycles of several weeks. In 2024 it took in 22,934 MWh and gave back 21,109 MWh over nine cycles, an 89% annual efficiency — [Solarthermalworld (Nov 2025)](https://solarthermalworld.org/?p=32619); [THI Ingolstadt](https://www.thi.de/en/research/institute-of-new-energy-systems-ines/details/news/inspiration-from-denmark-pit-thermal-energy-storage-for-the-heat-transition). The search extracts did not show clearly which of these two pages carries which figure.
- Germany: Bracht (26,600 m³, finished 2025) appears in a November 2025 PlanEnergi list of ten operating PTES. The Meldorf pit holds 50,000 m³ and up to 1,500 MWh — [Solarthermalworld](https://solarthermalworld.org/?p=32619); [THI](https://www.thi.de/en/research/institute-of-new-energy-systems-ines/details/news/inspiration-from-denmark-pit-thermal-energy-storage-for-the-heat-transition).
- Vojens, Denmark, the largest Danish pit, is listed at 203,000 or 210,000 m³; sources disagree — [Solarthermalworld](https://solarthermalworld.org/?p=32619).
- Policy tooling: the Danish Energy Agency updated the large-thermal-storage chapter of its Technology Catalogue (standardised cost and performance data) in February 2025 — [ENS](https://ens.dk/media/6445/download).
- A pan-European "TREASURE" project to speed up PTES rollout was reported in August 2026. Its details could not be retrieved — [Solarthermalworld (Aug 2026)](https://solarthermalworld.org/news/treasure-project-accelerates-pit-thermal-energy-storage-expansion-across-europe/).
- [Moderately known / commercial (TRL 9) / strong: operator-measured annual efficiency]

**3. Varanto cavern seasonal heat storage, Vantaa, Finland (under construction, due 2028)**
- Planned capacity is 90 GWh in 1.1 million m³ of water, held in three bedrock caverns each about 20 m wide, 300 m long and 40 m high, with their floors about 100 m below ground. Pressurised water up to 140 °C will feed the DH network — [pv magazine](https://www.pv-magazine.com/?p=36488); [Vantaa Energy](https://www.vantaanenergia.fi/?p=115877); [New Atlas](https://newatlas.com/energy/varanto-seasonal-thermal-energy-storage/).
- Cost is about €200 million, including a €19 million state investment grant. Partners are YIT and AFRY. Ground was due to break in summer 2024 for completion in 2028; the original 2026 target slipped — [pv magazine](https://www.pv-magazine.com/?p=36488); [IOM3](https://www.iom3.org/resource/world-s-largest-thermal-energy-storage-to-be-built.html).
- Construction status after 2024 was not confirmed in the results.
- [Moderately known / first-of-a-kind at this scale (TRL 7–8) / weak so far: no operating data]

**4. Data-centre waste heat to DH: Microsoft and Fortum, Espoo/Kirkkonummi, Finland**
- Full heat recovery from two Microsoft data-centre sites is expected to cover about 40% of DH demand in Espoo, Kauniainen and Kirkkonummi — [City of Espoo](https://www.espoo.fi/en/articles/using-waste-heat-new-data-center-cover-40-espoos-district-heating-needs). Fortum serves about 250,000 DH users there. Yle reports waste heat may eventually supply about 60% in total: 40% from data centres and 20% from treated wastewater — [Yle](https://yle.fi/a/3-12363215).
- Construction of the Kolabacken heat-pump plant in Kirkkonummi began in September 2023 for completion at the end of 2025. Until data-centre heat arrives, the plant can run on air-to-water heat pumps and electric boilers — [Fortum](https://www.fortum.com/data-centres-helsinki-region/construction-fortums-heat-pump-plant-has-started-microsofts-data-centre-site-kirkkonummi); [DCD](https://www.datacenterdynamics.com/en/news/fortum-begins-construction-at-finnish-heat-pump-plant-will-use-microsoft-data-center-waste-heat/).
- Fortum budgeted about €225 million for 2023–2027 ("Espoo Clean Heat"), including about 15 km of pipeline. It expects roughly 400,000 t of CO₂ cuts over time, mainly by displacing coal — [Datacenter Forum](https://www.datacenter-forum.com/fortum/fortum-to-invest-approx-225-million-in-waste-heat-projects-in-finland).
- No 2026 confirmation was found that the heat flows are operating.
- [Moderately known / commercial / moderate: plan-stage figures]

**5. Large wastewater and seawater heat pumps for DH (60–110 MW class)**
- Esbjerg, Denmark: a 70 MW CO₂-refrigerant seawater heat pump (MAN Energy Solutions) is delivering heat to Esbjerg and Varde, billed as the world's largest of its type — [Enlit](https://www.enlit.world/library/mega-heat-pump-delivers-first-heat-in-esbjerg); [Inspenet](https://inspenet.com/en/news/man-energy-commissions-largest-seawater-co2-heat-pump-in-denmark/).
- Tallinn, Estonia (Utilitas Paljassaare): 110 MW drawing on treated wastewater, with seawater added when needed. It is scheduled to supply Tallinn DH in winter 2026 at a cost of nearly €100 million (as of November 2025) — [Utilitas](https://utilitas.ee/en/construction-advances-rapidly-on-utilitas-paljassaare-heat-pump-plant/); [Utilitas plan](https://utilitas.ee/en/utilitas-to-build-a-heat-pump-plant-using-wastewater-and-sea-water-in-paljassaare-in-tallinn/).
- Hamburg, Germany: a 60 MW wastewater heat pump at a sewage plant is due to serve up to 39,000 households from 2026, reportedly as four 15 MW units — [Bayern Innovativ](https://www.bayern-innovativ.de/en/emagazine/detail/shower-water-warms-hamburgs-heating-systems/); [pv magazine](https://www.pv-magazine.com/?p=33419).
- Flensburg, Germany: a 60 MW seawater heat pump is contracted for operation in 2027 — [Clean Energy Wire](https://www.cleanenergywire.org/news/northern-german-city-orders-one-worlds-largest-seawater-heat-pumps).
- [Moderately known / commercial / moderate]

**6. Steady Energy LDR-50 heat-only small reactor, Helsinki, Finland (non-nuclear pilot under construction)**
- Design: a district-heating SMR of up to 50 MW per module, running at low temperature and pressure (about 150 °C and under 10 bar) — [Steady Energy](https://www.steadyenergy.com/news-article/construction-of-steady-energys-small-nuclear-heating-pilot-plant-begins-in-helsinki); [oEnergetice](https://oenergetice.cz/en/nuclear-power/finland-begins-construction-of-pilot-facility-for-small-modular-reactor-concept).
- Pilot: first concrete was poured on 12 February 2026 in the turbine hall of Helen's decommissioned Salmisaari B coal plant. The pilot is a 1:1 replica in which an electric resistor stands in for reactor decay heat. It will deliver about 6 MWt to Helen's DH network and is built to validate passive safety, not to produce commercial heat — [Steady Energy, 16 Feb 2026](https://www.steadyenergy.com/news-article/construction-of-steady-energys-small-nuclear-heating-pilot-plant-begins-in-helsinki); [oEnergetice](https://oenergetice.cz/en/nuclear-power/finland-begins-construction-of-pilot-facility-for-small-modular-reactor-concept).
- Funding and regulation: €22 million raised in March 2025 and €32 million in July 2025 — [BeBeez Mar 2025](https://bebeez.eu/2025/03/05/finlands-steady-energy-raises-e22m-to-advance-small-modular-reactor-for-district-heating/); [BeBeez Jul 2025](https://bebeez.eu/2025/07/08/finnish-steady-energy-secures-e32m-funding-to-develop-heat-focused-small-modular-reactor/). A June 2026 headline reports €10.5 million in pilot-plant financing, and a 3 July 2026 item says the regulator STUK published the summary of an international Joint Early Review of the design — [Steady Energy news](https://www.steadyenergy.com/news). The pilot budget is reported at €15–20 million — [oEnergetice](https://oenergetice.cz/en/nuclear-power/finland-begins-construction-of-pilot-facility-for-small-modular-reactor-concept). The company targets "ready-to-build" status by 2028. Helen runs a broader nuclear programme — [WNN](https://wnn.world-nuclear.org/articles/finnish-energy-firm-helen-launches-nuclear-programme).
- [Moderately known / TRL 5–6 (non-nuclear demonstrator) / weak: no operating data yet]

**7. Vertical bifacial rooftop PV for high latitudes: Over Easy Solar, Norway**
- Vestby pilot near Oslo (2022 data, measured with the research institute IFE): about 1,070 kWh per kW, against about 800 kWh/kW for a conventional rooftop array at the same site. The company claims up to 30% higher yearly specific yield. Panels stand above the snow line — [pv magazine (Mar 2023)](https://www.pv-magazine.com/2023/03/28/high-latitude-tests-show-vertical-rooftop-pv-yields-30-more-energy-in-winter/).
- Independent modelling by IFE in EPJ Photovoltaics found standard models overestimate in-plane irradiance by less than 1%, and measured a median heat-transfer coefficient of 55 W/m²K, meaning the panels run cool — [EPJ PV](https://epjpv.epj.org/articles/epjpv/abs/2024/01/pv230067/pv230067.html).
- Scale: a 284.4 kW array on the roof of Ullevaal Stadion is expected to produce 219,000 kWh a year — [pv magazine](https://www.pv-magazine.com/?p=37814). A Tromsø cold-storage roof is claimed as the largest vertical bifacial installation — [CleanTechnica (Sep 2025)](https://cleantechnica.com/2025/09/27/bifacial-rooftop-vertical-solar-panels-shine-in-norway/). An Oslo public-building pilot pairs the panels with green roofs — [pv Europe](https://www.pveurope.eu/bipv/oslo-pilot-shows-how-public-buildings-can-pair-solar-and-green-roofs).
- [Little-known / early commercial / moderate: IFE-measured pilot, no multi-winter peer-reviewed field series]

**8. Telecom base-station batteries as a virtual power plant: Elisa (now "Gridle"), Finland and Estonia**
- An AI/ML engine turns backup batteries at mobile base stations into a distributed VPP. The project covers more than 2,000 base-station sites — [The Mobile Network](https://the-mobile-network.com/2023/11/elisa-says-operators-can-become-power-suppliers/); [Elisa IndustrIQ case](https://www.elisaindustriq.com/resources/customer-stories/elisacase?hsLang=fi).
- A €3.9 million grant from the Finnish ministry (MEAE) supports a 150 MWh storage target, described as Europe's largest distributed VPP — [Enlit](https://www.enlit.world/library/finland-invests-in-150mwh-vpp); [TotalTele](https://totaltele.com/elisa-to-turn-network-into-europes-largest-virtual-power-plant/).
- Grid operator Fingrid gave technical pre-qualification for the automatic frequency restoration reserve (aFRR), reportedly the first distributed solution approved anywhere for that market — [TelecomTV](https://telecomtv.com/content/green-network/elisa-to-accelerate-distributed-energy-storage-solution-46697); [Grist](https://grist.org/beacon/a-new-kind-of-virtual-power-plant/). Later case material describes the 150 MWh as built and mentions Estonia — [Elisa IndustrIQ](https://www.elisaindustriq.com/resources/customer-stories/elisacase?hsLang=fi).
- [Little-known / commercial / moderate: TSO pre-qualification]

### Inferences
- Lithuania's DH is heat-network-heavy, and its power prices are exposed to Nordic/Baltic volatility. Electric-to-heat storage (sand batteries, PTES) lets DH operators absorb ultra-cheap hours, such as the €3/MWh hours seen in Finland, and so arbitrage the exact volatility Lithuanian consumers suffer. Smaller-town DH networks are the natural fit for Pornainen-scale (1 MW/100 MWh) units.
- Tallinn's 110 MW wastewater heat pump is the closest analog to Vilnius or Kaunas wastewater plants. The neighbour's cost of about €100 million for 110 MW is a useful benchmark (inference from the Utilitas figures).
- Data-centre heat recovery should be written into permitting terms for new Lithuanian data centres. The Espoo plan shows how a single hyperscaler can supply up to 40% of a mid-size network.
- The Elisa model transfers directly to Lithuanian mobile operators, which hold thousands of base-station batteries. Lithuania's balancing market reforms since Baltic synchronisation (February 2025) make aFRR/FCR participation relevant. The synchronisation date is background knowledge, not verified in this session.
- Lithuania's latitude (54–56°N) is close to Oslo's, so the vertical bifacial results plausibly transfer to the flat roofs of Soviet-era blocks and to agricultural edges.

### Gaps
- Not covered for lack of search budget: agrivoltaics, community-owned wind, peer-to-peer trading, industrial thermal batteries (e.g., Kyoto Group Heatcube, Kraftblock, Rondo). These are candidates for a follow-up.
- Polar Night Energy's next projects (names, sizes, customers) and any audited Pornainen data were not retrieved.
- No confirmation was found of 2026 operating status for Fortum's data-centre heat, Tallinn Paljassaare or Hamburg.
- Lithuanian existing analogs (thermal storage at Lithuanian DH companies, heat pumps in Vilnius/Kaunas DH) were not verified.

## Q2. Energy poverty, flexibility and citizen energy: which models lower bills or volatility for households?

### Takeaway
Lithuania already leads Europe on remote ("virtual") prosumer ownership: more than 170,000 prosumers, with remote now outnumbering rooftop among new connections. The transferable innovations therefore lie elsewhere. One is building-level energy sharing for apartment residents (Belgium). Another is household-battery and telecom-battery VPPs that earn balancing revenue (Belgium's Elia project, Elisa). The third is social leasing of heat pumps for low-income households, financed from ETS2 or the Social Climate Fund (EU pilots, Agora proposal). Outcome evidence is thin for social leasing and modest for residential VPPs.

### Cited Findings

**1. Lithuanian baseline (context for "already exists")**
- Lithuania passed 170,000 prosumers (January 2026 distribution-operator data). Among January's new connections, more than 1,300 chose remote solar or wind against about 1,200 rooftop or on-plot systems — [CEEnergyNews](https://ceenergynews.com/renewables/lithuania-surpasses-170000-prosumers/).
- Prosumer numbers rose from about 18,800 in 2021 to about 174,500 in 2025 — [Renewable Energy Magazine (May 2026)](https://www.renewableenergymagazine.com/panorama/european-parliament-conference-explains-how-lithuania-has-20260520).
- In 2019 Lithuania became the first European country with a digital platform for buying or renting parts of remote solar parks — [Environmental and Climate Technologies (2024)](https://reference-global.com/2/v2/download/article/10.2478/rtuect-2024-0025.pdf).

**2. Building-level energy sharing laws: Belgium (Wallonia, Flanders, Brussels)**
- Wallonia's framework rests on the decree of 5 May 2022 plus a government decree of 17 March 2023 on energy communities and energy sharing — [REScoop transposition tracker](https://www.rescoop.eu/policy/belgium-wallonia); [Bird & Bird](https://www.twobirds.com/en/insights/2024/belgium/walloon-energy-sector-set-for-a-fundamental-changes).
- All three regions allow energy communities, including sharing among residents of one apartment building. Brussels also has a "local" community type — [KPMG Belgium](https://kpmg.com/be/en/insights/industries/energy-natural-resources-insights/energy-sharing-in-belgium-overview-and-policy-recommendations.html). In Flanders, renewable and citizen energy communities must hold property or user rights over the installations, and some sharing is "for free only" — [EUI FSR / U2Demo webinar (Sep 2025)](https://fsr.eui.eu/wp-content/uploads/2025/09/U2Demo_webinar-energy-sharing-_-website.pdf).
- Weaknesses: REScoop judged that Wallonia lacked an explicit enabling framework for capacity building and support. Wallonia's 2025 government agreement promises to streamline regulatory, tariff and administrative rules, and an Interreg peer review in Namur (30 Sep–1 Oct 2026) aims to build a framework without new subsidies — [REScoop](https://www.rescoop.eu/policy/belgium-wallonia); [Interreg Europe](https://www.interregeurope.eu/policy-learning-platform/events/peer-review-regional-policies-for-increased-citizen-energy-sharing).
- No household uptake figures were found.
- [Moderately known / in force, still evolving / weak on outcomes]

**3. Residential battery VPP providing balancing to the grid operator: Belgium (Elia with Smart E-Grid and Opteco)**
- More than 2,000 household batteries are aggregated into a VPP providing balancing services. The target was more than 3,000 batteries and about 6 MW by year-end, roughly 15% of Belgium's daily need for that service (26 MW). The article date was unclear in the extract — [Energy-Storage.news](https://energy-storage.news/belgian-tso-residential-virtual-power-plant-will-cover-15-of-countrys-grid-balancing-needs-by-end-of-year).
- Earlier precedent: in 2018 Sonnen and Tiko gained TenneT pre-qualification to supply primary balancing from home batteries pooled into blocks of at least 1 MW. Sonnen cited about 30,000 systems and up to 300 MWh — [pv magazine (2018)](https://www.pv-magazine.com/2018/12/05/sonnen-to-provide-primary-balancing-power-to-german-grid-from-networked-home-storage/).
- Caution: several web pages that claim "2025 VPP case studies" (e.g., a 12,000-battery Hamburg pilot) appear to be unreliable or generated content and were excluded.
- [Moderately known / commercial / moderate]

**4. Social leasing and subscription models for heat pumps for low-income households (EU)**
- The European Commission's February 2025 Action Plan for Affordable Energy links heat pumps to tackling energy poverty, and Commission guidance on social leasing was expected in 2025. Its publication was not confirmed — [EHPA position](https://ehpa.org/news-and-resources/news/ehpa-position-on-social-leasing-for-heat-pumps); [Build Up](https://build-up.ec.europa.eu/en/resources-and-tools/publications/social-leasing-efficient-heating).
- Pilots: LIFE Street HP Reno is testing social leasing in social housing — [Energiesprong](https://www.energiesprong.org/post/social-leasing-of-heat-pumps-bridging-theaffordability-gap-for-energy-efficient-housing). LIFE install.res plans 10–12 demonstrations — [Housing Europe](https://www.housingeurope.eu/?p=6464). HP SUBSCRIBE is studying a subscription model — [EHPA annual report 2025](https://ehpa.org/news-and-resources/publications/annual-report-2025-affordable-heat-pumps).
- Agora Energiewende estimates social financing could support about 500,000 heat-pump installs a year using less than 5% of ETS2 revenues, and that frontloading those revenues could fund schemes from 2026–27 — [Agora Energiewende](https://www.agora-energiewende.org/news-events/investing-eu-carbon-revenues-in-affordable-clean-heating-for-all).
- Design lesson: France's social leasing for electric cars was so popular that its money ran out shortly after launch — [RAP webinar](https://raponline.org/?p=9637); [Agora](https://www.agora-energiewende.org/news-events/investing-eu-carbon-revenues-in-affordable-clean-heating-for-all).
- [Little-known / pilots (TRL n/a; policy pilots) / weak: no outcome data yet]

### Inferences
- Lithuania's remote-prosumer model already solves "solar for people without roofs". The next step is to let apartment-block residents share output from rooftop PV, including vertical bifacial panels, inside the building, which is the Belgian building-level model. It would also cut grid-fee bills for energy-poor residents of renovated blocks.
- A residential and telecom-battery VPP would monetise the batteries Lithuanian prosumers are now adding. Belgium's per-battery average of about 2 kW (6 MW / 3,000 batteries) is modest, so revenue per household is small. The system benefit is reserve capacity without new gas peakers.
- Lithuania's Social Climate Fund and ETS2 revenues (from 2027) could fund a capped social leasing scheme for heat pumps outside DH areas. Following the French EV lesson, it would need budget pacing and eligibility targeting.

### Gaps
- No measured household bill savings from Belgian energy sharing were found. The regulators' data (CWaPE, VREG) were not reached.
- Portuguese self-consumption collectives (UPAC/CER) and Dutch or German "Energy Sharing" (§42c EnWG) were not researched for lack of budget.
- Not checked: whether Lithuanian law (e.g., energy community rules) already permits in-building sharing in multi-apartment blocks.

## Q3. Renovating Soviet-era apartment blocks and housing affordability: what's new beyond conventional retrofits?

### Takeaway
The genuinely new elements are industrialised "serial" renovation, using 3D laser scanning plus factory-made facade and roof modules. These are proven in Germany, with costs down about a third and construction time halved, and piloted on Soviet blocks in Tallinn and Tartu. Lithuania already has a mature subsidised modernisation programme (3,445 buildings by 2021) and a Vilnius one-stop shop. Its gap is speed and industrialisation rather than finance architecture. On affordability, two little-known financing models stand out: Denmark's tenant-financed National Building Fund and Germany's Mietshäuser Syndikat, which removes buildings from the market permanently.

### Cited Findings

**1. Serial renovation (Energiesprong Germany, "Serielles Sanieren")**
- A dena evaluation (2026 publication) finds costs have fallen by roughly a third since launch, to about €1,200–1,860 per m² of living space before subsidies, and construction time has halved from an average of 14 to 7 weeks — [dena, "Energiesprong auf einen Blick" (2026)](https://www.dena.de/fileadmin/dena/Publikationen/PDFs/2026/Energiesprong_auf_einen_Blick.pdf).
- The often-quoted €650–1,000/m² range is a forward projection from the operator of the Hameln pilot, not an evaluated cost — [co2online](https://www.co2online.de/modernisieren-und-bauen/sanierung-modernisierung/serielle-sanierung/).
- Germany's BEG subsidy adds a 15% bonus for serial renovation, and dena estimates serial and conventional costs are now comparable after subsidies — [co2online](https://www.co2online.de/modernisieren-und-bauen/sanierung-modernisierung/serielle-sanierung/). The landlord sector discusses whether it can be made socially compatible — [IVV 2024](https://www.vermieter-ratgeber.de/sites/default/files/public/data-fachartikel/IVV_2024_10_Geht-doch_-Serielles-Sanieren-sozialvertraeglich_20-23.pdf).
- [Moderately known / commercial / moderate: agency evaluation; underlying sample not seen]

**2. Prefabricated modular renovation of Soviet blocks: Estonia (TalTech / MoreConnect, Tallinn Mustamäe; Tartu oPEN Lab)**
- Akadeemia tee pilot, Tallinn: a 1986 five-storey Soviet mass-produced building (80 one- and two-room flats, 4,318 m²) was renovated with prefabricated wooden modular elements to nZEB (nearly zero-energy) performance. 3D laser scanning captured facade and balcony geometry precisely enough for factory production. The work also added floor space and improved indoor climate — [GREEN Home good-practice sheet](https://xpv01649.vdiv.de/fileadmin/user_upload/1_GREEN_Home/2_GREEN-Home_GP-Akadeemia-EE_en.pdf); [Energy Cities](https://energy-cities.eu/mustamae-district-in-tallinn-a-typical-soviet-era-large-scale-estate-transformed-into-a-prestigious-neighbourhood/).
- Lessons: the main barriers were lack of industrial experience, scarce specialist workforce and resident awareness — [GREEN Home](https://xpv01649.vdiv.de/fileadmin/user_upload/1_GREEN_Home/2_GREEN-Home_GP-Akadeemia-EE_en.pdf).
- Tartu, Annelinn: a five-storey municipal building received factory panels integrating insulation, windows, ventilation components and building-integrated PV — [New European Bauhaus Prizes](https://prizes.new-european-bauhaus.europa.eu/node/361458); [Build Up](https://build-up.ec.europa.eu/es/node/68773).
- Scale-up: Estonia's economy ministry (MKM) and KredEx prepared support for about 20 standard-design Soviet buildings of up to five storeys. Implementation was not confirmed — [ERR News](https://news.err.ee/1127264/mkm-and-kredex-to-enable-renovations-for-soviet-era-apartment-buildings). The method is being piloted in Ukraine with the aim of renovating standard blocks "in a matter of weeks" — [ESTDEV](https://estdev.ee/en/articles/estonian-tech-aids-speedy-renovations-apartment-buildings-ukraine).
- [Little-known / pilot (TRL 7) / weak-moderate: no published measured energy or cost data found]

**3. Lithuanian baseline (what already exists)**
- Lithuania's multi-apartment renovation programme renovated 3,445 buildings with 955 GWh of savings, worth about €1 billion over 2013–2021. ETS auction revenue covered 4–42% of yearly funding, falling over time — [EEA](https://climate-energy.eea.europa.eu/topics/climate-finance/use-of-ets-auctioning-revenues/good_practices/renovation-of-multiapartment-buildings-programme-in-lithuania).
- A €100 million state and EIB initiative (2024) aims to mobilise up to €625 million for about 700 buildings — [EIB](https://trad.eib.org/press/all/2024-503-lithuania-and-eib-team-up-in-eur100-million-initiative-for-greener-housing). Šiaulių bankas has financed more than 3,000 renovations, with over €1.2 billion in loans — [CEELM](https://ceelm.com/lithuania/28420-tgs-baltic-advises-eib-on-eur-60-million-increase-in-multi-apartment-buildings-renovation-financing-in-lithuania).
- Vilnius one-stop shop "Amiestas" has operated since 2020 and was a 2025 EU Sustainable Energy Award finalist — [EUSEW](https://sustainable-energy-week.ec.europa.eu/news/one-stop-shop-supporting-multi-apartment-renovations-vilnius-announced-finalist-2025-european-2025-04-29_en). A national awareness project runs 2024–2029 — [APVA](https://apva.lrv.lt/en/project-implementation/national-projects/promoting-the-renovation-of-multi-apartment-buildings/).

**4. Mietshäuser Syndikat: permanently de-commodified rental housing, Germany**
- How it works: each house project is owned by a limited company held jointly by the residents' association and the Syndikat. The Syndikat's veto blocks resale, and buildings are financed partly by small direct loans from supporters. Stiftung Warentest calls the model "social and risky" for lenders — [test.de](https://www.test.de/Mietshaeuser-Syndikat-Anlegergeld-fuer-selbstverwaltete-Mietshaeuser-sozial-und-riskant-4802835-0/); [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mietsh%C3%A4user_Syndikat).
- Scale: 191 house projects plus 22 initiatives as of June 2024, up from 177 projects with more than 3,800 residents in September 2022 — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mietsh%C3%A4user_Syndikat). A separate Swiss Syndikat published a 2025 annual report — [MHS Schweiz](https://www.mietshaeusersyndikat.ch/wordpress/wp-content/uploads/2026/05/MHS_Jahresbericht_2025_lowres.pdf).
- Rents: a BTU Cottbus report found significantly below-average rents. In Freiburg the Syndikat committed 70% of flats to social-housing terms at one-third below the local rent index for 55 years — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mietsh%C3%A4user_Syndikat); [Utopia](https://utopia.de/ratgeber/mietshaeusersyndikat-ein-projekt-fuer-bezahlbaren-wohnraum_137416/).
- [Little-known outside Germany / mature (since 1990s) / moderate]

**5. Landsbyggefonden (National Building Fund), Denmark: tenant-financed revolving renovation fund**
- Set up in 1967. Once a non-profit housing estate's mortgage is paid off, tenants keep paying the same rent and the surplus flows into the national fund. The fund then finances renovation, accessibility upgrades, energy improvements and new construction — [Housing 2030](https://www.housing2030.org/?p=2161); [LBF](https://lbf.dk/footer/english).
- Parliament sets the fund's investment frame every four years — [Housing 2030](https://www.housing2030.org/?p=2161). In 2020 the government proposed releasing DKK 30 billion for renovation over 2020–2026, when 72,000 homes were on the waiting list and some tenants had waited up to 10 years — [Housing Europe](https://www.housingeurope.eu/?p=6333).
- Showcase: the Rosenhøj estate (Aarhus), renovated 2014–2017, won Denmark's RENOVER award in 2017 — [EFFEKT](https://www.effekt.dk/news/2020/8/18/rosenhj-model-example-for-international-public-housing-renovations).
- Criticism: housing associations appoint the board majority, which critics link to generous grants and renovations costing more than buildings are worth — [Altinget](https://www.altinget.dk/hovedstaden/artikel/afdelingsformand-det-er-urimeligt-at-renovere-almene-boliger-for-langt-mere-end-de-er-vaerd).
- [Moderately known among housing experts / mature / moderate]

### Inferences
- Lithuania has repeatable Soviet block types, such as the 1-464 and 1-605 series panel buildings. That repetition favours factory-made modules even more than Germany's mixed stock does, because a catalogue of pre-engineered elements per series could cut design costs. This is inference; the series names are background knowledge.
- Combining the existing Lithuanian finance chain (state subsidy, EIB, commercial bank loans, one-stop shop) with Estonian-style 3D scanning and prefab elements is the most direct transfer. A "serial renovation bonus" like Germany's 15% would be the policy lever.
- Lithuania's very high owner-occupancy and tiny social rental sector mean the Landsbyggefonden model would need adapting. One option is a municipal rental fund that recycles rents from paid-off municipal housing.
- The Mietshäuser Syndikat model fits Lithuania's emerging housing cooperatives as a legal template for permanently affordable rentals in Vilnius.

### Gaps
- Energiesprong results in the Netherlands, France and the UK, robotic renovation startups, Irish one-stop shops (Superhomes) and property-linked finance (e.g., EuroPACE) were not retrieved: the search budget ran out.
- No measured post-renovation energy or cost data for the Estonian prefab pilots was found.
- No 2025 count of renovated Lithuanian buildings was found, and whether any Lithuanian prefab-facade pilot exists was not checked.

## Q4. Rural public transport and road safety: what's working?

### Takeaway
For rural mobility, the best-evidenced low-cost models combine flows of people and goods (the KombiBus) and integrate on-demand services with the backbone timetable. Autonomous minibuses remain pilot-scale with tiny ridership. Hard per-passenger cost data for on-demand services is scarce. Road safety rests on strong evidence: 2+1 roads with median barriers cut deaths and serious injuries by about 44–55% in Sweden. Fleet trials of intelligent speed assistance (ISA) cut speeding by 62–64%. Norway reached 16 deaths per million in 2024 by systematic Safe System measures, including mandatory in-depth investigation of every fatal crash.

### Cited Findings

**1. KombiBus (passengers plus parcels plus regional goods), Uckermark, Brandenburg, Germany**
- In regular operation since 2012/2013. Buses carry goods in the passenger area or luggage space, with an optional trailer of up to 3.5 t — [Interreg Europe good practice](https://interregeurope.eu/good-practices/supporting-rural-mobility-infrastructure-through-innovative-transport-solutions-the-kombibus); [Mobilikon](https://mobilikon.de/print/pdf/node/1446).
- Parcels of up to 10 kg sent up to 100 km cost about €5, usually booked the day before, or same-day if capacity allows — [Tagesspiegel](https://www.tagesspiegel.de/berlin/bringdienste-in-brandenburgs-einode-der-bus-halt--fur-zwei-laib-brot-und-einen-roman-90725.html).
- Reported outcomes: regional producers widened their customer base, local firms cut costs and new sales points appeared at hubs. No volume figures were found — [Mobilikon](https://mobilikon.de/print/pdf/node/1446).
- [Little-known / mature local service / weak: qualitative]

**2. Rural demand-responsive ridepooling, Germany**
- EcoBus pilot, Harz (2018–2019), up to 10 vans in a mountainous rural area: users themselves cancelled most rejected requests (77.5%). The exact source among these results could not be pinned down — [KIT](https://publikationen.bibliothek.kit.edu/1000142738/174149517); [TUHH](https://tore.tuhh.de/handle/11420/10791).
- Hamburg's ioki service on the urban fringe works as a first- and last-mile complement to transit — [TUHH Open Research](https://tore.tuhh.de/entities/publication/4586bfb7-0bed-4e18-a34c-3d91ddfc0792).
- A national simulation of autonomous ridepooling (MOIA/TUM) found 77% pooling, mean occupancy 1.67, a 10-minute average wait and €3.6 billion a year in external-cost savings. This is a simulation, not operating data — [TUM mediaTUM](https://mediatum.ub.tum.de/doc/1859366/1859366.pdf).
- Agora Verkehrswende and PTV have a study on rural on-demand operating costs; results were not found — [Agora Verkehrswende](https://agora-verkehrswende.de/en/projects/demand-responsive-transport-in-rural-areas).
- [Moderately known / commercial / weak on cost-effectiveness]

**3. Autonomous minibuses, Norway**
- The road administration (Statens vegvesen) compiled pilots in Kongsberg, Oslo, Bærum, Forus, Gjøvik and Svalbard, with no accidents but early technical problems — [Statens vegvesen](https://www.vegvesen.no/fag/fokusomrader/miljovennlig-transport/smart-mobilitet/forerlose-minibusser/).
- Stavanger (Kolumbus) has run an autonomous bus since 2022, moving in December 2023 to a harder route with lane changes, higher speeds and a tunnel — [CBW Magazine](https://cbwmagazine.com/autonomous-bus-takes-to-the-roads-in-norway/); [electrive](https://www.electrive.com/es/2022/07/09/not-a-pilot-but-a-series-vehicle-autonomous-e-bus-enters-regular-service-in-norway/).
- Gjesdal, a rural village: three Toyota Proace minibuses plus an on-demand service carried almost 2,000 people in total (around 2021) — [SINTEF](https://www.sintef.no/en/publications/publication/1846149/); [The Driven](https://thedriven.io/2021/02/25/norwegian-self-driving-mini-bus-pilot-now-open-for-passengers/?nb=1).
- [Moderately known / TRL 6–7 / weak: very low ridership]

**4. 2+1 roads with median barriers or rumble strips: Sweden (Vision Zero)**
- Painted 2+1 roads with median rumble strips cut deaths and serious injuries on road links by 44% after correcting for regression to the mean (unadjusted −29%, not significant) — [Traffic Safety Research (Aug 2024)](https://doaj.org/article/9bea8638d5c9473f932f67ceedda5e28).
- 2+1 with a median cable barrier: −55% in deaths and severe injuries — [Trafikverket / Vadeby](https://bransch.trafikverket.se/contentassets/6af107d0cbe94281af73a57b9761c189/16_safety_benefits_vadeby.pdf). The design has roughly halved fatality and serious-injury rates — [iRAP case study](https://www.fofnl.org.uk/archives/docs/iRAP-Sweden-21-Case-Study.pdf); [SWOV](https://swov.nl/en/publicatie/21-roads-cable-barriers-swedish-success-story).
- [Widely known among road engineers / mature / strong]

**5. Intelligent speed assistance (ISA) in public fleets: New York City and London**
- NYC equipped about 500 municipal vehicles with active ISA. Speeding fell 64% against a control group. In a 270-vehicle matched analysis, time spent more than 11 mph over the limit fell 64.18%; the cut was 82% on highways and about 50% on 25 mph local roads. The programme is expanding to 2,100 vehicles with a $2.4 million US DOT grant — [NYC ISA Pilot Evaluation (Oct 2024)](https://www.nyc.gov/assets/dcas/downloads/pdf/fleet/nyc-intelligent-speed-assistance-pilot-evaluation-2024-oct.pdf); [NYC DCAS press release](https://www.nyc.gov/site/dcas/news/022-24/dcas-u-s-dot-volpe-municipal-speeding-reduction-64-new-report-intelligent).
- A London fleet trial cut speeding incidents 62% across 360 vehicles — [ETSC](https://etsc.eu/new-york-trial-confirms-isa-effectiveness/). In an IIHS study, one of 13 commercial fleets estimated a 30% drop in preventable collisions — [Trucknews](https://www.trucknews.com/transportation/fleets-cite-safety-financial-gains-with-intelligent-speed-assist/1003216730/).
- [Moderately known / commercial / strong: controlled evaluation with US DOT Volpe]

**6. Norway's Safe System results**
- 16 road deaths per million in 2024, the lowest of 32 countries in ETSC's PIN programme, and the 2025 ETSC PIN Award — [ETSC](https://etsc.eu/norway-wins-2025-etsc-pin-award-for-outstanding-road-safety-progress/).
- Measures: a 179-measure national action plan across 15 priority areas; a 2020 Road Traffic Act amendment requiring in-depth investigation of every fatal crash; automatic speed cameras; unmarked police vehicles — [The Local Norway (Jul 2025)](https://www.thelocal.no/20250725/vision-zero-why-are-norwegian-roads-so-safe); [NVF action plan](https://nvfnorden.org/wp-content/uploads/2022/10/02-National-Action-Plan-on-Road-Safety-2022-2025-NVF-K%C3%B8benhavn-13.10.22-1.pdf).
- Caveat: deaths have fallen only 12% since 2019, against the 27% needed to stay on track for −50% by 2030 — [ETSC](https://etsc.eu/norway-wins-2025-etsc-pin-award-for-outstanding-road-safety-progress/). Targets: at most 350 killed or seriously injured and at most 50 deaths by 2030, zero by 2050 — [The Local](https://www.thelocal.no/20250725/vision-zero-why-are-norwegian-roads-so-safe).

### Inferences
- Lithuania's rural bus networks and its declining rural postal network could merge through a KombiBus-style licence change that lets regional buses carry parcels and local produce. It is low-cost and needs no new technology.
- Before buying on-demand apps, Lithuanian municipalities should require cost-per-trip reporting, because German evidence shows high cancellation and rejection rates and few published cost figures.
- ISA retrofits in Lithuanian state and municipal fleets (police, municipal vans, school buses) are a cheap, evidence-backed quick win. Norway's mandatory in-depth fatal-crash investigation is a low-cost institutional transfer.
- Autonomous rural buses are not yet a cost-effective answer for Lithuania; the evidence is pilot-scale only.

### Gaps
- Not retrieved: Japan's Level-4 rural services (e.g., Eiheiji), German "Mitfahrbänke" (ride-share benches) evaluations, Finland's MaaS lessons, and cargo-bike rural logistics, all for lack of search budget.
- Not verified this session: Lithuania's existing road-safety instruments (section/average-speed control, 2+1 sections) and its 2025 fatality rate. Check ETSC PIN and the Lithuanian road administration (LAKD) before claiming novelty.

## Q5. Hybrid threats (drones and balloons, GNSS jamming, undersea-cable sabotage): which low-cost or novel countermeasures exist?

### Takeaway
The standout transferable idea is Ukraine's mass acoustic sensor network: about 10,000 phone-plus-microphone poles at roughly $400–500 each, for less than two Patriot missiles. Low-cost interceptor drones at $1,000–2,500 each (Ukraine) are moving to European production, with Estonia and Poland co-producing. For cables, fibre-optic distributed acoustic sensing (DAS), which turns existing telecom fibre into an anchor-drag alarm, was field-tested by Finland's Elisa with the Navy and Border Guard. For GNSS jamming, quantum and magnetic navigation is flight-proven but pre-commercial. Baltic terrestrial backup (R-Mode Baltic) reaches about 12–15 m accuracy but has not been proven under jamming. Lithuania faces a specific balloon problem (more than 60 balloons in one episode closed Vilnius airport), and no proven technical counter-solution was found.

### Cited Findings

**1. "Sky Fortress"-type acoustic drone-detection network, Ukraine**
- Each node is a microphone plus mobile phone on a pole, spaced about 4–5 km apart, streaming detections into air-defence data — [Defense One (Jul 2024)](https://defenseone.com/defense-systems/2024/07/ukraines-cheap-sensors-are-helping-troops-fight-waves-russian-drones/398204); [ITC.ua](https://itc.ua/en/news/in-ukraine-9-500-phones-detect-shaheds-by-sound-and-transmit-air-defense-data).
- Scale and cost: about 9,500 to nearly 10,000 sensors. First units cost about $300 according to USAFE commander Gen. James Hecker; after three modifications they now cost about $400–500 — [Defense Daily](https://defensedaily.com/u-s-air-force-commander-for-europe-pressing-nato-to-pursue-low-cost-sensors-to-counter-one-way-drones/air-force); [ITC.ua](https://itc.ua/en/news/in-ukraine-9-500-phones-detect-shaheds-by-sound-and-transmit-air-defense-data). Each pole costs under $500, making the whole network cheaper than two Patriot missiles — [Foreign Policy (Jun 2025)](https://foreignpolicy.com/2025/06/06/ukraine-russia-war-drone-attack-airbase-bombers/).
- Hecker estimated Romania could cover its territory for about $6 million, the cost of one radar — [Defense Daily](https://defensedaily.com/u-s-air-force-commander-for-europe-pressing-nato-to-pursue-low-cost-sensors-to-counter-one-way-drones/air-force).
- [Moderately known / operationally fielded (TRL 9 in Ukraine) / moderate: combat use, no public detection-rate data]

**2. Low-cost interceptor drones (Ukraine, now European production)**
- Cost gap: Shahed-class threats cost about $15,000–50,000, while traditional interceptors cost $1–12 million. A PitchBook analyst said investors should look for solutions under $30,000 per engagement — [Defense News (Jun 2026)](https://www.defensenews.com/global/europe/2026/06/03/at-a-nato-range-in-latvia-hits-and-misses-mark-europes-counter-drone-journey/).
- At NATO's Sēlija range in Latvia, European startups demonstrated autonomous interceptors, a mothership drone and jet interceptors. JetDrones, a German startup, showed a jet interceptor for 4–8 km altitude. Ukrainian operators told it the price must fall below a Shahed's, and noted that adding a radar roughly doubles the price — [Defense News (Jun 2026)](https://www.defensenews.com/global/europe/2026/06/03/at-a-nato-range-in-latvia-hits-and-misses-mark-europes-counter-drone-journey/).
- Ukrainian Sting and Bullet interceptors reportedly cost about $1,000–2,500 each — [Let's Data Science](https://letsdatascience.com/news/ukraine-deploys-low-cost-interceptors-to-protect-infrastruct-beb7e631); [DroneXL (Mar 2026)](https://dronexl.co/2026/03/01/ukraine-is-scaling-up-interceptor-drones-as-russias-shahed-threat-outpaces-every-defense).
- Poland's PGZ and Estonia's Frankenburg Technologies reportedly plan up to 10,000 "Mark 1" interceptors a year (Spanish-language report, March 2026; unverified) — [United24](https://united24media.com/es/latest-news/polonia-prueba-misil-interceptor-contra-drones-shahed-y-acelera-produccion-masiva-17365). Interceptor drones were called "Europe's new hope" against Shaheds as early as September 2025 — [Defense News (Sep 2025)](https://www.defensenews.com/global/europe/2025/09/15/interceptor-drones-are-europes-new-hope-for-downing-russian-shaheds/).
- [Moderately known / fielded in Ukraine, early in the EU / moderate]

**3. Lithuania-specific threat: smuggling balloons from Belarus**
- In late 2025 smuggling balloons repeatedly closed Vilnius airport. In one episode at least 60 balloons were launched from Belarus and 40 reached aviation-critical areas, apparently released at regular intervals and aimed at the runways — [NPR (Dec 2025)](https://www.npr.org/2025/12/03/nx-s1-5630000/belarus-weather-balloons-lithuania-airport); [transport-online](https://transport-online.de/en/news/vilnius-balloons-disrupt-flight-operations-185070.html).
- President Nausėda promised "kinetic measures" but noted destroying balloons "requires great precision" — [LRT (Oct 2025)](https://lrt.lt/en/news-in-english/19/2731107/nauseda-vows-all-possible-measures-after-another-belarusian-balloon-incident). Lithuania planned indefinite border closures, and Belarus then held more than 1,000 Lithuanian trucks — [Euronews (Oct 2025)](https://www.euronews.com/2025/10/27/lithuania-draws-up-plans-to-shut-belarus-border-crossings-indefinitely-after-balloon-incur); [NPR](https://www.npr.org/2025/12/03/nx-s1-5630000/belarus-weather-balloons-lithuania-airport).

**4. Fibre-optic distributed acoustic sensing (DAS) on subsea cables: Finland (Elisa, Finnish Border Guard, Finnish Navy)**
- DAS sends short light pulses down an existing cable's optical fibre so the cable itself becomes a continuous vibration sensor. Tests simulated scenarios such as anchor dragging. Elisa is building it into an automated alerting service for authorities and infrastructure owners. Announcement reported around June 2026 — [Light Reading](https://www.lightreading.com/security/elisa-gives-its-subsea-cable-ears); [Elisa press release](https://elisa.com/corporate/news-room/press-releases/elisa-the-finnish-border-guard-and-the-finnish-navy-successfully-tested-the-securing-of-submarine-cables/2C6DF14368A6075C/); [Janes](https://www.janes.com/defence-intelligence-insights/defence-news/defence/finland-tests-acoustic-monitoring-for-cui-protection); [BGR](https://www.bgr.com/2197190/finland-undersea-internet-cable-system-sensors/).
- Motivation: the late-2024 cable cuts included the Lithuania–Sweden link — [BGR](https://www.bgr.com/2197190/finland-undersea-internet-cable-system-sensors/).
- Research: DAS works independently of AIS (ship transponder signals) and is largely unaffected by light or weather. Published vessel-tracking work is still limited in scale and real-world validation — [arXiv 2509.11614](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.11614v2). Vendor perspective: [AP Sensing](https://www.apsensing.com/en/news/istening-beneath-the-waves-protecting-subsea-cables-with-fiber-optic-sensing).
- [Little-known as a civil service / TRL 7 (field-tested) / weak-moderate: no published detection statistics]

**5. NATO Baltic Sentry (launched January 2025)**
- At least 11 Baltic cables were damaged in the 15 months to January 2025 — [Defense News (Jan 2025)](https://www.defensenews.com/global/europe/2025/01/28/11-baltic-cables-damaged-in-15-months-pushing-nato-to-boost-security/).
- In August 2026 a NATO official said no malign damage had been detected since January, but had no data on whether such activity had declined. The Eagle S tanker dragged its anchor for about 90 km on 25 December 2024, and a Finnish court dismissed the sabotage case against three crew members — [The Moscow Times (Aug 2026)](https://www.themoscowtimes.com/2026/08/02/nato-detects-no-malign-damage-to-baltic-sea-infrastructure-since-january-official-says).
- [Widely known / operational / weak causal evidence: deterrence effect unproven]

**6. GNSS-independent navigation: quantum and magnetic navigation**
- The US Defense Innovation Unit flight-tested a quantum-sensor magnetic navigation system (Honeywell) on an Embraer 170 over the Pacific. It reported 89% better accuracy than legacy backup during a 4 h 23 min GPS-denied mission — [Airforce Technology](https://www.airforce-technology.com/?p=270020).
- Airbus Acubed and SandboxAQ's AQNav (quantum magnetometers plus AI on a single GPU) completed a nationwide flight-test campaign. They report it exceeded commercial navigation accuracy requirements — [Airbus Acubed](https://acubed.airbus.com/blog/quantum/magnetic-navigation-at-scale-advancing-resilient-flight-through-quantum-sensing/); [HPCwire](https://www.hpcwire.com/off-the-wire/sandboxaq-and-acubed-showcase-progress-in-magnetic-navigation/).
- Australia's Q-CTRL claims a commercially viable, jam-proof quantum navigation system; the headline says "50 times more accurate than GPS", a vendor claim — [Military & Aerospace Electronics](https://www.militaryaerospace.com/communications/article/55283615/q-ctrl-unveils-jam-proof-quantum-tech-thats-50-times-more-accurate-than-gps).
- MIT Technology Review (Dec 2025) judges the technology still far from commercial deployment. It also reports Boeing's quantum IMU flights (4 hours without GPS) — [MIT Technology Review](https://www.technologyreview.com/2025/12/16/1129887/quantum-navigation-militarys-gps-jamming-problem/). Attribution of the Boeing detail to this article is uncertain.
- Secondary coverage cites IATA 2025 data showing GPS jamming up 67% and spoofing up 193% versus 2023. The primary IATA report was not checked, and the attribution to this page is uncertain — [Airforce Technology](https://www.airforce-technology.com/?p=270020).
- [Little-known / TRL 6–7 / moderate: government flight tests]

**7. R-Mode Baltic: terrestrial maritime positioning backup using existing radio beacons and AIS/VDES**
- A 12-partner Baltic consortium built a testbed in the southern Baltic using eight MF radio beacons transmitting R-Mode signals, with AIS/VDES as a GNSS backup — [ION Navigation](https://navi.ion.org/content/68/3/465); [Interreg/keep.eu report](https://keep.eu/api/project-attachment/30444/get_file).
- Accuracy: 15.1 m (95%) horizontal under optimal conditions, limited at night by sky-wave interference (Deneb research vessel) — [BSH/DLR](https://digitale-bibliothek.bsh.bund.de/viewer/fulltext/188752/1/). Better than 12 m (95%) near the centre of three transmitters during ship trials — [ION Navigation](https://navi.ion.org/node/6080).
- A separate DLR Baltic jamming testbed, authorised by the German network agency (BNetzA), characterises real jamming — [DLR presentation](https://www.maritimes-cluster.de/fileadmin/user_upload/6_-_DLR_Gewies.pdf).
- [Little-known / TRL 6–7 / moderate on accuracy; not demonstrated under jamming]

### Inferences
- A Lithuanian acoustic and optical sensor grid modelled on Ukraine's would cost on the order of single-digit millions of euros. On Hecker's Romania estimate scaled to Lithuania's 65,000 km² (about 26% of Romania's area), the order of magnitude is roughly $1.5–2 million at original unit prices. This is a researcher's extrapolation, not a sourced figure. Such a grid could also be tuned to detect low, slow balloons and small drones near Vilnius airport and the Belarus border, possibly with Riflemen's Union volunteers maintaining nodes.
- Lithuania's balloon problem is not the Shahed problem. Balloons are slow, cheap, radar-faint and drift with the wind. A cost-effective response probably combines wind-trajectory forecasting, acoustic/optical/radar fusion, and cheap kinetic interceptors such as drones with nets or guns, plus airport procedures that allow partial operations. No proven integrated system was found.
- DAS can be added to Lithuania's existing subsea links (e.g., NordBalt, the BCS cable to Sweden) and to land fibre along the Belarus border. Its main cost is interrogator units, not new cable. Partnering with telecom operators, as Finland did, is the transferable model.
- Lithuanian ports (Klaipėda) and aviation would benefit from joining R-Mode Baltic and eLoran-type terrestrial backups. Quantum navigation is a 2028+ procurement watch item.

### Gaps
- Not retrieved: eLoran deployments (UK, South Korea), Baltic GNSS-jamming statistics for 2025–26, cyber-defence volunteer models (e.g., the Estonian Defence League cyber unit) and counter-balloon technical solutions. Search budget was exhausted.
- TYTAN (Munich) production targets for interceptors appeared in results but could not be tied to a reliable source and are omitted.
- Not verified: whether Lithuania has already procured acoustic sensor networks or interceptor drones in 2026.

## Q6. Disinformation, civil defence and societal resilience: which civic and institutional innovations have evidence?

### Takeaway
Three transferable models have evidence or scale. Prebunking video ads (Google Jigsaw, run in Poland, Czechia and Slovakia in 2022–23 and in five EU states in 2024) measurably improve recognition of manipulation techniques in experiments, with effects varying by country and localisation. Crowd-sourced, chatbot-based fact-checking inside messaging apps (Taiwan's Cofacts) reached more than 300,000 users, though volunteer editor capacity is the bottleneck. Small civic civil-defence academies (Taiwan's Kuma Academy, about 25,000 trained, about $40 per day) complement state agencies such as Sweden's Psychological Defence Agency (80 staff, about SEK 150 million).

### Cited Findings

**1. Prebunking ("inoculation") video campaigns: Google Jigsaw, Central Europe and the EU**
- In 2022–23 short ads on manipulation techniques used in anti-Ukrainian-refugee narratives ran in Poland, Czechia and Slovakia and were viewed 38 million times. Google's researchers reported better technique recognition and lower sharing intent — [Yahoo/AP](https://news.yahoo.com/google-hopes-inoculate-internet-users-184500761.html). The effect was strongest in Poland and weak or absent where the videos were only dubbed rather than made locally (reportedly Slovakia; the snippet was truncated) — [Mezha](https://mezha.media/en/?p=96966).
- Germany launch (2023): ads on YouTube, Facebook and TikTok — [Texxr](https://texxr.com/835926/jigsaw-tests-video-ads-prebunk-misinformation-germany).
- A 2024 campaign for the EU elections ran in Germany, Belgium, France, Italy and Poland. An independent paper in *Communications Psychology* (2025) ran experiments in 12 nations three months later and found the videos improved discernment of manipulative content and sharing decisions — [Springer Nature](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1038/s44271-025-00379-3). Moonshot reports that effects varied with education, digital literacy, geography and GDP — [Moonshot](https://moonshotteam.com/wp-content/uploads/Building-Resilience-to-Misinformation-in-Europe.pdf).
- [Moderately known / deployed at scale / moderate: peer-reviewed lab effects; real-world behaviour effects unproven]

**2. Cofacts: open-source, crowd-sourced fact-checking chatbot inside the LINE messaging app, Taiwan (g0v community)**
- How it works: users forward suspicious messages to a LINE bot. Volunteer editors write replies stored in an open database, and repeat forwards get automatic answers — [IJNet](https://ijnet.org/en/story/taiwanese-cofacts-fact-checks-information-line); [Taiwan Insight](https://taiwaninsight.org/2022/10/12/the-bot-fighting-disinformation-the-story-of-cofacts/).
- Scale: more than 87,000 suspicious messages reported and more than 2,000 contributors (2022) — [Taiwan Insight](https://taiwaninsight.org/2022/10/12/the-bot-fighting-disinformation-the-story-of-cofacts/). More than 300,000 users and over 650 new messages a week — [Rights CoLab](https://rightscolab.org/case_study/cofacts/). In one three-month window in 2018, 35,180 of more than 46,000 messages were answered automatically — [IJNet](https://ijnet.org/en/story/taiwanese-cofacts-fact-checks-information-line). Third-party bots reusing the open data reach more than 1 million people — [Taiwan Insight](https://taiwaninsight.org/2022/10/12/the-bot-fighting-disinformation-the-story-of-cofacts/).
- Bottleneck: only 10–20 weekly active editors despite about 3,000 Facebook-group volunteers — [MIT Solve](https://solve.mit.edu/solutions/7501).
- [Moderately known in civic-tech circles / mature (since about 2017) / moderate: usage data, impact on belief unmeasured]

**3. Sweden's Psychological Defence Agency (Myndigheten för psykologiskt försvar, est. 2022)**
- Mandate: identify, analyse and counter foreign information manipulation and interference (FIMI) aimed at Sweden, strengthen society-wide resilience, and fund research — [Euromaidan Press](https://euromaidanpress.com/2022/01/05/sweden-creates-psychological-defense-agency-to-counter-disinformation-from-russia-china-and-iran/); [Baltic Times (Jul 2025)](https://www.baltictimes.com/swedish_psychological_defense_agency_s_head_confident_about_swedes__resilience_to_foreign_information_operations/).
- Resources: 80 employees and a budget of about SEK 150 million (July 2025, director Magnus Hjort) — [Baltic Times](https://www.baltictimes.com/swedish_psychological_defense_agency_s_head_confident_about_swedes__resilience_to_foreign_information_operations/).
- No outcome metrics were found.
- [Moderately known / operational / weak on outcomes]

**4. Kuma Academy: civilian-run civil-defence training, Taiwan**
- About 20–25 classes a month of 40–50 students each, and about 25,000 people trained (date unclear). Roughly 70% of trainees are women. A one-day workshop costs about NT$1,200 (about $40) — [Foreign Policy (Dec 2022)](https://foreignpolicy.com/2022/12/19/taiwan-china-invasion-civil-defense-training/); [AmCham Taiwan (Mar 2024)](https://topics.amcham.com.tw/2024/03/bolstering-taiwans-civil-defense/). Which of these sources gives which number is uncertain; see also [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kuma_Academy).
- UMC co-founder Robert Tsao pledged NT$1 billion to train 3 million people in 3 years — [CCAS India](https://ccasindia.org/newsdetails.php?nid=7504); [Tamil Guardian](https://www.tamilguardian.com/node/948357). A peer NGO, Forward Alliance, reports 19,000 trainees since 2020 — [ASPI Strategist](https://www.aspistrategist.org.au/taiwan-mobilises-civil-society-to-bolster-civil-defence/).
- [Little-known in Europe / operating / weak-moderate: participation data only]

### Inferences
- The Jigsaw result that dubbed videos underperformed means a Lithuanian prebunking campaign must be produced locally in Lithuanian, plus Russian and Polish for minorities. It should target the techniques common in Lithuanian information space, such as scapegoating and false dichotomies around energy prices or NATO.
- A Cofacts-style bot for Viber, Messenger, WhatsApp and Telegram, the apps Lithuanians use, could federate existing Lithuanian debunking communities such as Debunk.org and the "Lithuanian Elves". Their existence is background knowledge, not verified here. Following Cofacts' bottleneck lesson, it should use LLM-drafted replies with human verification to stretch scarce editor time.
- Kuma Academy's low-cost, women-heavy, civilian-run model complements Lithuania's Riflemen's Union (Šaulių sąjunga) by reaching people who will not join a paramilitary body: first aid, tourniquets, evacuation, radio.

### Gaps
- Not retrieved for lack of budget: Finland's National Defence Courses and its comprehensive security model, Sweden's 2024 "If Crisis or War Comes" brochure, Ukraine's Diia lessons, the Estonian Defence League cyber unit, and EU DisinfoLab/Hybrid CoE evaluations.
- No 2025 annual report from Sweden's Psychological Defence Agency was found.
- Lithuania's own instruments (the lt72.lt 72-hour preparedness guidance, Debunk.org, civil-protection shelters app) were not verified in this session.

## Q7. Baltic Sea eutrophication: which nutrient-reduction measures have measured results and costs?

### Takeaway
The most cost-effective, best-evidenced measure for agricultural phosphorus is gypsum amendment of fields. In Finland's Savijoki pilot it cut phosphorus load and erosion by about 50% over 1,500 ha, with effects lasting about five years and no harm found to sensitive species. Structure liming of clay soils (Sweden, about 65,000 ha subsidised since 2010) cut total-P leaching by 38–45% in trials, though results depend on site. Mussel farming is expensive and highly site-dependent (€10 to €4,000 per kg N). Open-sea oxygenation remains at proposal stage, with a €5–6 million pilot (BOxIn) that would use electrolyser by-product oxygen.

### Cited Findings

**1. Gypsum amendment of fields: Finland (SAVE project, Savijoki river; John Nurminen Foundation, University of Helsinki, SYKE)**
- About 1,500 ha of fields were treated. Erosion and phosphorus load fell by about 50%, covering both particulate and dissolved P, and one application lasts about five years — [University of Helsinki SAVE](https://blogs.helsinki.fi/save-kipsihanke/project-save-in-english/?lang=en); [University of Helsinki news](https://www.helsinki.fi/en/news/life-sciences/gypsum-treatment-fields-enhances-water-protection-agriculture).
- First-year data was more modest: particulate P fell 21% in a catchment where 43% of fields were treated (138 vs 174 kg/km²) — [EGU 2018](https://meetingorganizer.copernicus.org/EGU2018/EGU2018-3629.pdf). The peer-reviewed paper (Agricultural and Food Science, 2020) finds costs low relative to other agricultural measures and judges about 0.5 million ha of southern Finnish arable land suitable — [AFS journal](https://journal.fi/afs/article/view/88902).
- Side effect: river sulphate tripled. Monitoring found no harm to the thick-shelled river mussel, trout or water moss — [University of Helsinki SAVE](https://blogs.helsinki.fi/save-kipsihanke/project-save-in-english/?lang=en); [JYX trout-egg study](https://jyx.jyu.fi/handle/123456789/64580). Foundation involvement and later scale-up: [John Nurminen Foundation](https://johnnurmisensaatio.fi/?p=33179).
- [Little-known outside Finland / proven at catchment scale / strong-moderate]

**2. Structure liming of clay soils: Sweden**
- A mix of limestone and slaked lime is spread to stabilise clay aggregates. It has been subsidised in Swedish environmental schemes since 2010, with about 65,000 ha treated — [Acta Agriculturae Scandinavica B (Dec 2022)](https://doaj.org/article/0d63f5f1e3d7443eab39fdce0b7f2473).
- Total-P leaching fell 45% and 38% in years 2 and 3 in a tile-drained clay loam, with no effect on N leaching or yield — [Evergreening library / Norberg & Aronsson](https://library.evergreening.org/items/06560c99-f3dd-4f35-8583-1da14c6b8305). Effects persisted 6–8 years after application at trial sites, but site characteristics determine how long effects last and yield responses were mixed — [AGRIS/FAO](https://agris.fao.org/search/en/records/675981ddc7a957febdf9ae2f).
- A Norwegian review warned that Swedish use had outpaced effectiveness studies and that benefits may be limited to high-clay, high-P soils — [Bioforsk 2014](https://nibio.brage.unit.no/nibio-xmlui/bitstream/11250/2444436/2/Bioforsk-Rapport-2014-09-81.pdf).
- [Little-known / subsidised practice / moderate, site-dependent]

**3. Mussel farming for nutrient removal (German Baltic coast, Poland, Baltic Blue Growth)**
- German coast model: yearly N-removal cost for a 1-ha farm ranges from €162/kg N (Flensburg) to €4,018/kg N (Darss-Zingst Bodden chain). Farms are profitable only at a few sites, and only selling for human consumption at about €2.2/kg — [Frontiers in Marine Science (2020)](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/marine-science/articles/10.3389/fmars.2020.00371/full).
- Baltic Blue Growth puts mussel farming without sales revenue at €10–64/kg N, against agricultural measures at €0–150, wastewater upgrades at €11–136 and wetlands at €2–93 — [Interreg Baltic Blue Growth](https://interreg-baltic.eu/projectlibrary/web/balticbluegrowth.html).
- Gulf of Gdańsk: about €130/kg N via mussels versus about €11.8 at a sewage plant with 90% efficiency — [Sokołowski / SUBMARINER](https://www.2020.submariner-network.eu/images/banners/Prospects_for_Mussel_Farming_in_the_Gulf_of_Gdańsk_-_Sokołowski.pdf).
- [Moderately known / pilot-to-commercial / moderate, widely divergent cost estimates]

**4. Deep-water oxygenation pilots: Sweden; proposed BOxIn pure-oxygen injection**
- Gothenburg researchers in the BOX project pumped oxygen-rich surface water into deep water in Byfjorden (west coast) and Kanholmsfjärden (east coast). The 2010–2013 fjord pumping saw oxygen-loving organisms return to the bottom — [ScienceDaily (2008)](https://sciencedaily.com/releases/2008/12/081218132258.htm); [Cosmos](https://cosmosmagazine.com/biology/extra-oxygen-breathes-life-back-into-a-deep-fjord).
- Proposed BOxIn (Baltic Sea Pilot for Pure Oxygen Injection): about six years including at least three of injection, budget €5–6 million. It is linked to Lhyfe's BOxHy concept of using electrolyser by-product oxygen. Status unconfirmed — [Hydrogen Today](https://hydrogentoday.info/?p=60625).
- Limits: oxygen from the 2014 major Baltic inflow was largely consumed within about a year in the East Gotland Basin, and reoxygenated bottoms under a thick hypoxic layer lacked recolonisation potential — [PMC5709267](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC5709267/). SMHI's annual oxygen survey offers baseline monitoring — [SMHI 2024](https://smhi.se/download/18.4cdca89f195b3c486e53433/1743575814314/Oxygen_timeseries_1960_2024.pdf).
- [Little-known / TRL 4–6 for open sea / weak: fjord pilots only]

### Inferences
- Gypsum and structure lime target agricultural phosphorus, which matters for Lithuania's Nemunas basin and the Curonian Lagoon. Before scaling, Lithuania would need soil mapping to find clay or high-P fields where the measures work. Gypsum's low cost makes it the first measure to pilot, ideally with Finnish partners (John Nurminen Foundation, SYKE).
- Mussel farming looks least promising on Lithuania's exposed, low-salinity coast. Low-salinity sites tend to yield small mussels and poor economics, as the high Darss-Zingst costs suggest (inference). It would only make sense as a niche feed or fertiliser product.
- If Lithuania develops green hydrogen (e.g., around Klaipėda), electrolyser by-product oxygen could make it a partner in BOxHy-type oxygenation experiments. This is speculative and research-stage.

### Gaps
- Not retrieved: manure nutrient recycling and biogas nutrient recovery (Finland's Soilfood, Danish manure processing), seaweed farming, and HELCOM 2025 nutrient-input assessments, for lack of budget.
- No gypsum or structure-lime cost per kg P was found in the extracts.
- Not checked: whether Lithuania already uses gypsum or structure lime in its CAP Strategic Plan eco-schemes.

## Q8. Labour shortages and productivity: which automation and AI deployments show measured gains?

### Takeaway
Measured public-sector AI gains are real but modest. The UK's Microsoft 365 Copilot trial with 20,000 civil servants saved a self-reported 26 minutes a day (about two weeks a year), with 17% of participants reporting no savings. Purpose-built tools score higher: the UK's "Humphrey" Minute saves about one hour of admin per one-hour meeting, and Consult processed 50,000+ consultation responses in two hours. In agriculture, solar-powered autonomous seeding and weeding robots (Denmark's FarmDroid) replace 150–250 hours of manual hoeing per hectare on organic farms, with payback reported anywhere from one season to six years.

### Cited Findings

**1. Generative AI copilots for civil servants: UK government (GDS Copilot trial; DSIT "Humphrey")**
- The Microsoft 365 Copilot experiment ran September–December 2024 with about 20,000 civil servants in 12 organisations. Participants saved an average of 26 minutes a day, about two working weeks a year, with the biggest gains in drafting documents and presentations; 17% reported no time savings — [GOV.UK (Jun 2025)](https://www.gov.uk/government/news/landmark-government-trial-shows-ai-could-save-civil-servants-nearly-2-weeks-a-year); [UK Parliament written statement](https://questions-statements.parliament.uk/written-statements/1807718).
- Analysts called the gains "not really results that will blow anybody away" — [TechMarketView](https://www.techmarketview.com/news/archive/2025/06/04/ukhotviewsextra-government-trial-shows-ai-could-save-civil-servants-2-weeks-a-year).
- Humphrey Minute (meeting notes) was trialled by 25 local authorities and saved about 1 hour of admin per 1-hour meeting in central-government tests — [Computing](https://www.computing.co.uk/news/2025/ai/humphrey-ai-trialled-by-25-local-authorities).
- Humphrey Consult analysed more than 50,000 responses to the Independent Water Commission in two hours. DSIT estimates 75,000 civil-servant days saved a year; these are departmental claims — [Civil Service World](https://www.civilserviceworld.com/professions/article/dsit-says-ai-tool-could-free-up-75000-civil-servant-days-a-year).
- [Widely known (Copilot) / little-known (Humphrey) / commercial / moderate: self-reported time savings]

**2. Autonomous solar-powered seeding and weeding robot: FarmDroid FD20, Denmark**
- A French organic farmer previously spent 150–250 hours of manual hoeing per hectare a year and reports the robot paid for itself in one season, about 200 hours per hectare saved — [Future Farming](https://futurefarming.com/tech-in-focus/field-robots/the-farmdroid-fd20-payed-for-itself-in-just-one-season).
- An Ontario estimate puts ROI on the roughly $130,000 robot at 5–6 years, depending on row units — [Farmtario](https://farmtario.com/machinery/farmdroid-provides-scalable-horticultural-seeding-and-weeding/).
- Throughput is about 6–6.5 ha a day. In UK tests, weeding speed was 650–800 m/h, and human interventions were mostly for refilling seed or fixing mechanical faults — [Farmers Weekly](https://www.fwi.co.uk/machinery/technology/solar-powered-robot-drills-and-weeds-on-shropshire-farm); [FAO Agritech Observatory](https://agritechobservatory.review.fao.org/en/farmdroid-fd20).
- [Moderately known / commercial / weak-moderate: farmer testimony, no controlled study]

### Inferences
- Lithuania's public sector could replicate the UK pattern cheaply. Purpose-built tools (meeting minutes, consultation analysis, citizen-letter drafting in Lithuanian) give larger, more measurable gains than generic copilots. Any rollout should measure outcomes, given that 17% of UK users saw no savings.
- For Lithuania's vegetable and organic sector, where seasonal weeding labour is short, solar field robots are viable mainly for high-value row crops (sugar beet, vegetables). Payback depends on hectares per robot.

### Gaps
- Not retrieved for lack of budget: robotics-as-a-service for manufacturing SMEs, forestry automation, Estonia's Bürokratt and X-Road successors, and Danish or Finnish SME automation-voucher evaluations.
- No independent, controlled productivity studies were found for either the UK AI tools or FarmDroid.
