## 1. Suicide: latest rate, EU ranking, risk groups, prevention programmes and how well they work

### Takeaway
Lithuania still has the EU's highest suicide rate: about 19.6 per 100,000 in 2023 and 2024, close to double the EU average. Suicides rose slightly in 2022–2024 (527 → 562 → 566) after decades of decline. The 65+ age group now has the highest relative rate. Prevention infrastructure exists (free municipal psychological services since 2020, a 2018 national suicide-prevention algorithm, volunteer crisis lines), but it is under-resourced. The volunteer lines say they cannot answer everyone, and rural districts lack specialists.

### Cited Findings
- **Rate and EU rank:** Eurostat data put Lithuania at 19.6 suicides per 100,000 in both 2023 and 2024, the highest in the EU and "almost double" the EU average — [Euronews, 11 Oct 2025](https://www.euronews.com/health/2025/10/11/suicide-rate-in-lithuania-remains-one-of-the-highest-in-the-eu-despite-previous-declines); [Xinhua, 9 Oct 2025](https://english.news.cn/europe/20251009/aac5e0c11e2546e597ca344004406df4/c.html)
- **Absolute numbers (Hygiene Institute):** 566 suicides in 2024, 562 in 2023 and 527 in 2022, a slight rise "after decades of decline", with older adults particularly affected — [Xinhua, Oct 2025](https://english.news.cn/europe/20251009/aac5e0c11e2546e597ca344004406df4/c.html); [LRT](https://www.lrt.lt/en/news-in-english/19/2697333/suicides-in-lithuania-after-decades-of-decline-still-highest-in-eu)
- **Older adults:** Paulius Skruibis (Vilnius University Suicidology Research Centre) says the decline has been slowest among people 65+, who now have the highest relative number of suicides. He says the national level is still "several times higher" than the EU average — [Xinhua](https://english.news.cn/europe/20251009/aac5e0c11e2546e597ca344004406df4/c.html); [LRT](https://www.lrt.lt/en/news-in-english/19/2697333/suicides-in-lithuania-after-decades-of-decline-still-highest-in-eu)
- **Senior share of suicides:** The co-founder of Sidabrinė linija (Silver Line) says seniors account for more than 39% of all suicides and names loneliness as a major factor. This is an NGO claim, not an official statistic — [Aukok.lt / Sidabrinė linija](https://www.aukok.lt/projects/Sidabrine-linija-People-Need-People)
- **Why older people go unhelped:** Older people are often reluctant to seek professional help, and the situation is "particularly bad in the districts" (rural municipalities), which lack mental health specialists — [LRT, "Suicide remains grave problem among Lithuania's elderly"](https://www.lrt.lt/en/news-in-english/19/2357171/suicide-remains-grave-problem-among-lithuania-s-elderly); [LRT, "They've learned to suffer"](https://www.lrt.lt/en/news-in-english/19/2383834/they-ve-learned-to-suffer-suicide-rate-remains-high-among-lithuanian-elderly)
- **Long-run trend:** Suicide rates have more than halved since the mid-1990s peak — [Our World in Data](https://ourworldindata.org/data-insights/suicide-rates-in-lithuania-have-more-than-halved-since-their-1990s-peak). WHO data give a 2022 total rate of 18.6 — [Wikipedia, Suicide in Lithuania (citing WHO)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Suicide_in_Lithuania)
- **Rate conflict, 2023:** One secondary analysis gives 18.83/100,000 for Lithuania versus 3.47 for Cyprus (about 5.4× higher). This conflicts slightly with Eurostat's 19.6; treat it as approximate — [Germanpedia (low-quality aggregator)](https://germanpedia.com/highest-suicide-rate-in-europe/)
- **2017 National Audit Office audit (covering 2012–2015):**
  - Suicide prevention was "planned inappropriately", with "no comprehensive system" of aid for people at risk.
  - National coordination was lacking and planned measures were poorly implemented.
  - Access to services at mental health centres was very unequal.
  - There was no mechanism to share information across institutions about patients found to be at risk.
  - Source: [Valstybės kontrolė audit (EN summary)](https://www.valstybeskontrole.lt/EN/Product/Download/3510)
- **Funding limited the national plans:** The OECD says stakeholders and the 2017 audit found the 2016–2020 suicide prevention plan was held back by lack of funding. The 2020–2024 plan aimed to improve continuity of care from inpatient to outpatient services and to expand psychological services in mental health centres — [OECD, Community-based mental health care in Lithuania (2022)](https://www.oecd.org/content/dam/oecd/en/publications/reports/2022/07/the-provision-of-community-based-mental-health-care-in-lithuania_3c11f47d/18de24d5-en.pdf)
- **Improvements 2015–2019:** Expanded primary mental health services and municipal suicide-prevention programmes — [Biological Psychiatry & Psychopharmacology, 2020](http://biological-psychiatry.eu/wp-content/uploads/2020/06/BPP_May2020_3to10.pdf)
- **Suicide-prevention algorithm (2018):** A national algorithm was rolled out across medical and social services to identify at-risk people and route them to specialist support. This comes from a solutions-journalism source and was not verified against an official document — [Reasons to be Cheerful](https://reasonstobecheerful.world/how-lithuania-halved-its-suicide-rate/)
- **Free psychological wellbeing services:** Available since 2020 at centres in all 60 municipalities, drop-in and without a doctor's referral — [Reasons to be Cheerful](https://reasonstobecheerful.world/how-lithuania-halved-its-suicide-rate/)
- **Crisis lines:**
  - Vilties linija (Hope Line) offers 24/7 phone support in Lithuanian. Jaunimo linija (Youth Line) is on 8 800 28888 — [findahelpline.com](https://findahelpline.com/countries/lt)
  - Jaunimo linija online chat runs 18:00–24:00 — [UNHCR Lithuania](https://help.unhcr.org/lithuania/where-to-find-help-in-lithuania/helpline-services/)
  - Senior lines: Silver Line 0 800 800 20 and Caritas's line for older people 0 800 700 08 — [Catholic World Report, Jul 2026](https://www.catholicworldreport.com/2026/07/22/as-pope-warns-of-pandemic-of-loneliness-caritas-lithuania-offers-a-catholic-answer/)
- **Crisis-line capacity:** In 2025 Jaunimo linija had 494 volunteers and received more than 150 help requests a day (loneliness, anxiety, self-harm, suicide risk), and it "still cannot reply to everyone" — [Aukok.lt fundraiser](https://www.aukok.lt/projects/One-conversation-can-save-a-life)
- **Training study:** The Hygiene Institute's mental health centre has run a feasibility study on suicide-prevention training for the public and professionals, a sign that gatekeeper training is still being built out — [Hygiene Institute (hi.lt)](https://www.hi.lt/uploads/Psichikos%20sveikatos%20centras/Feasibility%20study%20on%20the%20accessibility%20and%20development%20of%20suicide%20prevention%20training%20for%20the%20public%20and%20professionals%20in%20Lithuania.pdf)

### Inferences
- The fastest falls came alongside alcohol control (2017–2018, see section 3) and the expansion of low-threshold services after 2018–2020. The 2022–2024 plateau and uptick suggests the "easy gains" are exhausted. The remaining burden sits with groups those measures reach least: older, rural, lonely men who do not seek help.
- Crisis lines depend on volunteers and fundraising (Jaunimo linija fundraises on Aukok.lt), and their published figures show unmet demand.
- **Solution opportunities:**
  - Paid, professionally staffed 24/7 crisis capacity, including text and chat at night.
  - Proactive outreach to older rural men, for example through GPs, pharmacists, postal workers or social workers using the 2018 algorithm.
  - Follow-up after self-harm or ED discharge.
  - Postvention for bereaved families.
  - Gatekeeper training in rural municipalities.

### Gaps
- I found no official full-year 2025 suicide count or rate; the Hygiene Institute would normally publish it around autumn 2026.
- No breakdown of 2024 rates by sex, age and urban/rural. The common claim that middle-aged rural men face the highest risk was not re-verified with current numbers; the 2020 Wiley paper on male suicide determinants, https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1002/psp.2372, was found but not read.
- No outcome evaluation of the crisis lines, the 2018 algorithm or the municipal services. No National Audit Office follow-up audit after 2017 was found.
- The status and content of a post-2024 suicide prevention action plan, and the current role of the "State Mental Health Centre", were not confirmed.

## 2. Mental health: prevalence, access to psychologists and psychiatrists, stigma, waiting times, free municipal help

### Takeaway
Lithuania has relatively many psychiatrists (about 24 per 100,000 versus an EU average of about 17) but one of the OECD's lowest psychologist rates. Child and adolescent psychiatrists and public-sector psychologists are particularly short. Free, no-referral counselling exists in every municipality (Public Health Bureaus and mental health centres), but session caps are short and unclear, queues are common, and access is socially graded. In a late-2025 survey, about 40% reported waiting times and only about 1 in 5 called access easy. Seniors and lower earners fare worst.

### Cited Findings
- **Workforce mix:**
  - Lithuania employs more psychiatrists than the OECD average but has "one of the lowest rates of psychologists in the OECD". Shortages are sharpest for psychologists in the public system and for child and adolescent psychiatrists — [OECD 2022](https://www.oecd.org/content/dam/oecd/en/publications/reports/2022/07/the-provision-of-community-based-mental-health-care-in-lithuania_3c11f47d/18de24d5-en.pdf)
  - Roughly 24 psychiatrists per 100,000 versus an EU average of 17 (date of data unclear) — [Depression Scorecards](http://www.depressioncare.eu/lithuania.html)
- **Prevalence (older data):**
  - In the 2019 national health interview survey (Statistics Lithuania, PHQ-8), 13.1% had mild and 5.1% moderate–severe depressive symptoms in the past two weeks. The figure was extracted via a Ministry of Health presentation and should be checked against the original — [Ministry of Health, "Public mental health: Lithuanian perspective" (2024)](https://sam.lrv.lt/media/viesa/saugykla/2024/6/yZsa-Vy7BQs.pdf)
  - About 2.8% of people over 15 have diagnosed depression — [Depression Scorecards](http://www.depressioncare.eu/lithuania.html)
  - A WHO estimate gives 5.6% annual prevalence in 2015 — [PMC8311845](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8311845/)
  - A Eurostat-based slide shows self-reported chronic depression of 7% in Lithuania versus 7.2% in the EU; low context — [Ministry of Health presentation](https://sam.lrv.lt/media/viesa/saugykla/2024/6/yZsa-Vy7BQs.pdf)
- **Access survey (late 2025, LRT-commissioned):**
  - 40% said they face waiting times for psychological services, the largest single group.
  - Only about one in five described access as easy.
  - Access is easier for high earners and hardest for seniors.
  - Experts say continuity is the bigger problem, because "a few free consultations are rarely enough".
  - Source: [LRT, "Psychologist access in Lithuania: easier for the high earners, a struggle for seniors"](https://www.lrt.lt/en/news-in-english/19/2759002/psychologist-access-in-lithuania-easier-for-the-high-earners-a-struggle-for-seniors)
- **Free routes:**
  - Free counselling without referral is available at the mental health centre attached to a person's registered polyclinic, or at the municipal Public Health Bureau; every municipality has one — [psichologeiveta.lt guide](https://psichologeiveta.lt/en/free-psychological-support-in-lithuania/)
  - Vilnius Public Health Bureau says there is "no universal promise" on session numbers or waiting times — [Vilniaus visuomenės sveikatos biuras](https://vilniussveikiau.lt/en/free-psychological-support-services/)
- **Waiting times (weak sources only):**
  - A commercial clinic site claims 2–6 month waits for public consultations and therapy — [Bookimed (commercial, potentially biased)](https://us-uk.bookimed.com/clinics/country=lithuania/direction=psychiatry/)
  - An expat guide says 2–4 weeks for a free psychiatrist consultation — [Lithuania Handbook](https://lithuaniahandbook.com/how-to-access-mental-health-services-in-lithuania-as-an-expat/)
  - The two conflict, and neither is official.
- **Stigma:** Stigma makes people avoid mental health specialists — [Depression Scorecards](http://www.depressioncare.eu/lithuania.html). Stigma also deters health professionals from seeking help — [PMC8311845](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8311845/). Older people are especially reluctant to seek help — [LRT](https://www.lrt.lt/en/news-in-english/19/2357171/suicide-remains-grave-problem-among-lithuania-s-elderly)

### Inferences
- The bottleneck is not psychiatrists but psychologists and psychotherapists in the public system, plus a model of only a few free sessions. Demand probably spills over to private, out-of-pocket care, which fits the income gradient in the LRT survey.
- **Solution opportunities:**
  - Stepped-care and digital CBT tools in Lithuanian, integrated with the free municipal sessions.
  - Group and peer-support formats.
  - Tele-psychology for rural areas and seniors.
  - Task-shifting (trained counsellors, nurses).
  - Transparent waiting-time and session-entitlement information; a national booking or availability tool.
  - Child and adolescent mental health capacity.

### Gaps
- No official 2025 waiting-time data for public psychologists or psychiatrists (VLK or Hygiene Institute) was found.
- No recent (2023–2026) quantified national stigma survey was found.
- No up-to-date prevalence data (the EHIS 2025 wave was not found).
- Utilisation numbers for Public Health Bureau psychological services in 2024–2025 were not found.
- The exact current session entitlement (sometimes cited as 10–15 psychotherapy sessions a year) is unverified.

## 3. Alcohol and drugs: consumption after 2017–2018, current debates, drug trends

### Takeaway
Recorded alcohol consumption fell to about 10.16 litres of pure alcohol per person aged 15+ in 2025, the lowest in the 2010–2025 series and about one third below the 2012 peak. Consumption fell 24% between 2013 and 2023, and peer-reviewed studies credit the 2017 excise doubling and the 2018 package with reduced mortality. Lithuania still drinks heavily by EU standards. The Seimas partly loosened the law in May 2024 (events, tastings, information exceptions), but the core rules survive: minimum age 20, limited retail hours and the advertising ban. In 2026 policy moved toward tightening again (a ban on alcohol-free beer and wine sales to minors). Drug deaths are dominated by opioids, and naloxone coverage is thin.

### Cited Findings
- **Recorded consumption, 2025:** 10.16 L of pure alcohol per resident aged 15+, 0.05 L less than in 2024, the lowest in the official 2010–2025 series and about one third below 2012. This is State Data Agency data via a secondary source; it counts legal recorded alcohol only — [NordicAlcohol.org Lithuania profile](https://www.nordicalcohol.org/lithuania)
- **Earlier recorded figure:** About 11 L in 2023, 0.2 L less than in 2022 — [LRT](https://www.lrt.lt/en/news-in-english/19/2285678/alcohol-tobacco-consumption-down-in-lithuania)
- **Total consumption estimates (include unrecorded alcohol):** 11.23 L in 2024 versus a 2012 peak of 15.35 L — [Statbase (WHO-based)](https://statbase.org/data/ltu-alcohol-consumption-per-capita/). These figures differ by method and are not comparable with the recorded series.
- **Unrecorded alcohol and recent trend:** Unrecorded alcohol was about 8.3% of total consumption in 2019. Consumption fell to about 11.3 L in 2018, rose temporarily during COVID to about 12.1 L in 2021, then resumed falling — [European Journal of Public Health 2025, economic costs of alcohol in Lithuania 2015–20](https://academic.oup.com/eurpub/article/35/4/726/8128106). The attribution of the 11.3/12.1 L figures to this paper comes from a search extract; verify.
- **OECD/Observatory 2025:** Alcohol consumption dropped 24% between 2013 and 2023 thanks to stricter controls, but remains high, and "alcohol control policies were relaxed recently" — [Country Health Profile 2025](https://www.oecd.org/content/dam/oecd/en/publications/reports/2025/12/country-health-profile-2025-country-notes_7e72146d/lithuania_48218446/25bc0724-en.pdf)
- **What worked (evidence):**
  - An interrupted time-series analysis found the alcohol control policies reduced adult all-cause mortality over and above secular trends — [Štelemėkas et al., Addiction 2021](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/add.15470)
  - Štelemėkas, in an interview, said the 2017 excise doubling (in force 1 March 2017) was the most significant measure and was linked to about a 3% fall in all-cause mortality. He said 2018 (sales hours, age 20, advertising ban) "may have saved more than a thousand lives". These are interview figures, not peer-reviewed estimates — [Think Global Health](https://www.thinkglobalhealth.org/article/reducing-alcohol-related-deaths-lithuania)
  - Effects vary by cause: for male pneumonia mortality, only the 2008 policy produced a significant slope change — [PMC9128348](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC9128348/)
  - Further work exists on alcohol taxation and mortality inequalities — [PMC9841962](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9841962/); on cardiovascular mortality and alcohol policy — [BMC Public Health 2021](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/s12889-021-12177-7); on an integrated alcohol policy — [Rehm et al., Drug & Alcohol Review 2025](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/dar.13980); and on Baltic benchmarking — [PubMed 41968287](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41968287/). Their findings were not extracted.
  - There is no Lancet paper I could confirm. The key paper is in *Addiction* (2021).
- **Current rules:** Minimum purchase and consumption age 20. Off-premise sales 10:00–20:00 Monday to Saturday and 10:00–15:00 on Sunday — [NordicAlcohol.org](https://www.nordicalcohol.org/lithuania)
- **May 2024 loosening:**
  - Passed 59–25 with 11 abstentions.
  - Drinks up to 15% ABV may now be sold at fairs, exhibitions and mass events (previously only beer and cider up to 7.5%).
  - Factual information (pairings, awards, production information on online sales platforms) is carved out of the advertising definition.
  - Licences can be issued once a criminal record is cleared (previously convicted persons were barred outright).
  - Tastings are allowed at production premises.
  - Minimum age, retail hours and the general advertising ban are unchanged.
  - Sources: [LRT](https://www.lrt.lt/en/news-in-english/19/2269199/lithuanian-parliament-eases-alcohol-sale-advertising-restrictions); [Baltic Times](https://www.baltictimes.com/lithuanian_parliament_eases_alcohol_sale__advertising_restrictions/)
  - Conflict: Movendi described the package as lowering the age to 18 for drinks under 15% and extending Sunday sales to 20:00 — [Movendi, May 2024](https://movendi.ngo/news/2024/05/17/lithuania-parliament-voted-to-water-down-world-class-alcohol-law/). LRT and NordicAlcohol say age and hours were left intact. Movendi may describe an earlier draft; treat LRT as more reliable.
- **Public opinion (advocacy source):** 74.6% oppose lowering the minimum age of 20, and 59.2% oppose extending sales hours — [Movendi](https://movendi.ngo/news/2024/05/17/lithuania-parliament-voted-to-water-down-world-class-alcohol-law/)
- **2022 attempt:** A bill to lower the age to 18 and extend hours was voted down 63–51 — [BNN](https://bnn-news.com/lithuania-rejects-attempt-to-lower-countrys-high-legal-drinking-age-231932); see also [LRT, liberals propose easing](https://www.lrt.lt/en/news-in-english/19/1433931/lithuanian-liberals-propose-easing-alcohol-restrictions-lowering-drinking-age)
- **2026 tightening:** Bill XVP-893(3), an amendment to the Food Law, bans selling non-alcoholic beer- or wine-named drinks to under-18s. The Seimas approved it after the consideration stage on 19 May 2026 by 66–7 with 26 abstentions; planned entry into force is 1 Nov 2026 — [NordicAlcohol.org](https://www.nordicalcohol.org/post/lithuania-moves-to-restrict-alcohol-free-beer-and-wine-sales-to-minors)
- **Political context (partly verified):** NordicAlcohol says a new government led by Mindaugas Sinkevičius took office on 14 July 2026 — [NordicAlcohol.org](https://www.nordicalcohol.org/lithuania). Separately, Wikipedia lists Health Minister Marija Jakubauskienė serving until 14 July 2026, succeeded by Linas Kukuraitis — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marija_Jakubauskien%C4%97). The dates match, but neither source is primary.
- **Drugs (EUDA):**
  - In 2022 heroin appeared in fewer than 1 in 6 overdose deaths in Estonia, Latvia, Lithuania and Finland. Lithuania reported 33 deaths involving non-cocaine stimulants, the most among the countries listed (Denmark 20, Sweden 17) — [EUDA European Drug Report 2024, drug-induced deaths](https://www.euda.europa.eu/publications/european-drug-report/2024/drug-induced-deaths_en)
  - Opioids are involved in at least 8 in 10 overdose deaths in northern European countries including Lithuania — [EUDA](https://www.euda.europa.eu/publications/european-drug-report/2024/drug-induced-deaths_en)
  - Lithuania showed a downward overdose trend over the 10 years to 2024 (search extract; verify) — [EUDA 2026 overdose news](https://www.euda.europa.eu/news/2026/overdose-deaths-europe-euda-highlights-growing-concerns-over-highly-potent-opioids-and-cocaine_en)
  - In 2024 naloxone was provided at only 6 of 14 sites, plus 38 doses on release from prison — [EUDA FAQ, preventing overdose deaths](https://www.euda.europa.eu/publications/topic-overviews/drug-induced-deaths-faq/preventing-overdose-deaths_en)

### Inferences
- The excise-plus-availability package is Lithuania's clearest public-health success. The 2024 partial loosening, together with the OECD's warning, means the gains are politically fragile. Each new government brings pressure from industry and liberal parties.
- Recorded consumption of about 10 L is still high by EU standards. The remaining harm is concentrated among heavy drinkers, older men and rural areas, which links to suicide, cardiovascular disease and injuries.
- **Solution opportunities:**
  - Screening and brief intervention in primary care.
  - Accessible outpatient alcohol treatment, including digital and anonymous options for men.
  - Monitoring cross-border and online purchasing.
  - Wider naloxone distribution and take-home naloxone.
  - Drug checking and harm reduction outside the largest cities.

### Gaps
- Searches were cut off before I could check 2025–2026 drug policy: decriminalisation of small-amount possession, NTAKD prevalence surveys, synthetic opioid (nitazene) alerts and 2024–2025 overdose counts. No data was gathered on these.
- No confirmed 2025–2026 Seimas proposals to loosen hours or the age limit were found, and I could not verify what the new (July 2026) government plans on alcohol.
- No 2025 data on binge drinking or on youth drinking (ESPAD 2024) was gathered.

## 4. Life expectancy and avoidable mortality: gender gap, cardiovascular disease, cancer, injuries, road deaths

### Takeaway
Life expectancy reached 77.6 years in 2024, 4.1 years below the EU average. That is the EU's largest gain since 2010. The male–female gap remains about 9 years, among the widest in the EU: men about 73 and women about 82. Cardiovascular disease causes about 53% of deaths versus about 33% in the EU, and male cancer mortality is about 30% above the EU level. Preventable mortality exceeds 300 per 100,000 (EU 168). Road deaths rose about 12% in 2025 to 48 per million after a decade of steep decline.

### Cited Findings
- **Life expectancy, 2024:** 77.6 years, 4.1 years below the EU average; Lithuania had the greatest increase in the EU between 2010 and 2024 — [Country Health Profile 2025](https://www.oecd.org/content/dam/oecd/en/publications/reports/2025/12/country-health-profile-2025-country-notes_7e72146d/lithuania_48218446/25bc0724-en.pdf)
- **Gender gap, 2024:** Women 81.9 years, men 73.1 years, a gap of 8.8 years — [OECD 2025 country materials: Country Health Profile 2025](https://www.oecd.org/content/dam/oecd/en/publications/reports/2025/12/country-health-profile-2025-country-notes_7e72146d/lithuania_48218446/25bc0724-en.pdf) / [Health at a Glance 2025 country note](https://www.oecd.org/content/dam/oecd/en/publications/reports/2025/11/health-at-a-glance-2025-country-notes_2f94481e/lithuania_3d0c2a24/0beba903-en.pdf). The search extract did not make clear which of the two documents gives the split.
- **2025 (State Data Agency, via secondary source):** Men about 73 years, women about 82, a gap of about 9 years; the gap narrowed by almost 1 year compared with 2021 — [Lygiadieniai.lt](https://lygiadieniai.lt/daugiau-apie-lyciu-lygybe/ka-rodo-lyciu-nelygybes-statistika/sveikata/)
- **Conflicting estimates:**
  - UN-projection sites give a lower 2025 estimate (76.3 total, 71.6 men, 80.9 women); these conflict with official data — [Worldometer](https://www.worldometers.info/demographics/lithuania-demographics/)
  - 2023: women 80.7, men 71.2 — [Our World in Data](https://ourworldindata.org/profile/health/lithuania)
  - 2023 life expectancy of 77.43 years was the highest since records began in 1958 (Hygiene Institute) — [LRT (Lithuanian)](https://www.lrt.lt/naujienos/mokslas-ir-it/11/2474412/lietuva-turi-daug-potencialo-sveiko-gyvenimo-trukme-salyje-auga-pirmauja-moterys)
- **Cardiovascular disease:**
  - Cardiovascular disease caused 53% of all deaths in 2023 versus 33% in the EU (2022), and is the leading cause of death — [Country Health Profile 2025](https://www.oecd.org/content/dam/oecd/en/publications/reports/2025/12/country-health-profile-2025-country-notes_7e72146d/lithuania_48218446/25bc0724-en.pdf)
  - Ischaemic heart disease alone accounts for almost a third of all deaths — [Country Health Profile 2023](https://www.oecd.org/content/dam/oecd/en/publications/reports/2023/12/lithuania-country-health-profile-2023_7ab0a5d9/5ed683c8-en.pdf)
  - Older data: the ischaemic heart disease mortality rate was more than four times the EU average — [Country Health Profile 2017](https://health.ec.europa.eu/document/download/17d49c95-7735-46c7-bfb7-3342f86771eb_en)
- **Cancer:**
  - Male cancer mortality was about 400 per 100,000 in 2021, about 30% above the EU; female cancer mortality was no different from the EU in 2021 — [EU Country Cancer Profile Lithuania 2025](https://www.oecd.org/content/dam/oecd/en/publications/reports/2025/02/eu-country-cancer-profile-lithuania-2025_9a52818d/b260e7d1-en.pdf)
  - Lung, colorectal and stomach cancers cause the most cancer deaths — [Country Health Profile 2023](https://www.oecd.org/content/dam/oecd/en/publications/reports/2023/12/lithuania-country-health-profile-2023_7ab0a5d9/5ed683c8-en.pdf)
- **Avoidable mortality:**
  - In 2022 Hungary, Lithuania and Romania had preventable mortality above 300 per 100,000 — [Eurostat Statistics Explained](https://ec.europa.eu/eurostat/statistics-explained/index.php?title=Preventable_and_treatable_mortality_statistics)
  - EU averages in 2022 were 168 preventable and 98 treatable deaths per 100,000. Lithuania is among the countries with total avoidable mortality above 470 per 100,000 — [Euronews, Dec 2025](https://www.euronews.com/2025/12/02/more-than-1-million-deaths-avoidable-in-the-eu-which-country-has-highest-preventable-death)
  - Preventable mortality was 340 per 100,000 in 2020, a figure that includes COVID-19 deaths — [Country Health Profile 2023](https://www.oecd.org/content/dam/oecd/en/publications/reports/2023/12/lithuania-country-health-profile-2023_7ab0a5d9/5ed683c8-en.pdf)
- **Risk factors:** Smoking and obesity remain concerns, and alcohol consumption remains high — [Country Health Profile 2025](https://www.oecd.org/content/dam/oecd/en/publications/reports/2025/12/country-health-profile-2025-country-notes_7e72146d/lithuania_48218446/25bc0724-en.pdf)
- **Road deaths:**
  - Preliminary 2025 figure: 48 per million, up from 43 in 2024 (+12%), while EU road deaths fell about 3% to about 19,400 — [European Commission DG MOVE, 24 Mar 2026](https://transport.ec.europa.eu/news-events/news/eu-road-deaths-drop-3-2025-2026-03-24_en)
  - Road deaths fell from 267 in 2014 to 121 in 2024 (−55%) — [ETSC PIN Report 2025](https://etsc.eu/wp-content/uploads/ETSC-2025-Annual-PIN-Report-DIGITAL-V2.pdf)
  - About 138 deaths in 2025, 10th on a European list that includes non-EU countries — [VignetteSwitzerland (citing ETSC PIN 2026)](https://vignetteswitzerland.com/europes-deadliest-countries-for-driving-in-2026/)

### Inferences
- The male disadvantage is mostly avoidable: cardiovascular disease, alcohol-related causes, suicide, injuries and male cancers (lung and stomach, linked to smoking, diet and H. pylori). Interventions aimed at middle-aged men would have outsized effects on the gap.
- The 2025 rise in road deaths reverses a strong trend and needs monitoring (speed enforcement, drink-driving).
- **Solution opportunities:**
  - Men's health check-ups and community cardiovascular risk screening in workplaces, farms and pharmacies.
  - Hypertension control programmes.
  - Smoking cessation services.
  - Better cancer screening uptake (colorectal, lung pilots).
  - Rural cardiac rehabilitation.

- No 2023 preventable and treatable mortality values for Lithuania were obtained.

- Healthy life years were not obtained.

## 5. Healthcare system: funding, out-of-pocket costs, waiting lists, staff shortages, rural access, hospital reform, e-health

### Takeaway
Lithuania spends 7.3% of GDP on health (EU 10.0%). Only 68% of spending is public, and 31% is paid out of pocket, almost double the EU's 16%. Out-of-pocket spending on medicines (€237 per person) is the EU's fourth highest. About 9% of households face catastrophic health spending. Unmet need is above average and rising because of waiting times. The workforce problem is acute and getting worse:
- Nurses fell to 7.5 per 1,000 in 2023, the lowest since 2010.
- About 3,000 nurses are missing now, and the gap could exceed 8,000 nurses and 700 doctors by 2032.
- Pay is low.
- The 2026 plan to send new residents to the regions met backlash.

### Cited Findings
- **Spending and financial protection:**
  - Health spending was 7.3% of GDP in 2023 (EU 10.0%); public funding was 68% of the total — [Country Health Profile 2025](https://www.oecd.org/content/dam/oecd/en/publications/reports/2025/12/country-health-profile-2025-country-notes_7e72146d/lithuania_48218446/25bc0724-en.pdf)
  - Out-of-pocket payments were 31% of health spending in 2023, among the highest in the EU and almost double the EU average of 16%. Medicines out-of-pocket spending was €237 per person, the fourth highest in the EU. Pharmaceuticals make up about a third of out-of-pocket payments, followed closely by dental care — [Country Health Profile 2025](https://www.oecd.org/content/dam/oecd/en/publications/reports/2025/12/country-health-profile-2025-country-notes_7e72146d/lithuania_48218446/25bc0724-en.pdf)
  - Catastrophic health spending fell from 11.5% of households in 2016 to 9.4% in 2021, driven by lower out-of-pocket spending on medicines after the 2017–2020 medicine policies; about 9% of households are still affected. Medicines are the largest driver, especially for the poorest — [ScienceDirect 2026 / PMC12964039](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12964039/)
  - 2026 state budget: nearly €4.8 billion allocated to healthcare — [China-CEE Institute briefing, Jan 2026](https://china-cee.eu/2026/01/15/lithuania-monthly-briefing-lithuanias-2026-economic-outlook-and-state-budget-growth-momentum-amid-rising-fiscal-pressures/)
- **Unmet needs and waiting:**
  - 4.2% reported unmet healthcare needs versus an OECD average of 3.4% — [Health at a Glance 2025 country note](https://www.oecd.org/content/dam/oecd/en/publications/reports/2025/11/health-at-a-glance-2025-country-notes_2f94481e/lithuania_3d0c2a24/0beba903-en.pdf)
  - Unmet needs due to waiting times rose by 1.4 percentage points or more since 2019 — [OECD Health at a Glance 2025, unmet needs chapter](https://www.oecd.org/en/publications/2025/11/health-at-a-glance-2025_a894f72e/full-report/unmet-needs-for-healthcare_c994e8b1.html)
  - Earlier EU-SILC data: 2.9% unmet medical examination needs in 2022 versus 2.2% in the EU — [Country Health Profile 2023](https://www.oecd.org/content/dam/oecd/en/publications/reports/2023/12/lithuania-country-health-profile-2023_7ab0a5d9/5ed683c8-en.pdf)
  - 2017: more than 1 in 4 patients waited over four weeks for an outpatient specialist appointment — [Country Health Profile 2021](https://health.ec.europa.eu/system/files/2021-12/2021_chp_lt_english.pdf)
  - The legal maximum wait for a specialist consultation is 30 calendar days — [OECD, Waiting Times for Health Services (2020)](https://www.oecd.org/content/dam/oecd/en/publications/reports/2020/05/waiting-times-for-health-services_9d746179/242e3c8c-en.pdf)
  - From April 2025, patients can book appointments up to four months ahead. Specialist care requires a GP referral; if no slot is free the patient is put on a waiting list — [WHO/Observatory HSPM update](https://eurohealthobservatory.who.int/monitors/health-systems-monitor/updates/hspm/lithuania-2013/changes-to-patient-registration-and-appointments-scheduling)
  - 90% of people could see their family doctor within a week, but waits are much longer in some municipalities — [Country Health Profile 2025](https://www.oecd.org/content/dam/oecd/en/publications/reports/2025/12/country-health-profile-2025-country-notes_7e72146d/lithuania_48218446/25bc0724-en.pdf)
- **Workforce, nurses:**
  - 7.5 practising nurses per 1,000 in 2023, the lowest since 2010 and below the EU's 8.5. A government forecast warns of a shortfall of over 8,000 nurses and nursing assistants and over 700 doctors by 2032, with acute rural shortages. The target physician-to-population ratio, deferred from 2020 to 2025, is "unlikely to be achieved" — [Country Health Profile 2025](https://www.oecd.org/content/dam/oecd/en/publications/reports/2025/12/country-health-profile-2025-country-notes_7e72146d/lithuania_48218446/25bc0724-en.pdf)
  - About 3,000 nurses are short now (January 2025), rising to 5,000 if ignored. The system loses about 400 nurses a year (about 600 leave, about 200 graduates enter). Some nurses work 24-hour shifts and take second jobs. Against recommended ratios, about 40,000 nurses' assistants are missing. Many nurses are past retirement age and still working — [LRT, Jan 2025](https://www.lrt.lt/en/news-in-english/19/2457272/lithuania-is-short-of-3-000-nurses-experts-warn-of-system-collapse); [Xinhua, 13 Jan 2025](https://english.news.cn/20250113/771e8c43b9f94d75a9430aabd8babaa0/c.html)
  - Nurse pay in 2023: about €2,000 a month gross, under €1,300 net, roughly half of doctors' pay — [LRT](https://www.lrt.lt/en/news-in-english/19/2275837/are-lithuanian-nurses-worst-paid-in-europe)
- **Workforce, emigration and rural doctors:**
  - Health-worker emigration directly contributes to shortages; push factors are low pay, workload, limited careers and psychological strain. Higher salary was the top reason to leave for 83% of medical students, 66% of residents and 50% of physicians surveyed — [Healthcare (MDPI) 2025, 13(19):2470](https://doi.org/10.3390/healthcare13192470)
  - A June 2026 plan ties state-funded residencies to five years of regional service; only 20 of 385 places would be free of the obligation, and it sparked backlash. The article cites (attributed to "Leiputė") a projected shortage by 2032 of 269 family doctors, 207 internists and 146 paediatricians — [Euronews, 18 Jun 2026](https://www.euronews.com/health/2026/06/18/lithuanias-plan-to-send-newly-trained-doctors-to-rural-areas-sparks-backlash)
  - Rokiškis district offers incentives to attract doctors; the search extract mentions an €18,000 grant over five years, while the headline cites €50,000. Primary care vacancies persist. The article is from about 2024 — [LRT](https://www.lrt.lt/en/news-in-english/19/2212225/we-have-no-other-option-lithuanian-municipality-offers-eur50-000-to-attract-doctors)
- **Hospital network reform (older):** An earlier plan set €418m to reorganise health institutions under minister Arūnas Dulkys (about 2022). Its current implementation status is unknown — [LRT](https://www.lrt.lt/en/news-in-english/19/1955610/lithuania-to-spend-eur418m-on-health-system-reorganisation)
- **E-health (ESPBI IS):**
  - As of February 2025 the system held about 357 million coded diagnoses from 126 million outpatient visits covering 3.7 million patients. Integration between different electronic health record systems "is still a challenge", and most clinical data sits locally in varying formats — [Int J Epidemiology 2025 data profile, PMC12228963](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12228963/)
  - Use has been mandatory since 2018; slow adopters (especially dental) were threatened with licence restrictions — [VLK](https://ligoniukasa.lrv.lt/en/news/ministry-of-health-all-lithuanian-medical-institutions-must-process-the-data-of-their-patients-in-the-e-health-system-as-of-1-september/)
- **Leadership:** Health Minister Marija Jakubauskienė served December 2024 to 14 July 2026; Linas Kukuraitis is listed as successor (Wikipedia; verify) — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marija_Jakubauskien%C4%97)

### Inferences
- Lithuania's main financial-protection problem is medicines and dental care, not hospital care. Its main capacity problem is nurses, nursing assistants and rural primary care, not the overall number of doctors (doctor density is high, but the distribution is skewed).
- Coercive rural placement (the 2026 residency rule) suggests incentives alone have not worked, but it risks pushing graduates to emigrate.
- **Solution opportunities:**
  - Pharmacy-led medicine affordability programmes and generic-switch tools.
  - Dental coverage for low-income adults and seniors.
  - Rural telemedicine hubs and mobile clinics.
  - Nurse-led primary care, task-shifting, and nurse retention through pay and scheduling (rostering platforms, career ladders).
  - Recruiting and integrating foreign-trained nurses.
  - Real-time waiting-list transparency and booking (a cross-provider slot marketplace).
  - Interoperability and clinician-usability layers for ESPBI.

### Gaps
- No 2025 VLK waiting-time statistics (average days to specialist by specialty or region) were found.
- No 2026 PSDF (Compulsory Health Insurance Fund) budget or deficit figures were found.
- The current hospital network or regional consolidation plans of 2025–2026 (including the July 2026 government's programme) were not found.
- No reporting on ESPBI outages or clinician complaints in 2025 was found; Lithuanian-language searches were not possible after the budget ran out.
- The doctor age profile (share over 55 or 65) was not obtained.
- The 2026 health-worker pay rise (+8%) is reported only by a non-authoritative blog; unverified.

## 6. Long-term care and elderly: care homes, home care, informal carers, the LTC reform, loneliness

### Takeaway
Long-term care is fragmented between the Ministry of Health, the Ministry of Social Security and Labour and the municipalities. It is badly understaffed: in 2019 there was 1 LTC worker per 100 people aged 65+ versus 4 in the EU. Home help is capped (up to 10 hours a week), and more than 1,000 people were waiting for care-home places in late 2025. A one-window Long-Term Care Services Law is being drafted but would only start in 2029. Loneliness is widespread: about 1 in 5 people aged 60+ report it. NGOs such as Sidabrinė linija and Caritas fill much of the gap, and loneliness is linked to the high senior suicide rate.

### Cited Findings
- **Governance and workforce:** LTC governance is fragmented across the Ministry of Health, the Ministry of Social Security and Labour and municipalities, producing unequal access because eligibility differs. There was 1 LTC worker per 100 people aged 65+ versus an EU average of 4 per 100 (2019) — [OECD, Integrating Services for Older People in Lithuania](https://www.oecd.org/en/publications/integrating-services-for-older-people-in-lithuania_c74c44be-en/full-report.html)
- **Service limits:** Older and disabled people may receive home assistance of up to 10 hours a week. Residential care is available for up to 6 months (short-term) or indefinitely (long-term) — [European Commission, Employment, Social Affairs & Inclusion](https://ec.europa.eu/social/main.jsp?catId=1119&intPageId=4663&langId=en)
- **Current capacity:** In November 2025 there was a shortage of long-term care places, with more than a thousand people waiting. The ministry hopes to add about 1,500 social care places by 2027 — [LRT (Lithuanian), Nov 2025](https://www.lrt.lt/naujienos/lietuvoje/2/2733062/truksta-vietu-ilgalaikes-globos-namuose-laukia-per-tukstanti-zmoniu)
- **Older capacity data:** In 2014, 47% of older people needing LTC were on a waiting list (old data) — [Eurocarers country profile](https://eurocarers.org/country-profiles/lithuania/). The Ministry once estimated the country needs about 80 senior living homes (older article) — [LRT](https://www.lrt.lt/en/news-in-english/19/1140241/elderly-care-homes-independent-living-or-isolation-trap)
- **EU funding:** As of 2025, the Recovery and Resilience Fund had allocated about €11 million to Lithuania for LTC, covering workforce training and municipal infrastructure — [Eurocarers](https://eurocarers.org/country-profiles/lithuania/)
- **The reform (draft Long-Term Care Services Law):**
  - Drafted jointly by the Ministry of Social Security and Labour and the Ministry of Health.
  - Integrates social care and nursing in a "one-window" model at home, in day centres and in inpatient facilities.
  - Sets three levels of need (low, medium, high), licenses providers, and allows electronic applications.
  - Funded from four sources: state budget, municipal budgets, the Health Insurance Fund and user payments.
  - The system is planned to start in 2029, and the package was planned for submission to the Seimas in autumn 2026.
  - Sources: [Žiniu.lt / Mano kraštas, Sep 2026](https://ziniu.lt/2026/09/ilgalaike-prieziura-lietuvoje-pagalba-zmogui-bus-teikiama-vieno-langelio-principu/); [77.lt](https://77.lt/dideli-pokyciai-nuo-2029-uju-slaugos-ir-globos-paslaugos-bus-jungiamos-pagal-vieno-langelio-principa)
- **Home nursing:** Home nursing funded by the Health Insurance Fund has run under a new procedure since 1 January 2025, with a questionnaire that scores need — [VLK (ligoniukasa.lrv.lt)](https://ligoniukasa.lrv.lt/lt/naujienos/slaugos-paslaugos-namuose-kas-pasikeite/)
- **Concerns about the reform:**
  - Nursing specialists are sceptical, mainly because of staff shortages — [Diena.lt](https://diena.lt/naujienos/sveikata/sveikata/slaugos-sistema-keisis-esmes-1773059)
  - Sector bodies warn of "chaos" in long-term care and ask for urgent decisions. Stricter staffing rules introduced in 2024 reportedly did not address the shortage (search extract; verify) — [TV3.lt](https://www.tv3.lt/naujiena/lietuva/perspeja-del-chaoso-ilgalaikes-prieziuros-srityje-praso-imtis-skubiu-sprendimu-n1427544)
  - A shortfall of about 40,000 nurses' assistants against recommended ratios was cited by a palliative-care association — [LRT, Jan 2025](https://www.lrt.lt/en/news-in-english/19/2457272/lithuania-is-short-of-3-000-nurses-experts-warn-of-system-collapse)
- **Loneliness:**
  - About 1 in 5 people aged 60+ experience loneliness (Lithuanian statistics cited in a 2025 Caritas report). Caritas launched its "Caritas Listens" support line in 2025 with Ministry of Social Security and Labour support, logging 67,308 minutes of conversation in its first year — [Catholic World Report, 22 Jul 2026](https://www.catholicworldreport.com/2026/07/22/as-pope-warns-of-pandemic-of-loneliness-caritas-lithuania-offers-a-catholic-answer/)
  - An older NGO estimate puts loneliness at more than a third of older people (definitions differ) — [Aukok.lt / Sidabrinė linija](https://www.aukok.lt/projects/Sidabrine-linija-People-Need-People)
  - Sidabrinė linija, founded in 2016, has befriended about 6,000 seniors through regular phone calls — [Aukok.lt](https://www.aukok.lt/projects/Sidabrine-linija-People-Need-People)
  - A Silver Line coordinator says more than half of older people have felt lonely at least once — [LRT](https://www.lrt.lt/en/news-in-english/19/2383834/they-ve-learned-to-suffer-suicide-rate-remains-high-among-lithuanian-elderly)
  - A SHARE-based study (4,429 people aged 50+ in the three Baltic states, 2017–2020) examined predictors of loneliness — [PMC8574871](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8574871/)

### Inferences
- With the reform not due until 2029, families (mostly women) and NGOs will keep carrying the load for at least 3 more years in a fast-ageing, emigration-hit population. Adult children abroad is a distinctively Lithuanian driver of isolation.
- **Solution opportunities:**
  - Home-care marketplaces and scheduling platforms for municipalities.
  - Remote monitoring, fall detection and telecare for rural seniors.
  - Respite and day-care services for informal carers, plus carer training and allowances.
  - "Social prescribing" linking GPs to befriending programmes.
  - Tools for diaspora families to coordinate and pay for care.
  - Recruitment and training pipelines for care assistants.
  - Scaling volunteer befriending (Silver Line, Caritas) with sustainable funding.

### Gaps
- No current national counts were found of care-home places, home-care recipients, LTC spending as a share of GDP, or the number of informal carers and their burden. The Eurocarers profile was found but its content was not extracted beyond the €11m figure.
- No official 2025 loneliness survey (State Data Agency or EU-SILC/Eurofound) was found; the 1-in-5 figure is second-hand via Caritas.
- No dementia care data was gathered.

## 7. Youth wellbeing: bullying (HBSC) and youth mental health

### Takeaway
Lithuania remains at or near the top of the HBSC bullying rankings. In the 2021/22 round, Lithuania had the highest victimisation of all 44 countries and regions for boys aged 11 (34%) and girls aged 13 (33%). Nationally about 27% of adolescents are bullied regularly and about 14% bully others. Lithuania is also among the top countries for boys' cyberbullying. Youth crisis lines are overloaded, and there are shortages of child and adolescent psychiatrists and of early-intervention services.

### Cited Findings
- **HBSC 2021/22 (44 countries and regions):**
  - Bullying victimisation for boys ranged from 2% (age 15, Belgium-French and France) to 34% at age 11 in Lithuania. For girls it ranged from 3% (age 15, Italy, Portugal and Spain) to 33% at age 13 in Lithuania. The highest boys' levels were in Bulgaria, Latvia and Lithuania — [HBSC international report, "A focus on adolescent peer violence and bullying"](https://www.hbsc.org/publications/reports/a-focus-on-adolescent-peer-violence-and-bullying-in-europe-central-asia-and-canada/); [PDF copy](https://www.hbsc.ch/pdf/hbsc_bibliographie_404.pdf)
  - The highest cyberbullying levels were among boys in Bulgaria, Lithuania, Moldova and Poland. Across all countries, about 16% of 11–15-year-olds were cyberbullied in 2022, up from 13% in 2018 — [ScienceAlert summarising WHO/Europe HBSC](https://www.sciencealert.com/shocking-extent-of-cyberbullying-among-kids-revealed-in-major-report)
- **Lithuanian HBSC team (2022 data):** About 27% of adolescents experience regular bullying, with little difference between boys and girls, and about 14% regularly bully others. There has been a slight decrease in victimisation among older adolescents since 2014 — [hbsc.lt presentation](https://hbsc.lt/wp-content/uploads/2024/04/Pristatymas.pdf)
- **2018 round (older):** Almost a third of Lithuanian adolescents were bullied, with a peak at age 13 (32% of boys, 31% of girls). Lithuania was also among the leaders for perpetration — [LRT](https://www.lrt.lt/en/news-in-english/19/1180226/lithuanian-adolescents-among-most-bullied-in-europe-who-survey); [Baltic Course](http://www.baltic-course.com/eng/analytics/?doc=155990)
- **Research links:** Bullying is associated with lower subjective happiness and wellbeing among Lithuanian adolescents — [MDPI 2025](https://www.mdpi.com/2673-5318/6/3/97). Cyberbullying and school bullying are associated with suicidality (HBSC Israel, Lithuania, Luxembourg) — [Crisis journal (Hogrefe)](https://econtent.hogrefe.com/doi/10.1027/0227-5910/a000536)
- **Youth help capacity:** Jaunimo linija receives more than 150 help requests a day (loneliness, anxiety, self-harm, suicide risk), runs on 494 volunteers (2025) and cannot answer everyone — [Aukok.lt](https://www.aukok.lt/projects/One-conversation-can-save-a-life)
- **Specialist and early-intervention shortages:**
  - Child and adolescent psychiatrists are in short supply — [OECD 2022](https://www.oecd.org/content/dam/oecd/en/publications/reports/2022/07/the-provision-of-community-based-mental-health-care-in-lithuania_3c11f47d/18de24d5-en.pdf)
  - Early intervention for children with developmental disorders shows significant regional disparities, long waits, few validated diagnostic tools and a shortage of qualified professionals — [European Journal of Pediatrics 2025](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00431-025-06554-2)

### Inferences
- Bullying is chronic despite a decade of programmes, which suggests weak fidelity or coverage of school-wide anti-bullying programmes and a culture that tolerates peer violence.
- **Solution opportunities:**
  - Evidence-based whole-school programmes (KiVa, Olweus) with sustained funding and outcome monitoring.
  - Anonymous reporting apps for students.
  - Cyberbullying moderation and education.
  - School psychologist capacity.
  - Paid staff for youth crisis lines.
  - Digital, low-threshold youth mental health services.

### Gaps
- The HBSC 2025/26 round results are not yet published (or were not found).
- No figure for Lithuania's 15-year-old cyberbullying rate or its exact rank was found.
- No youth suicide rate (15–24) for 2023–2024 was gathered.
- The coverage of KiVa/Olweus programmes in Lithuanian schools, the school psychologist ratio, and the Ministry of Education's current anti-bullying policy were not researched before the search budget ran out.

## 8. Domestic violence: prevalence and response

### Takeaway
Police recorded 54,602 domestic violence reports and 4,759 domestic violence crimes in 2024, and issued 21,022 protection orders, 81% of them against men. More than 13,000 cases involved people receiving repeat orders, which points to chronic reoffending. In the EU-GBV survey, about 31% of ever-partnered Lithuanian women have experienced intimate partner violence including psychological violence (16.6% physical or sexual only). Lithuania still has not ratified the Istanbul Convention.

### Cited Findings
- **Police data, 2024:**
  - 54,602 domestic violence reports.
  - 4,759 domestic violence criminal offences, of which 4,257 caused physical pain or harm.
  - 21,022 protection orders, 81% against men; in more than 13,000 cases the same person received repeat orders.
  - 85% of suspects were men.
  - Source: [Lygybės plėtros centras, "Gender Stereotypes and Domestic Violence in Lithuania" (public opinion survey 2025, published Dec 2025)](https://lygybe.lt/wp-content/uploads/2025/12/gender-based-violence_public-opinion-survey-in-lithuania-2025.pdf)
- **2025 trend:** Domestic violence, violent robberies and assaults fell about 6% in 2025 — [US OSAC Lithuania Country Security Report](https://www.osac.gov/Content/Report/b56016f0-f20d-4554-b2e6-1cec521730bd)
- **Victims (EIGE):** Women were 77% of domestic violence victims in 2022, and police recorded 3,485 women victims of intimate partner violence in 2022. Data on protection orders granted to victims are "not available" in EIGE's framework — [EIGE country profile](https://eige.europa.eu/gender-based-violence/countries/lithuania?language_content_entity=en)
- **Prevalence (EU-GBV survey, fieldwork about 2021, published 2024):**
  - 31% of ever-partnered women experienced psychological, physical or sexual intimate partner violence in their lifetime — [EIGE](https://eige.europa.eu/gender-based-violence/countries/lithuania?language_content_entity=en)
  - Lifetime intimate partner violence excluding psychological violence: 16.6% (EU-27: 17.7%). Including psychological violence: 30.7%. Past 12 months including psychological violence: 4.3%. ## 9. Cross-cutting: what has worked, what is failing, and where new solutions are needed

### Takeaway
What worked: aggressive alcohol control (2017 excise doubling, 2018 availability and advertising package), the post-2018 roll-out of low-threshold psychological services in all 60 municipalities, and medicine-reimbursement reforms that cut catastrophic spending. These coincided with the EU's largest life-expectancy gain (2010–2024). What is failing:
- Suicide among older and rural people.
- Workforce supply (nurses, rural GPs, care assistants).
- Out-of-pocket costs for medicines and dental care.
- Long-term care fragmentation, with reform not until 2029.
- Persistent youth bullying.
- Domestic violence reoffending.
- A fragile political consensus on alcohol.

### Cited Findings
- **Largest life-expectancy gain in the EU, 2010–2024**, and a 24% fall in alcohol consumption over 2013–2023, while policy has recently been relaxed — [Country Health Profile 2025](https://www.oecd.org/content/dam/oecd/en/publications/reports/2025/12/country-health-profile-2025-country-notes_7e72146d/lithuania_48218446/25bc0724-en.pdf)
- **Alcohol policy reduced all-cause mortality** beyond trend — [Štelemėkas et al. 2021](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/add.15470)
- **Catastrophic spending fell** from 11.5% to 9.4% (2016–2021) after medicine policies — [PMC12964039](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12964039/)
- **Suicides rose again 2022–2024**, concentrated among the 65+ — [Xinhua](https://english.news.cn/europe/20251009/aac5e0c11e2546e597ca344004406df4/c.html)
- **Nursing shortfall:** more than 8,000 nursing professionals short by 2032 — [Country Health Profile 2025](https://www.oecd.org/content/dam/oecd/en/publications/reports/2025/12/country-health-profile-2025-country-notes_7e72146d/lithuania_48218446/25bc0724-en.pdf)
- **LTC reform start date of 2029** — [Žiniu.lt](https://ziniu.lt/2026/09/ilgalaike-prieziura-lietuvoje-pagalba-zmogui-bus-teikiama-vieno-langelio-principu/)

### Inferences
Concrete unmet needs, ranked by impact and tractability (my synthesis):
1. **Rural and older men's mental health and suicide:** proactive outreach and follow-up after crisis or discharge; paid 24/7 crisis staff; tele-psychology for rural areas.
2. **Long-term care before 2029:** home-care coordination and telecare platforms; respite care; tools for diaspora families; scaled befriending against loneliness.
3. **Workforce:** nurse retention (pay, rostering, career paths); onboarding of foreign nurses; task-shifting to nurse-led and pharmacist-led care in rural municipalities.
4. **Affordability:** medicine and dental cost-reduction services for low-income groups and seniors.
5. **Men's cardiovascular and metabolic risk:** community screening, hypertension and smoking programmes.
6. **Youth:** sustained whole-school anti-bullying programmes, anonymous reporting, digital youth mental health.
7. **Domestic violence:** perpetrator programmes linked to protection orders, joint domestic violence and alcohol treatment, rural shelters.
8. **Data and transparency:** public waiting-time dashboards; ESPBI interoperability.
