## 1. Defense and civil resilience: budget, German brigade, conscription, shelters, civilian preparedness, public attitudes

### Takeaway
Lithuania is among NATO's highest defence spenders. Its 2026 budget is a record EUR 4.79bn, or 5.38% of GDP, with a 5–6% target for 2026–2030. A new defence tax package helps pay for it, and the German Panzerbrigade 45 is due to be fully ready in 2027. Civilian readiness lags far behind military spending. Shelters cover only ~53–56% of the population, often on paper only: locked or dirty, 91% not accessible to people with disabilities. The May 2026 Vilnius drone "red alert" exposed these gaps. Roughly half the population says it would take up arms, but only a small minority say they know exactly what to do in an emergency (2023 data).

### Cited Findings
**Defence budget and financing**
- The Seimas approved the 2026 defence budget of EUR 4.79bn = 5.38% of GDP, the highest share in Lithuania's history, on 11 Dec 2025 (80 for, 39 against, 7 abstentions). It is 43% above 2025 (EUR 3.34bn = 4.01% of GDP). — [KAM (Ministry of National Defence)](https://kam.lt/en/the-seimas-approves-record-defence-budget-of-eur-4-79-billion-or-5-38-of-gdp-in-2026//); [Ministry of Finance](https://finmin.lrv.lt/en/news/lithuania-approves-the-2026-state-budget-with-record-high-defence-spending-at-538-of-gdp-ifBM)
- About 60% of the MoD budget goes to modernising and expanding the armed forces, and personnel pay is ~EUR 650m. A separate 0.25% of GDP is earmarked for Ukraine support in 2026. The State Defence Council set a goal of 5–6% of GDP for defence in 2026–2030. — [KAM](https://kam.lt/en/the-seimas-approves-record-defence-budget-of-eur-4-79-billion-or-5-38-of-gdp-in-2026//); [LRT, 15 Oct 2025](https://www.lrt.lt/en/news-in-english/19/2715277/lithuania-plans-record-5-38-of-gdp-for-defence-spending-next-year)
- Lithuania is to receive EUR 6.3bn in EU SAFE defence loans. Planned uses include Baltic Defence Line counter-mobility measures and mines. — [KAM](https://kam.lt/en/lithuania-to-receiver-eur-6-3-billion-in-safe-defence-funding/)
- 2026 tax reform for defence: corporate income tax rises from 16% to 17%, a 10% "national defence contribution" applies to non-life insurance premiums, and a new excise duty applies to sugary drinks. — [The Conversation](https://theconversation.com/a-sign-of-europes-troubled-times-lithuania-brings-in-tax-reforms-to-boost-defence-spending-271275). CONFLICT: another site reports a 15%→16% corporate tax change — [Infoerdve (low-quality source)](https://infoerdve.lt/en/lithuanias-parliament-votes-on-defense-tax-package-impact-on-residents-and-business). Grant Thornton says the defence fund also draws on 2% of personal income tax plus insurance-premium and real-estate revenue — [Grant Thornton](https://www.grantthornton.ee/en/insights1/lithuanias-2026-tax-reform--what-to-expect/). Verify the rates against official sources.

**Military build-up**
- Baltic Defence Line was announced 19 Jan 2024. As of 2026, 27 counter-mobility parks were reportedly set up and fully equipped, and the MoD proposed expanding to 50. — [Wikipedia (secondary)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baltic_Defence_Line)
- Rheinmetall 155mm ammunition plant at Baisogala (JV Rheinmetall Defence Lietuva): groundbreaking took place in Nov 2025 — [Rheinmetall](https://www.rheinmetall.com/en/media/news-watch/news/2025/11/2025-11-04-groundbreaking-ceremony-for-munitions-factory-in-lithuania). Investment figures conflict: Rheinmetall says up to €300m in Lithuania, while Defense News cites €180m and said operations would start in mid-2026 — [Defense News, Mar 2025](https://www.defensenews.com/global/europe/2025/03/31/lithuania-buys-more-weapons-beckons-their-makers-to-invest-locally/). No confirmation found that production had started by Oct 2026.
- German Panzerbrigade 45 "Litauen" has been active since 1 Apr 2025 — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/45th_Panzer_Brigade_(Bundeswehr)). The plan is 4,800 soldiers and 200 civilian staff in Lithuania by 2027 — [Goethe-Institut](https://www.goethe.de/ins/lt/de/kul/sup/asp/var/rud.html). The Bundeswehr roadmap folds the Multinational Battlegroup Lithuania into the brigade and transfers battalions 122 and 203 in 2026, with full operational readiness in 2027 (described as "current planning") — [Defence Network](https://defence-network.com/panzerbrigade-45-litauen-erreicht-meilenstein/). Phase I construction (buildings, sanitary network, roads) of the Rūdninkai campus began in 2025. The camp was expected to take ~36 months, and the contractor was trying to shorten that — [KAM](https://kam.lt/en/?p=98412); [The Defense Post, Jun 2025](https://thedefensepost.com/2025/06/09/lithuania-german-military-town/amp/). Roughly 500 German personnel were in Lithuania in 2025 (per search summary of Lithuanian sources).
- Conscription: 5,000 conscripts in 2026 (call-up 2 Jan–31 Dec 2026), up from ~3,828 (2022) and ~3,845 (2024). The split is 3,870 for standard 9-month service, 90 for 3-month specialist service, 650 for junior officer training and 450 for basic training (90–120 days). Service in the Special Operations Forces becomes possible from 2026. — [Lithuanian Armed Forces (karys.lt)](https://www.karys.lt/en/communicate/news/2026-list-of-conscripts-finalized/763); [Euromaidan Press, 3 Dec 2025](https://euromaidanpress.com/2025/12/03/lithuania-conscription-2026-baltic-defense)

**Shelters and civil protection**
- Interior Ministry, March 2026: 6,513 shelters able to hold ~1.6m residents, ~56% of the population. 385 shelters are being modernised, with a target of ≥1,900 modernised shelters by 2030. — [Ministry of the Interior (vrm.lrv.lt)](https://vrm.lrv.lt/en/news/lithuania-seeks-to-learn-from-finland-focus-on-shelter-network-and-crisis-readiness-yrW2)
- National Audit Office (data at end-2024): 6,344 shelters, theoretically protecting ~53% of the population, still leaving a shortage of space for ~361,000 people. 91% of shelters are not accessible to people with disabilities. The legal targets are 60% coverage in urban municipalities and 40% in districts by 2030 (Civil Protection Concept). An EU-funded programme includes €52m for installing or modernising shelters. — [National Audit Office (Valstybės kontrolė)](https://www.valstybeskontrole.lt/EN/Post/18404)
- Sept 2025: 6,453 shelters for ~1.5m people (~54%) — [LRT, 11 Sep 2025](https://www.lrt.lt/en/news-in-english/19/2672539/lithuania-preps-its-bomb-shelter-network-what-you-should-know). Older figure (2023): ~3,300 shelters for 912,000 people (~31%), likely using a narrower definition — [LRT](https://www.lrt.lt/en/news-in-english/19/2065630/lithuania-looks-to-beef-up-disaster-preparedness-shelters-can-accommodate-third-of-population)
- Municipalities select, mark and prepare shelters. The Fire and Rescue Department (PAGD, under the Interior Ministry) coordinates them, and its Civil Protection Training Centre is the main civil-protection training body. Municipalities must draw up civil protection preparedness plans. — [Valstybės kontrolė](https://www.valstybeskontrole.lt/EN/Post/18404); [Law on Civil Protection](https://e-seimas.lrs.lt/rs/legalact/TAD/TAIS.281598/)
- **May 20, 2026 Vilnius drone alert:** a radar signature in Belarus near the border led to a yellow, then red alert for Vilnius county, with phone alerts ordering residents to shelter. Vilnius Airport closed, public transport was suspended, and President Nausėda, PM Ruginienė and Speaker Olekas were taken to shelters. NATO jets scrambled but could not locate the object, and the alert lifted around 11:00. Euronews described it as the first alert in an EU/NATO country since 2022 to order the whole population, including top leaders, to shelter. — [Euronews, 20 May 2026](https://euronews.com/my-europe/2026/05/20/vilnius-airport-shuts-and-nato-jets-scrambled-after-drone-warning); [Novaya Gazeta Europe](https://novayagazeta.eu/en/articles/2026/05/20/lithuania-tells-vilnius-residents-to-seek-shelter-after-drone-spotted-near-border-en-news)
- After the alert, residents found shelters locked, or deserted and covered in cobwebs, and a deputy defence minister acknowledged "some gaps that we would like to fix." — [Euronews, 3 Jun 2026](https://www.euronews.com/my-europe/2026/06/03/blind-spots-may-drone-alert-highlights-poor-access-to-air-raid-shelters-for-lithuanians); [AFP via WFMZ](https://www.wfmz.com/partners/afp/blind-spots-drone-alert-lays-bare-lithuania-poor-shelter-access/article_51f701d4-3c79-5cbc-9240-11d1ddc7f85c.html)

**Civil resilience training (2026, fragmented)**
- The Department of Mobilisation and Civil Resistance runs 8-hour courses for residents (e.g., Pagėgiai, 12 Jun 2026) focused on the "first 72 hours" and family safety planning. — [Infoerdve (low-quality aggregator)](https://infoerdve.lt/en/pagegiai-to-host-civil-resistance-and-emergency-preparedness-training)
- The National Public Health Center plans to train 20,000 residents (aged 16+) in emergency first aid from 2026. — [Infoerdve](https://infoerdve.lt/en/lithuania-to-train-20000-citizens-in-life-saving-emergency-skills)
- The Red Cross and municipalities ran free civil-defence tours (shelter and evacuation point inspection, family emergency plans) in 15 cities on 8 May 2026 — [Infoerdve](https://infoerdve.lt/en/birstonas-ir-dar-14-miestu-nemokamos-civilines-saugos-ekskursijos-gyventojams). Municipal evacuation-centre management training was held in Kėdainiai on 20–21 May 2026 — [Infoerdve](https://infoerdve.lt/en/kedainiai-district-conducts-mass-evacuation-management-training)
- Officials recommend a 72-hour emergency bag, checked twice a year. — [LRT](https://www.lrt.lt/en/news-in-english/19/1697516/amid-war-in-ukraine-lithuania-releases-survival-guide-what-should-be-in-your-emergency-bag)

**Public attitudes**
- MoD survey (Feb 2025): 52% would take up arms if Lithuania were attacked, 61% would join unarmed resistance, and 90% back NATO membership. — [LRT, 24 Feb 2025](https://www.lrt.lt/en/news-in-english/19/2495434/more-than-half-of-lithuanians-willing-to-participate-in-armed-resistance)
- Vilnius University study (2022–2024): 83% believe the country should be defended with arms, but only ~34% would personally contribute with weapons and ~68% through non-violent resistance. — [Vilnius University](https://www.vu.lt/en/all-news/if-day-x-came-who-would-defend-lithuania-and-what-would-motivate-them). Peer-reviewed comparison with Latvia and Taiwan (Dec 2025) — [Politologija](https://www.journals.vu.lt/politologija/article/view/42967)
- LRT poll (Sept 2025, before Zapad 2025): 15% "definitely" and 35% "somewhat" anxious about Russian/Belarusian drills, while 44% were not worried. Women and people over 65 were most anxious. — [LRT, 18 Sep 2025](https://www.lrt.lt/en/news-in-english/19/2681778/poll-half-of-lithuanians-anxious-over-russian-belarusian-military-drills)
- 2023 ministry survey (older data): only 9% said they know exactly what to do in an emergency and 41% "think they know." About a fifth had survival bags and a tenth had a family plan. — [LRT, 10 Jan 2023](https://www.lrt.lt/en/news-in-english/19/1863164/lithuania-launches-campaign-to-prep-society-for-war-crises)

### Inferences
- There is a big gap between military and civilian investment. Defence spending reached 5.38% of GDP, but the shelter programme cited is only ~€52m, and only ~385 of ~6,500 shelters are being upgraded. Shelter "capacity" is largely theoretical: basements, parking garages and similar spaces that are locked, unmaintained, unsigned or inaccessible.
- The May 2026 alert was a live stress test. Alerts reached phones, but the "last 100 metres" failed: knowing where to go, finding it open, and access for disabled and elderly people.
- Willingness to defend (~50% armed, ~60–68% unarmed) is high in principle. But practical household preparedness (2023: 9% confident, ~20% with kits) is low, and anxiety is concentrated among women and older people, the groups least targeted by military-centred programmes.
- Training is fragmented across MoD (civil resistance), Interior/PAGD (civil protection), the health centre (first aid), the Red Cross and municipalities, with no single citizen-facing entry point evident.
- **Solution opportunities:**
  - A real-time shelter status and access system: digital unlock or keyholder rosters, open/closed status, occupancy, and accessibility attributes, integrated into the phone alert so it carries "your nearest open shelter" routing.
  - Low-cost shelter retrofit kits (signage, ventilation, water, lighting, ramps) for apartment-block basements.
  - Building-manager or "block warden" training and certification.
  - A unified national preparedness app or one-stop portal that combines courses, family plans and kit checklists.
  - Preparedness programmes aimed at the elderly and people with disabilities.
  - Household kit subscription or refresh services.

### Gaps
- No 2026 national survey found on how many people know their nearest shelter or hold emergency kits; the latest national figure found is from 2023. One Vilnius survey snippet (67% see military attack as a major threat; only 25% of households have 3 days of drinking water) could not be attributed to a verifiable URL or date, so it is excluded.
- No 2026-dated confirmation of German brigade headcount, Rūdninkai construction status, or whether the Rheinmetall plant started production in mid-2026.
- No official confirmation of final 2026 defence tax rates (sources conflict). Public trust in government crisis management was not measured in sources found.
- No data found on national evacuation plans (e.g., Vilnius evacuation scenarios), their public availability, or rehearsal frequency.

## 2. Hybrid threats 2024–2026: balloons, drones, cable sabotage, GPS jamming, cyber, disinformation, migration — countermeasures and gaps

### Takeaway
Lithuania faces a continuous, multi-vector hybrid campaign from Belarus and Russia. Smuggler balloons repeatedly shut Vilnius and Kaunas airports in Oct 2025–Jan 2026, prompting a national emergency, border closures and Belarusian retaliation that stranded 1,000+ Lithuanian trucks. Drones, including one found with explosives, crossed from Belarus. GPS interference affects 1,000+ aircraft a month. Baltic cables, including Lithuania–Gotland, were cut. A GRU-linked arson hit Vilnius, AI-generated separatist propaganda spread on TikTok, and instrumentalised migration pressure rose again in 2025. Countermeasures are mostly reactive: closures, diplomatic protests, sanctions requests, NATO patrols and fences. Low-cost detection and interception of balloons and drones, and economic compensation for affected businesses, are visible gaps.

### Cited Findings
**Balloons from Belarus**
- In Oct 2025 Lithuania closed Vilnius and Kaunas airports and its Belarus border crossings after helium smuggling balloons (cigarette contraband) drifted in. Airspace above Vilnius was shut on Oct 5 and 21, cancelling dozens of flights, and a large launch came on Oct 24. Officials called it a deliberate act tolerated by Lukashenko's regime. — [Al Jazeera, 24 Oct 2025](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2025/10/24/lithuania-shuts-airports-belarus-border-crossings-after-balloon-sightings); [Kyiv Independent](https://kyivindependent.com/lithuania-shuts-down-airports-for-3rd-night-in-row-closes-border-crossings-indefinitely-due-to-balloons-from-belarus/)
- Minsk retaliated by preventing 1,000+ Lithuanian cargo trucks from leaving Belarus. Lithuania reopened the border in under three weeks, but the trucks stayed stuck, and Lithuania's president said Minsk rejected all requests to return them. — [NPR, 3 Dec 2025](https://www.npr.org/2025/12/03/nx-s1-5630000/belarus-weather-balloons-lithuania-airport); [Polskie Radio](https://polskieradio.pl/395/7785/Artykul/3614681,lithuania-urges-eu-help-to-free-trucks-stuck-in-belarus-seeks-new-sanctions-over-smuggler-balloons)
- Closures continued in late Nov–Dec 2025, with one December incident involving at least 60 balloons — [NPR](https://www.npr.org/2025/12/03/nx-s1-5630000/belarus-weather-balloons-lithuania-airport). Lithuania declared a nationwide emergency in Dec 2025 over the balloons, and in Jan 2026 the airport cited a "hybrid attack" by Belarus when restricting airspace (per search summaries).
- Scale (attribution among search results uncertain, OSW/Polskie Radio/Kyiv Post cluster): 613 balloon incursions recorded in 2025, affecting 320 flights and ~47,000 passengers. A later Transport Ministry tally cited ≥16 airspace violations, 370+ flights, ~53,000 passengers and losses exceeding €2.3m. — [OSW](https://www.osw.waw.pl/en/node/33641?page=1); [Polskie Radio](https://polskieradio.pl/395/7785/Artykul/3614681,lithuania-urges-eu-help-to-free-trucks-stuck-in-belarus-seeks-new-sanctions-over-smuggler-balloons)
- Countermeasures: diplomatic complaints to Minsk — [Pozirk](https://pozirk.online/en/news/166762); a request for new EU sanctions on Belarus and evidence submitted to ICAO — [Polskie Radio](https://polskieradio.pl/395/7785/Artykul/3614681,lithuania-urges-eu-help-to-free-trucks-stuck-in-belarus-seeks-new-sanctions-over-smuggler-balloons); former PM Skvernelis proposed compensating hauliers from frozen Belarusian assets — [Reform.news](https://reform.news/en/lithuanian-politician-proposes-compensating-hauliers-using-belarusian-assets)

**Drones**
- July 2025: a Gerbera-type drone entered from Belarus on 10 July, and another crashed in the Gaižiūnai military training area (Jonava district). Prosecutors later found it contained explosives. Officials suspected Russian drones diverted by Ukrainian electronic warfare. — [LRT](https://lrt.lt/en/news-in-english/19/2634744/explosive-device-found-in-drone-from-belarus-that-crashed-in-lithuania); [Liga.net](https://news.liga.net/en/amp/world/news/lithuanian-prosecutors-office-drone-that-flew-in-from-belarus-in-late-july-contained-explosives)
- 20 May 2026: the Vilnius red alert and shelter order after a suspected drone approached from Belarus (see Section 1) — [Euronews](https://euronews.com/my-europe/2026/05/20/vilnius-airport-shuts-and-nato-jets-scrambled-after-drone-warning); [IntelliNews](https://new.intellinews.com/articles/top-lithuanian-officials-take-shelter-after-air-alert-after-suspected-drone-approaches-from-belarus-443966)

**Sabotage (physical)**
- Prosecutors attributed the May 2024 arson at the Vilnius IKEA to Russian GRU military intelligence (announced March 2025), calling it terrorism. The GRU allegedly recruited two Ukrainian citizens under 20 as part of a group meant to commit attacks in Lithuania and Latvia. — [The Defense Post, 18 Mar 2025](https://thedefensepost.com/2025/03/18/lithuania-russian-arson/); [Kyiv Post](https://www.kyivpost.com/amp/post/49085)

**Undersea cables**
- The 218 km BCS fibre-optic cable linking Lithuania and Sweden's Gotland was severed on 17 Nov 2024, and the Finland–Germany C-Lion1 was damaged hours later. Germany's defence minister called it sabotage or "hybrid action." — [Marine Insight](https://www.marineinsight.com/two-undersea-data-cables-damaged-in-the-baltic-sea-sabotage-suspected/); [UkraNews](https://ukranews.com/en/amp/news/1048220-pistorius-admits-that-damage-to-cables-on-bottom-of-baltic-sea-sabotage-and-hybrid-action); [New Eastern Europe](https://neweasterneurope.eu/2025/02/26/baltic-tensions-rise-as-russias-phantom-fleet-strikes-cables/)
- On 25 Dec 2024, Estlink 2 (Finland–Estonia power) and four telecom lines were damaged, with the shadow-fleet tanker Eagle S implicated. At least 10–11 Baltic cables were damaged in ~15 months. — [Baird Maritime factbox](https://www.bairdmaritime.com/marine-projects/marine-infrastructure/factbox-suspected-underwater-cable-sabotage-in-the-baltic-sea); [Barchart/Reuters](https://www.barchart.com/story/news/30633425/at-least-11-baltic-cables-have-been-damaged-in-15-months-prompting-nato-to-up-its-guard)
- The Latvia–Gotland cable failure and Sweden's seizure of the bulk carrier Vezhen were later ruled accidental (anchor in high winds) by a Swedish prosecutor. - NATO launched Baltic Sentry on 14 Jan 2025 (frigates, patrol aircraft, naval drones). **GPS jamming and spoofing**
- Pilots filed 302 GPS-interference reports to Oro Navigacija between January and early March 2026. June 2025 was a record month (1,022 reports), and over 1,000 aircraft per month were affected in H1 2025, with about a 15% drop in H2 2025. The regulator says true counts are higher. — [LRT, 9 Mar 2026](https://www.lrt.lt/en/news-in-english/19/2862230/gps-jamming-incidents-reported-by-pilots-continue-in-lithuania-regulator-says)
- Russia expanded spoofing antennas in Kaliningrad from 3 (early 2025) to 36 (late 2025), with a range of up to ~450 km. Russia denies responsibility. — [To Vima](https://www.tovima.com/world/lithuania-warns-russia-can-spoof-gps-across-europe/amp/); [Reuters via syndication](https://935thelloyd.com/?p=971535)
- Lithuania's Seimas speaker said jamming is tied to Russia protecting Kaliningrad from air attack and "will persist as long as Russia's war continues." — [LRT, Jun 2025](https://www.lrt.lt/en/news-in-english/19/2587090/gps-jamming-will-persist-as-long-as-russia-s-war-continues-says-lithuanian-official)

**Cyber**
- National Cybersecurity Status Report 2025 (MoD/NKSC): 2,888 recorded incidents in 2025, ~25% fewer than 2024. Cybercrime fell 28% and detection improved 10.5%. Social engineering was the largest category. Attacks are described as deliberate, persistent and part of hybrid warfare, and reporting is incomplete. — [KAM report summary PDF (LT), Jul 2026](https://kam.lt/wp-content/uploads/2026/07/Santrauka_el-versija-final-Kibernetines-bukles-ataskaita-2025.pdf)
- 2024: NKSC registered 3,874 incidents (2,378 in 2023), with the rise attributed mainly to better reporting. **Disinformation**
- April 2026: the National Crisis Management Centre flagged an AI-generated TikTok separatism campaign ("Klaipėda People's Republic") claiming Klaipėda belongs to Russia. The VSD called it consistent with Russian information operations, and the Klaipėda prosecutor opened a pre-trial investigation. Reach was assessed as limited, but the campaign was seen as possible groundwork for a wider attack. — [LRT, 9 Apr 2026](https://www.lrt.lt/en/news-in-english/19/2893167/lithuania-says-it-identified-separatism-disinformation-campaign); [RTK (Radio & TV Commission) appeal to TikTok](https://www.rtk.lt/en/news/lrtk-appeals-to-tiktok-to-stop-the-spread-of-disinformation-about-the-klaipeda-peoples-republic)
- Telegram emerged as a key platform for Kremlin narratives in the Baltics, while in Lithuania TikTok had less traction than Facebook. — [BECID / Re:Baltica report, May 2025](https://becid.eu/wp-content/uploads/2025/05/d2.3.pdf); [RTK study](https://www.rtk.lt/en/news/study-telegram-is-the-main-channel-for-spreading-kremlin-narratives)

**Instrumentalised migration (Belarus border)**
- 2025: over 1,600 unauthorised crossing attempts from Belarus vs 1,002 in 2024 (LRT figures cited by Nasha Niva) — [Nasha Niva](https://nashaniva.com/amp/en/392681). 2026: 612 migrants denied entry by late April and 731 irregular crossings by 8 May. The peak was 50 attempts in 24 hours. The border guard says 25,000+ have been prevented since 2021, and a tunnel under the fence was found in Varėna district in May 2026. — [Pozirk](https://pozirk.online/en/news/186743/); [The Public](https://thepublic.info/en/news/naibilse-cyogo-roku-za-dobu-z-bilorusi-do-litvi-namagalosia-potrapiti-50-migrantiv-yix-vidpravili-nazad)
- The Sienos Grupė NGO counts 1,652 documented pushbacks in 2025 and 432 in 2026 (as of 17 Apr). It says the figures count incidents, not individuals (repeat attempts), and reports 8 deaths since pushbacks began in 2021. — [Sienos Grupė](https://www.sienosgrupe.org/en/statistics.php)
- OSW (Jul 2026): secondary migration from Latvia rose fourfold in H1 2026 year-on-year, with 905 cases at the Lithuania–Belarus border in the same period. — [OSW](https://www.osw.waw.pl/en/publikacja/34184/pdf)
- June 2025: clear-cutting along the Belarus border strip for surveillance (the State Forest Enterprise was allowed to cut 3,000 m³) angered local residents, and an environmental investigation was opened. — [LRT, 25 Jun 2025](https://www.lrt.lt/en/news-in-english/19/2597581/belarus-border-logging-sparks-outcry-officials-cite-security-concerns)

### Inferences
- Detection seems to work: radar picks up balloons, NATO scrambles. The response toolkit is blunt, though. Closing airports or borders imposes costs on Lithuania's own citizens and businesses (airlines, ~53,000 passengers, stranded hauliers) that may exceed the cost to the attacker. That asymmetry is the core vulnerability.
- No cost-effective way to defeat slow, cheap balloons and small drones was evident. Using fighter jets against $50 balloons is unsustainable. The May 2026 alert also showed that a single unconfirmed radar track can paralyse the capital.
- GPS interference is chronic, and officials expect it to last for the duration of the war. Aviation adapts, but resilience of GNSS-dependent civilian systems (agriculture, logistics, telecom timing, emergency services) was not documented.
- Cyber incident counts fell in 2025, but social engineering dominates. This points to citizen and SME awareness as the weak link, not just state systems.
- **Solution opportunities:**
  - Low-cost counter-balloon and counter-UAS systems near airports (passive RF and acoustic sensors, cheap interceptors, laser and EW).
  - Airspace management that narrows closures, by sector or time window, instead of shutting whole airports.
  - Insurance and compensation instruments for hybrid-attack losses (hauliers, airlines).
  - Resilient positioning, navigation and timing services (eLoran, multi-constellation and anti-spoofing receivers) for civilian users.
  - Cable monitoring (fibre-optic acoustic sensing, AIS anomaly analytics for shadow-fleet vessels).
  - SME phishing and social-engineering training.
  - Rapid-response debunking tooling, including AI-generated content detection in Lithuanian and Russian.
  - Border surveillance tech (sensors, tunnel detection) that reduces the need for clear-cutting forests.

### Gaps
- No 2026 update on whether the 1,000+ stranded trucks were returned, compensation paid, or EU sanctions or ICAO action delivered. No full 2026 balloon incursion count was found.
- No information found on specific counter-balloon or counter-drone technology Lithuania has procured or deployed.
- No 2025 attribution of specific cyber intrusions to Russia found (only 2022 Killnet DDoS precedent). The 2025–2026 VSD National Threat Assessment itself was not retrieved.
- No 2026 Baltic cable incident directly affecting Lithuania was confirmed, and the dating of the Vezhen and Lithuania–Latvia cable events in search summaries is suspect.
- Source quality: several balloon impact figures are from aggregated search summaries where the specific originating URL among the cluster could not be confirmed.

## 3. Energy: BRELL desync outcome, prices and volatility, import dependence, heating costs, offshore wind, prosumers, storage, hydrogen — household and business problems

### Takeaway
The 9 Feb 2025 synchronisation with continental Europe went smoothly, at a cost of ~€1.6bn, ~75% EU-funded. Lithuania's renewables boom is real. Wind and solar reached 5.5 GW by end-2025 and 6.2 GW by mid-2026, local plants covered ~73–74% of 2025 demand (up from 59% in 2024), batteries reached ~0.7 GW and prosumers ~170,000. Households still face high and volatile prices: February 2026 wholesale averaged ~155 €/MWh and the final consumer price was ~29.5 ct/kWh. Spikes are driven by low wind, interconnector maintenance and NordBalt derating. District heating bills doubled for some in January 2026, after VAT on heating went from 9% to 21% during a cold snap. Offshore wind has stalled: the second 700 MW tender failed with one bidder.

### Inferences
- Lithuania's local generation share jumped from 59% (2024) to 73–74% (2025), but price exposure remains. Pricing is set in coupled regional markets and constrained by a few interconnectors, NordBalt above all. Every maintenance outage or heat-related derating translates into household price spikes.
- The 2.2 GW installed vs 1.8 GW permitted prosumer gap, together with the shift to remote parks, signals grid-hosting limits. Midday solar gluts coexist with evening spikes, so the system needs flexibility (storage, demand response) rather than more midday solar.
- The offshore wind pipeline is the biggest hole in the 2030 strategy. A failed tender plus a single-owner first project without FID means no offshore power before ~2030+. Hydrogen is at pilot scale (127 t/yr vs a 129,000 t national ambition, ~1,000× short).
- The heating shock of January 2026 came from a policy decision (VAT 9→21%) colliding with cold weather in poorly insulated Soviet-era blocks. Energy affordability is thus tied to building renovation (Section 4).
- **Solution opportunities:**
  - Home batteries and smart-tariff optimisation apps (shifting load to cheap hours).
  - Community or virtual-power-plant aggregation of prosumers and batteries for balancing markets.
  - Heat pumps and thermal storage in district heating.
  - Dynamic-tariff literacy tools.
  - Grid-hosting-capacity maps and flexible connection agreements.
  - De-risking instruments (CfDs, state co-investment) to revive offshore wind bidding.
  - Targeted heating-bill support for low-income households and pensioners in place of the blanket VAT cut.

## 4. Transport and infrastructure: Rail Baltica, regional bus connectivity, road safety, Soviet-era housing renovation

### Takeaway
Rail Baltica is Lithuania's biggest infrastructure project and the most troubled. The European Court of Auditors (Jan 2026) says it will miss 2030 with no firm end date. Lithuania has secured only €1.6bn of a €5.6bn first phase, a 2026 planning document points to 2034 for the Lithuanian section (Kaunas–Vilnius 2033), and the project-wide gap is ~€10bn. Only Kaunas–Panevėžys (114 km) is under construction. Road deaths fell sharply (43 per million in 2024, below the EU average of 45), but rural public transport has shrunk. Renovation of ~35,000 Soviet-era apartment blocks is far too slow, per the European Commission (2026), and this directly feeds heating-bill shocks.

### Cited Findings
**Rail Baltica**
- European Court of Auditors (Jan 2026): the €23.8bn project will not be completed by 2030, and there is no final deadline for the full line — [IntelliNews](https://www.intellinews.com/eu-auditors-say-23-8bn-rail-baltica-project-will-miss-2030-deadline-final-completion-date-uncertain-421392/)
- The project-wide funding gap is ~€10bn. RB Rail's CEO said in June 2026: "No one can predict exactly how much money we'll get from the European Union, but it won't be enough." Funding depends on the next EU MFF (due end-2026 or early 2027). — [IntelliNews](https://www.intellinews.com/baltic-states-face-10bn-funding-gap-for-rail-baltica-451484/)
- Lithuania's first phase through 2030 is projected at €5.6bn, of which €1.6bn is secured (85% EU, 15% state). A government planning document (Apr 2026, not yet approved) sets 2034 for completing the Lithuanian section, with land acquisition from 2028 to 2030. — [UIRR](https://www.uirr.com/web-news/rail-baltica-lithuania-be-complete-2034); [LRT](https://www.lrt.lt/en/news-in-english/19/2904251/lithuania-aims-to-complete-rail-baltica-by-2033)
- The Kaunas–Vilnius section would cost ~€2.5bn, with completion by end-2033 (Transport Ministry special plan, unapproved). Only ~€200m had been earmarked as of early 2025. It requires ~2,500 land plots (~1,700 ha) and 467 buildings, including 83 residential. — [LRT](https://www.lrt.lt/en/news-in-english/19/2904251/lithuania-aims-to-complete-rail-baltica-by-2033)
- CONFLICT: Baltic heads of government reaffirmed 2030 at a meeting in Tallinn on 17 April, and the Lithuanian PM said national funds must fill any EU shortfall — [Baltic Times](https://www.baltictimes.com/baltic_leaders_must_show_will_to_complete_rail_baltica_by_2030__lithuania_pm_says/). This contradicts the 2033/2034 planning dates and the ECA finding.
- Construction status: 114 km of Kaunas–Panevėžys is under construction, with 8.8 km of track laid on the most advanced section. Two embankment contracts are worth €375.8m incl. VAT. Design procurement is under way for Panevėžys–Latvian border, depots and regional stations. — [LTG, 2026](https://ltg.lt/en/naujienos/2026/rail-baltica-in-lithuania-reaches-maturity-all-sections-advancing/); [Rail Baltica](https://www.railbaltica.org/news/rail-baltica-construction-in-lithuania-successfully-expanded-to-114-km-section-new-contracts-worth-e376-million-signed/)

Phase I for all three states is €15.3bn per CINEA (updated Feb 2026) — [CINEA](https://cinea.ec.europa.eu/featured-projects/rail-baltica-connecting-baltic-states-rest-europe-high-speed-train_en)

**Road safety**
- 2024: Lithuania had 43 road deaths per million (down 22% vs 2023, −52.6% vs 2014's 91) against an EU average of 45. Sweden was lowest at 20, and Romania (78) and Bulgaria (74) highest. — [European Commission, Oct 2025](https://transport.ec.europa.eu/news-events/news/road-safety-statistics-2024-progress-continues-amid-persistent-challenges-2025-10-17_en)
- Older data: 120 deaths in 2022 (lowest since independence), 148 in 2021 and 175 in 2020 — [LRT, Jan 2023](https://www.lrt.lt/en/news-in-english/19/1869533/lithuania-reports-lowest-number-of-road-deaths-since-independence). **Regional / rural public transport**
- Jan 2024: an intercity bus reform with stricter carrier and bus-stock rules left rural municipalities underserved. Šilalė (~5,000 people) lost its links to major cities when two routes were discontinued, and without subsidies private carriers won't run unprofitable routes. — [LRT, 19 Jan 2024](https://www.lrt.lt/en/news-in-english/19/2175651/lithuania-s-rural-municipalities-left-without-public-transport-amid-stricter-rules-for-carriers)

- Good practice: Tauragė made public transport free in 2020, and with three neighbouring municipalities created a regional ticket in 2023 (a €12/yr annual pass for unlimited regional travel) — [Adobe Express case page (Tauragė)](https://new.express.adobe.com/webpage/N7YugXW5TkxEL)

**Soviet-era housing and renovation**
- The European Commission's 2026 assessment says renovation in Lithuania is too slow to meet energy-efficiency goals, and the IEA calls for faster, neighbourhood-scale renovation of multi-apartment buildings linked to district heating — [Net Zero Compare, Sept 2026](https://netzerocompare.com/articles/building-retrofits-gain-importance-for-urban-resilience-across-the-baltics)
- After nearly two decades of preferential loans and subsidies (up to 30% of costs), fewer than 15% of eligible Soviet-era blocks have been renovated — [CEPR VoxEU](https://cepr.org/voxeu/columns/housing-market-structure-and-energy-efficiency-renovations)
- Housing stock: ~38,000 apartment buildings housing ~66% of the population, ~35,000 Soviet-era, with only ~4% renovated (undated figure, attributed in search summary to EBRD-related coverage; it conflicts with CEPR's <15%) — [CEEnergyNews (EBRD)](https://ceenergynews.com/finance/ebrd-makes-the-largest-direct-investment-in-renovating-privately-owned-buildings-in-lithuania/)
- Financing: Šiaulių bankas has helped renovate 3,000+ buildings with loans exceeding €1.2bn, and the EIB raised its investment to €255m. The new €275m "SB Modernizavimo Fondas" targets 600 buildings. — [Artea](https://www.artea.lt/en/news/the-newly-established-eur-275-million-sb-modernizavimo-fondas-will-help-renovate-600-apartment-buildings); [CEELM](https://ceelm.com/lithuania/28420-tgs-baltic-advises-eib-on-eur-60-million-increase-in-multi-apartment-buildings-renovation-financing-in-lithuania). EBRD 2021 loan of €67.5m, with ≥40% energy-performance improvement required — [Renewable Energy Magazine](https://www.renewableenergymagazine.com/energy_saving/ebrd-lends-a-67-5-million-for-20210804)

### Inferences
- Rail Baltica's official 2030 date is politically maintained but practically abandoned. Planning documents point to 2033–2034, and the ECA sees no end date. The Kaunas–Vilnius link, which matters most to ordinary Lithuanians, is the least advanced (land acquisition not before 2028) and the most underfunded (€200m of €2.5bn). Military-mobility value depends on completing the whole corridor.
- Rural residents are squeezed by shrinking bus networks, long distances and high fuel costs. This also undermines evacuation capacity in a crisis (Section 1), since carless rural elderly people depend on public transport.
- Renovation is the "hidden" link between energy prices, heating shocks and health. Collective decision-making by apartment owners (needing majority consent and taking on joint loans) appears to be the key bottleneck, rather than money alone.
- **Solution opportunities:**
  - Demand-responsive rural transport (on-demand minibuses, app- or phone-booked) and regional fare integration replicating Tauragė.
  - Digital tools for apartment-owner associations (voting, loan simulation, contractor comparison).
  - Industrialised prefabricated façade retrofits to cut cost and time.
  - One-stop-shop renovation services.
  - Data-driven road safety (speed and risky-road analytics, rural junction improvements).
  - Transparent public dashboards for Rail Baltica land acquisition and compensation, to reduce local conflict.

### Gaps
- No full-year 2025 road death count or 2025 EU ranking for Lithuania was found (the EC usually publishes in spring).
- No 2025–2026 data from BETA/APVA on the number of buildings renovated per year or current programme budgets.
- No 2025–2026 reporting on rural bus route numbers. The most recent hard data is from 2023–2024.
- Rail Baltica: the final MFF allocation is unknown, the Vilnius-connection timeline is still in conflict, and no cost-overrun breakdown for the Lithuanian section specifically was found.

## 5. Environment: Baltic Sea, Curonian Lagoon, forests, waste/recycling, air quality, climate adaptation (floods, storms, drought, farmers)

### Takeaway
The environmental picture is poorly covered by recent (2025–2026) Lithuania-specific data. The Curonian Lagoon remains in "poor ecological status," with longer cyanobacteria bloom periods driven by Nemunas River nutrients, mainly from agriculture. Baltic oxygen depletion is expected to worsen with warming. Lithuania risks missing the EU 2025 recycling target (49% in 2022 vs 55% required). Forest clear-cutting is contested, now including security logging on the Belarus border. Climate shocks are already hitting farmers: a nationwide emergency was declared in May 2025 after spring frosts destroyed up to 70–100% of fruit and berry harvests, and the Curonian Spit faces storm-surge erosion.

### Cited Findings
**Curonian Lagoon / Baltic Sea**
- Satellite time series (1985–2018, older data): cyanobacteria scum onset stays in June–July but now persists later, into October–November, with longer scum periods since 2008. The lagoon remains in poor ecological status, and the Nemunas River is the main nutrient source. — [MDPI Water, 2021](https://www.mdpi.com/2073-4441/13/13/1760)
- Lagoon toxins (2013–2017 data): 10 microcystins, nodularin, anatoxin-a and others were detected. Health risk was low overall but higher in the southern lagoon. — [Frontiers in Marine Science, 2018](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/marine-science/articles/10.3389/fmars.2018.00434/pdf)
- HELCOM: ~75% of nitrogen and ≥95% of phosphorus loads enter the Baltic via rivers or direct discharge, with diffuse agriculture the main source. The 2021 Baltic Sea Action Plan commits the nine coastal states, Lithuania included, to measures by 2030. Deep-basin hypoxia and anoxia are widespread. — [HELCOM](https://helcom.fi/helcom-pressure-group-meeting-focuses-on-reducing-the-input-of-nutrients-into-the-baltic-sea/); [HELCOM](https://helcom.fi/?p=17160)
- A Baltic Sea Action Group researcher (Feb 2026) expects oxygen deficiency to grow as warming and eutrophication continue — [Anadolu Agency](https://www.aa.com.tr/en/greenline/climate-change/global-warming-and-human-waste-continue-to-threaten-the-baltic-sea/1829096)

**Forests**
- June 2025: clear-cutting along the Belarus border (3,000 m³ permitted at the Border Guard's request) and on the Nemunas riverbanks in Lazdijai drew local outcry and an environmental investigation, and environmentalists sought alternatives — [LRT](https://www.lrt.lt/en/news-in-english/19/2597581/belarus-border-logging-sparks-outcry-officials-cite-security-concerns); [Derevo.ua](https://derevo.ua/en/publication/lietuvosbaltarusijos-pasienio-mik-plynieji-3532)

**Waste / recycling**
- In 2022, Lithuania prepared for reuse or recycled ~49% of municipal waste and is at risk of missing the EU 2025 target of 55%. It appears on track for the 2035 landfill target. — [EEA country profile, Mar 2025](https://www.eea.europa.eu/publications/many-eu-member-states/lithuania)
- The recycling rate fell from 48% (2016) to 45.2% (2020), partly because of new calculation rules. Landfilling was 16.3% in 2020, reduced through mechanical-biological treatment and incineration capacity. — [Council of the EU document](https://data.consilium.europa.eu/doc/document/ST-10478-2023-ADD-11/en/pdf); [EEA](https://eea.europa.eu/publications/managing-municipal-solid-waste/lithuania-municipal-waste-management)

**Climate impacts on farmers and coasts**
- On 28 May 2025 the government declared a nationwide state-level emergency over spring frosts that destroyed up to 70–80% of fruit and berry harvests in some areas, in some cases 100%. The Agriculture Minister became state operations manager and requested EU Agricultural Reserve aid. — [LRT, 28 May 2025](https://www.lrt.lt/en/news-in-english/19/2576586/lithuania-declares-nationwide-emergency-over-spring-frost-that-decimated-crops)
- Apple yields were down ~90% (SEB economist), with consumer prices expected to rise 10–20%. Spring frost was followed by heavy summer rain. — [FreshPlaza](https://www.freshplaza.com/europe/article/9756208/lithuania-s-fruit-harvest-hit-hard-by-spring-frosts-and-summer-rains/)
- Older precedent: a nationwide drought emergency in 2018 (harvest losses up to ~50%) — [IntelliNews](https://www.intellinews.com/drought-wreaks-havoc-on-lithuanian-farmers-as-state-declares-nationwide-emergency-144687). LRT (2019) described Lithuania as facing crop failures and extreme weather "three years in a row" — [LRT, 2019](https://www.lrt.lt/en/news-in-english/19/1059338/lithuania-at-the-center-of-climate-change-crop-failures-and-extreme-weather-three-years-in-a-row)
- Curonian Spit (UNESCO site): rising sea level, stronger storms and sand deficits have increased erosion in the southern part, and the national park monitors the protective dune ridge annually — [UNESCO](https://whc.unesco.org/document/224589). A Dec 2025 paper warns that more frequent severe storm surges could eventually breach the spit at its base — [Proceedings of YSU, 2025](https://journals.ysu.am/proceedings-geol-geogr/en/article/vol59_no3_2025_pp620-631). Shifting wind and wave directions (1950–2019) may destabilise sections of the Lithuanian shore — [AGU Ocean Sciences abstract](https://agu.confex.com/agu/osm20/preliminaryview.cgi/Paper645317.html)

### Inferences
- Agriculture is the common thread: it is the main nutrient source for the Nemunas, the Curonian Lagoon and the Baltic, and it is also the sector most exposed to climate shocks (frost 2025, drought 2018). Solutions that cut fertiliser runoff and raise farm climate resilience serve both goals.
- Security priorities now compete with environmental ones, through border-strip clear-cutting and marshland restoration for counter-mobility. Tech-based border surveillance could reduce the trade-off.
- The 2025 recycling target was likely missed: 49% in 2022 with a flat or declining trend, against 55% required. This exposes Lithuania to EU pressure and possible penalties.
- **Solution opportunities:**
  - Precision fertilisation and nutrient-runoff monitoring (sensor networks on Nemunas tributaries).
  - Constructed wetlands and buffer strips.
  - Frost-protection and early-warning services for orchards, plus parametric crop insurance (frost, drought).
  - Better separate collection and sorting of biowaste, packaging and textiles to close the gap to the 55% target.
  - Coastal erosion monitoring and nature-based dune restoration.
  - Satellite forest-cut transparency platforms to reduce conflict over logging.
