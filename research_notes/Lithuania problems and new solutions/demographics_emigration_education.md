## 1. Population size, natural decrease, fertility, ageing and projections to 2040/2050

### Takeaway
Lithuania has about 2.887 million residents (1 Jan 2026). That is down from about 3.7 million in 1991. The country has had natural decrease every year since 1993. In 2025 births fell to about 17,500, the lowest since independence, against about 37–38k deaths. The TFR is around 1.0. Positive net migration since 2019 now offsets most of the natural loss: the population fell only about 0.13% in 2025. Projections still show a shrinking, fast-ageing country. The national VDA projects 2.68m by 2050, while Eurostat projects 2.3m and the UN/OECD 2.2m. The working-age population is projected to fall 27–30% between 2022 and 2050.

### Cited Findings
**Population level**
- Permanent residents: 2,890,664 on 1 Jan 2025 and 2,886,883 on 1 Jan 2026. The 2025 change was −3,781 (−0.13%). This is a secondary site citing the official VDA/Statistics Department series; the figure was not checked on stat.gov.lt itself. — [mapijoziai.lt – Gyventojų skaičius Lietuvoje 1992–2026](https://mapijoziai.lt/gyventoju-skaicius-lietuvoje-1992-2020-m/)
- Provisional count: about 2.889 million at the beginning of October 2026. The same page also shows a headline figure of 2,887,761, so it is internally inconsistent. — [Lithuanian Data Portal](https://dataportal.gov.lt/en)
- The Registrų centras count of *registered* persons was 3,044,322 on 3 Jan 2026. This counts registrations, not residents, and should not be used as the population figure. Media have flagged confusion between the two counts ("Painioja statistiką…"). — [mapijoziai.lt](https://mapijoziai.lt/gyventoju-skaicius-lietuvoje-1992-2020-m/); [15min – Painioja statistiką](https://www.15min.lt/naujiena/aktualu/lietuva/painioja-statistika-kaip-is-tiesu-pasikeite-gyventoju-skaicius-lietuvoje-per-pastaraji-penkmeti-56-2678346)
- Long-run loss: about 3.706m in 1991 to about 2.885m at the start of 2024. Net migration was negative every year 1990–2018 and has been positive since 2019. — [Seimas Research Dept, Analytical review 25/82 "Lietuvos demografinė situacija 1990–2025 m."](https://www.lrs.lt/sip/getFile3?p_fid=111021)
- OSW: Lithuania is among the world's ten fastest-depopulating countries, having lost 821,000 people over three decades (2.885m on 1 Jan 2024). (Dec 2024, older.) — [OSW Commentary, 3 Dec 2024](https://www.osw.waw.pl/en/publikacje/osw-commentary/2024-12-03/lithuania-losing-people-without-a-fight)
- The UN WPP 2024 series is lower than the national series: about 2,830,144 at mid-2025 and 2,797,338 at mid-2026 (aggregator). The difference comes from a different estimation base. — [Worldometer (UN WPP-based)](https://www.worldometers.info/world-population/lithuania-population/)

**Births, deaths, natural change**
- Natural decrease has run every year since 1993, when deaths first exceeded births in independent Lithuania. — [Seimas review 25/82](https://www.lrs.lt/sip/getFile3?p_fid=111021)
- Births fell from 56.9k in 1990 to about 19k in 2024, the lowest of the independence period. — [Seimas review 25/82](https://www.lrs.lt/sip/getFile3?p_fid=111021); [77.lt summary](https://77.lt/nykstame-1990-m-lietuvoje-gime-56-tukstanciai-kudikiu-o-2024-m-tik-19-tukst)
- 2024: 18,979 births, the fewest of any year since 1990. — [LRT English](https://www.lrt.lt/en/news-in-english/19/2460129/lithuania-s-birth-rate-reaches-all-time-low)
- 2025: about 17,500 births, the lowest since independence was restored. Deaths were "over 38 thousand" per 15min citing official statistics. — [15min – Demografinė duobė](https://www.15min.lt/gyvenimas/naujiena/ar-zinai/demografine-duobe-kai-kuriose-lietuvos-savivaldybese-mirsta-7-kartus-daugiau-zmoniu-negu-gimsta-1634-2620972); conflicting death count of 36,869 in [China-CEE Institute briefing, 5 Mar 2026](https://china-cee.eu/2026/03/05/lithuania-monthly-briefing-demographic-decline-in-lithuania-causes-consequences-and-policy-responses/)
- Crude birth rate in 2025: 6.1 live births per 1,000, the lowest in the Eurostat series (via aggregator). — [Apiar Data (Eurostat)](https://apiardata.com/statistics/lt-live-births/)
- 2023 (older): 20,675 births and 37,043 deaths, natural change −16,368 (aggregator; source not clear). — [Georank](https://georank.org/birth-rate/lithuania)
- OSW (2024) cites "approximately 22,000" births and about 40,000 deaths a year in recent years. This is older and rounded. — [OSW](https://www.osw.waw.pl/en/publikacje/osw-commentary/2024-12-03/lithuania-losing-people-without-a-fight)

**Fertility (TFR)**
- The TFR collapsed in the 1990s, recovered to about 1.63 in 2010–2016, then fell again to a "historically very low" level in 2023. — [Seimas review 25/82](https://www.lrs.lt/sip/getFile3?p_fid=111021)
- 2025 TFR: about 1.0, a historic low. — [China-CEE briefing, Mar 2026](https://china-cee.eu/2026/03/05/lithuania-monthly-briefing-demographic-decline-in-lithuania-causes-consequences-and-policy-responses/). Wikipedia gives 1.03 for 2025 — [Wikipedia: Demographics of Lithuania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Demographics_of_Lithuania). A UN-modelled figure of 1.22 for 2025 is an outlier and contradicts the observed fall in births — [Statbase](https://statbase.org/data/ltu-fertility-rate/)

**Ageing and dependency**
- OECD: population 2.9m in 2024, 0.8m fewer than in 1992. It is projected to reach 2.2m by 2050, and the old-age dependency ratio to rise by almost 20 percentage points to 2050, "one of the highest in the OECD". — [OECD Economic Surveys: Lithuania 2025, ch. "Addressing demographic challenges"](https://www.oecd.org/en/publications/oecd-economic-surveys-lithuania-2025_4abf1ea5-en/full-report/addressing-demographic-challenges_c5c79266.html)
- OECD (ageing and employment assessment): old-age dependency ratio of 32% in 2022, projected 51% in 2040. — [OECD – Assessment of current situation, Lithuania](https://www.oecd.org/content/dam/oecd/en/topics/policy-issues/ageing-and-employment/assessment-current-situation-lithuania.pdf)
- OECD Box 4.1: working-age population falls 30% over 2022–2050 under UN assumptions and 27% under Eurostat's. The gap comes from migration assumptions: Eurostat includes Ukrainian inflows and positive net migration to 2050. Lithuania faces "one of the steepest declines in the working age population of any OECD country". — [OECD Survey 2025](https://www.oecd.org/en/publications/oecd-economic-surveys-lithuania-2025_4abf1ea5-en/full-report/addressing-demographic-challenges_c5c79266.html)
- Median age: 42.6 in 2026, projected 50.3 in 2050 (UN-based aggregator, indicative only). — [PopulationPyramids.org](https://www.populationpyramids.org/lithuania). Eurostat: Lithuania's median age could exceed 53 by 2100 — [Eurostat – Population projections in the EU](https://ec.europa.eu/eurostat/statistics-explained/index.php?title=Population_projections_in_the_EU)

**Projections**
- VDA's own projections (2026–2100) were prepared with the Centre for Demographic Research and Expertise from 12 fertility, mortality and migration scenarios; the central scenario is "medium" on all three. They give about 2.68m residents at the start of 2050, which is 210.4k (7.3%) fewer than at the start of 2025, and about 2.39m in 2075 (−17.4%). VDA argues international forecasts are less accurate for Lithuania. — [VDA – Population projections (EN)](https://vda.lrv.lt/en/news/population-projections-in-lithuania-are-being-presented-at-the-state-data-agency-statistics-lithuania-mrB/); [VDA (LT)](https://vda.lrv.lt/lt/naujienos/valstybes-duomenu-agenturoje-pristatytos-gyventoju-skaiciaus-prognozes-lietuvoje-5Pe/)
- International comparators: Eurostat about 2.3m by 2050, UN about 2.2m by 2050 (as summarised alongside the VDA release). — [VDA](https://vda.lrv.lt/en/news/population-projections-in-lithuania-are-being-presented-at-the-state-data-agency-statistics-lithuania-mrB/); [OECD Survey 2025](https://www.oecd.org/en/publications/oecd-economic-surveys-lithuania-2025_4abf1ea5-en/full-report/addressing-demographic-challenges_c5c79266.html)

### Inferences
- Arithmetic check for 2025: births of about 17.5k and deaths of about 37–38k give a natural change of roughly −19k to −21k. The population fell only about 3.8k, which implies net migration of about +16k. That matches the migration figures in section 2 (+16.2k), so the numbers are internally consistent. Migration, not fertility, is now the main lever on the headline population number.
- The VDA projection (2.68m in 2050) is far more optimistic than the UN/OECD figure (2.2m) because it assumes continued positive net migration. If foreign-worker inflows fall, because of quota caps, security-driven restrictions on Belarusians/Russians, or departing Ukrainians, the outcome will drift toward the UN path. The difference is about 480k people, roughly 17% of today's population.
- A TFR near 1.0 is among the lowest in the EU. Even with migration, each new birth cohort is about one-third of the 1990 cohort (17.5k vs 56.9k). This drives the school-closure and teacher-demand dynamics in section 5.
- **Solution gaps:** (a) Policymakers lack a single trusted public dashboard: residents vs registered persons vs UN figures are routinely confused in the media. (b) Nobody seems to be doing municipality-level population, school and health-demand forecasting from the VDA scenarios (see section 3).

### Gaps
- The official VDA 2025 TFR, median age and old-age dependency ratio for 1 Jan 2026 could not be confirmed on stat.gov.lt because fetches failed. The TFR is about 1.0–1.03 from secondary sources.
- The VDA 2040 projection figure and the Eurostat EUROPOP2023 2050 old-age dependency ratio for Lithuania were not found.
- Exact 2025 deaths: "over 38k" (15min) vs 36,869 (China-CEE). Unresolved.

## 2. Emigration, immigration, return migration and the makeup of newcomers; integration challenges

### Takeaway
Since 2019 Lithuania has had net immigration. About 40% of immigrants are returning Lithuanian citizens, mostly from the UK, Norway, Germany and Ireland. In 2024 emigration of Lithuanian citizens hit its lowest level since 2001 (about 9.5k). The foreign population is about 217k (about 7.5% of residents), dominated by Ukrainians (about 80k) and Belarusians (about 50k), with fast growth from Uzbekistan, Tajikistan and India. That stock stopped growing in 2024–2025, and foreign churn is high. Foreign immigration fell about 33% in 2024 and fell again in 2025, and about 18.9k foreigners left in 2025. A single national quota for third-country workers now caps inflows.

### Cited Findings
**2024 flows (VDA/Statistics Lithuania)**
- 2024 immigration was about 51.8k. 18.9k were returning citizens (36.4% of immigrants, up 7.7% on 2023) and 32.9k foreigners (63.6%), which is 16.2k (32.9%) fewer foreigners than in 2023. 28.7k residents emigrated. Overall net migration was +23.1k, and the citizens-only balance was +9.4k. Positive net migration was driven by Ukrainian war refugees and arrivals from Belarus, Uzbekistan, Tajikistan and India. — [Statistics Lithuania, "Lietuva skaičiais 2025 – Resident population"](https://publikacijos.stat.gov.lt/lietuva-skaiciais-2025/en/categories/3)
- Migration Department (2024): 9,486 citizens left and 18,934 returned. Top countries of return were the UK (6,581), Norway (2,064) and Germany (1,439). Citizen emigration of about 9.46k was a historic low since 2001. — [Migration Department – Migration Yearbook key statistics](https://migracija.lrv.lt/en/news/latest-migration-yearbook-key-statistics/); [EMN Lithuania – Migration trends](https://123.emn.lt/en/migration-trends/)
- The EMN gives about 52k immigrants and almost 29k emigrants for 2024, consistent with the figures above. — [EMN Lithuania](https://123.emn.lt/en/migration-trends/)

**2025 flows (preliminary)**
- 18,155 Lithuanian citizens returned and 9,669 left in 2025. This was the sixth consecutive year in which more citizens returned than left. — [Ministry of Foreign Affairs (URM) – "Šešerius metus iš eilės į Lietuvą grįžta daugiau piliečių, nei išvyksta"](https://www.urm.lt/naujienos/141/seserius-metus-is-eiles-i-lietuva-grizta-daugiau-pilieciu-nei-isvyksta:45511)
- Returning citizens were "slightly more than 40%" of all 2025 immigrants. Total emigration was about 28k, of which nearly 10k were citizens (slightly more than in 2024). Immigration was lower than in 2024, with fewer arrivals from some third countries. Net migration stayed positive. Top countries of return in 2025: UK 7,429, Norway 2,119, Germany 1,680, Ireland 1,345. — [laikmetis.lt – "Statistika negailestinga"](https://laikmetis.lt/statistika-negailestinga-lietuva-mazeja-padetis-taisoma-per-imigracija/); [URM](https://www.urm.lt/naujienos/141/seserius-metus-is-eiles-i-lietuva-grizta-daugiau-pilieciu-nei-isvyksta:45511); [Duomenų galia – Migracija 2025](https://www.duomenugalia.lt/lietuvoje/migracija-lietuvoje-2025-m)
- Foreigners in 2025: 26,550 arrived and 18,871 left, a net of +7,679 (secondary site; it was not fully clear whether these counts cover foreigners only). — [Duomenų galia – Migracija Lietuvoje 2025](https://www.duomenugalia.lt/lietuvoje/migracija-lietuvoje-2025-m)
- A World Bank indicator listing 2025 net migration as −24,618 conflicts with all national data. It is probably a modelled UN estimate and should not be used. — [World Bank – Lithuania](https://data.worldbank.org/country/lithuania)

**Stock and composition of foreigners**
- Foreigners living in Lithuania: 221,848 at the start of 2024 and 217,290 at the start of 2025, the first year-on-year decline since 2016. — [Migration Department Yearbook](https://migracija.lrv.lt/en/news/latest-migration-yearbook-key-statistics/)
- 1 Jan 2026: 217,067 foreigners legally resident. Ukrainians numbered 79,868 (77,058 on temporary permits) and Belarusians 50,286 (46,122 temporary). Russians were about 13.7k (1 Dec 2025). — [Migration Department – "Imigrantai Lietuvoje, 2026 m. sausio 1 d."](https://migracija.lrv.lt/public/canonical/1767790317/1508/imig-2026-01.pdf)
- Earlier points: 1 Oct 2025, Ukrainians 77,351, Belarusians 51,213, Russians 14,005. At the start of 2024 there were over 86k Ukrainians, and about 95k a year earlier, so the Ukrainian stock is shrinking. — [Migration Department news](https://migracija.lrv.lt/lt/naujienos/naujausiame-migracijos-metrastyje-aktualiausi-statistiniai-rodikliai/)
- IMF: the foreign-born population roughly doubled to about 8% between 2022 and 2024. Ukrainians and Belarusians were the largest groups. — [IMF Selected Issues Paper SIP/2025/138, "Potential Growth and Migration" (L. Boer)](https://www.imf.org/-/media/Files/Publications/Selected-Issues-Papers/2025/English/SIPEA2025138.ashx)
- In 2025, temporary residence permit applications were led by Ukraine, then Uzbekistan, Belarus, India and Tajikistan. Most were for employment. — [EUAA – Migration Department publishes 2025 annual report](https://dip.euaa.europa.eu/developments/lithuania/migration-department-publishes-its-2025-annual-report)
- Migration Department: foreigners arriving in Lithuania now stay longer (headline only; detail not retrieved). — [Migration Department](https://migracija.lrv.lt/lt/naujienos/i-lietuva-atvykstantys-uzsienieciai-musu-salyje-uzsibuna-ilgiau/)

**Labour-migration regime and integration**
- Since 2025, sector-specific quotas have been replaced by a single annual national quota for third-country workers. The 2026 quota is cited as 24,706 (Migration Department data via search summary). Reportedly it was exhausted on 7 Sep 2026, but that comes from a low-authority travel site and is unconfirmed. LRT reported about 20,260 of 24,830 quota places used by 27 July; the year is not clearly 2025 or 2026. — [LRT – Lithuania nears annual foreign worker quota](https://www.lrt.lt/en/news-in-english/19/3006058/lithuania-nears-annual-foreign-worker-quota); [Travel And Tour World](https://www.travelandtourworld.com/news/article/mp9yetmz85ik/). One search summary described the quota as "1.4% of population", but 24,706 is only about 0.86% of 2.89m. The definition is unverified.
- Instruments: work permits based on labour-market tests, a Shortage Occupations List and a high-value-added list. A lower salary threshold of one national average wage applies to listed shortage occupations. — [Migration Information Centre – Work permit](https://micenter.lt/en/work-permit); [Baltic Capital Partners – shortage list](https://balticcapitalpartners.com/eu-critical-skills-shortage-list/lithuania/); [EMN study "Labour Migration in Times of Labour Shortages – Lithuania"](https://www.emn.lt/uploads/Products/product_2098/IOM_shortages_EN_FINAL.pdf)
- From 2026, customer service must be "only in Lithuanian", tightening language requirements for foreign workers in service jobs (headline: "Nuo 2026-ųjų su klientais tik lietuviškai: kas laukia užsieniečių Lietuvoje"). — [tv3.lt](https://www.tv3.lt/naujiena/lietuva/nuo-2026-uju-su-klientais-tik-lietuviskai-kas-laukia-uzsienieciu-lietuvoje-n1460100)
- In September 2026 public debate turned to the need for the state to define "how many and what kind of migrants Lithuania needs" (headline). — [alkas.lt, 16 Sep 2026](https://alkas.lt/2026/09/16/kiek-ir-kokiu-migrantu-reikia-lietuvai-valstybes-kryptis/)
- The Education Ministry reports that some higher-education institutions have difficulties selecting foreign students (headline). — [tv3.lt](https://www.tv3.lt/naujiena/lietuva/ministerija-atrenkant-uzsieniecius-studentus-sunkumai-ne-visoms-aukstosioms-mokykloms-n1480487)
- OECD recommends sustaining positive net migration, encouraging return migration and "improving the system for attracting foreign workers". — [OECD Survey 2025](https://www.oecd.org/en/publications/oecd-economic-surveys-lithuania-2025_4abf1ea5-en.html); [OECD press release, Mar 2025](https://www.oecd.org/en/about/news/press-releases/2025/03/lithuania-will-need-to-strengthen-fiscal-sustainability-tackle-labour-shortages-and-foster-productivity-to-address-ageing-challenges.html)
- The IMF recommends integrating migrants "in the most productivity-enhancing way" and letting foreign professionals into the sectors with the largest shortages. — [IMF SIP/2025/138](https://www.imf.org/-/media/files/publications/selected-issues-papers/2025/english/sipea2025138.pdf)
- EU integration-hub country page and IOM/EMN 2025 migration-policy overview (content not retrieved). — [European Commission – Migrant integration in Lithuania](https://home-affairs.ec.europa.eu/policies/migration-and-asylum/migrant-integration/migrant-integration-hub/eu-countries-updates-and-facts/migrant-integration-lithuania_en); [EMN/IOM "Migration policy in Lithuania 2025"](https://www.emn.lt/uploads/Products/product_2103/IOM_politika_2025_EN_FINAL_VERSION.pdf)

### Inferences
- Arithmetic check for 2025: 18,155 returning citizens plus 26,550 foreign arrivals is about 44.7k immigrants, and returnees are 40.6% of that, which matches "slightly more than 40%". 9,669 citizens plus 18,871 foreigners leaving is about 28.5k emigrants, matching "about 28k". Net migration was therefore about +16.2k: about +8.5k citizens and about +7.7k foreigners. Net immigration fell from about +23.1k (2024) to about +16k (2025).
- Foreign churn is high: about 19k foreigners left in 2025 against about 26.5k arriving. Many third-country workers on temporary permits do not settle. This points to weak retention and integration (housing, language, family reunification) as a bigger problem than recruitment alone.
- Inflows face policy ceilings: a national quota of about 24.7k that appears to run out before year-end, stricter language rules from 2026, and security screening of Belarusians and Russians. Together these sit in tension with OECD/IMF advice to sustain migration.
- Return migration is now steady at about 18–19k a year and is concentrated from the UK. Returnees are a large, identifiable flow that needs re-entry services: school placement for children who speak little Lithuanian, recognition of skills, and jobs outside Vilnius.
- **Solution gaps:** (a) affordable, scalable Lithuanian-language learning for adult workers and their families, timed to the 2026 service-language rule; (b) employer-side integration and retention services (housing, onboarding, family reunification help) for Central Asian and Indian workers; (c) a "returnee concierge" connecting UK/Norway/Ireland returnees with regional jobs, housing and schools; (d) transparent tracking of quota usage so employers can plan.

### Gaps
- No full-year 2025 breakdown of immigrants by citizenship (Uzbekistan, Tajikistan, India, Kyrgyzstan etc.) was retrieved. Only the 2025 ranking of temporary residence permit *applications* is available.
- No data was found on integration outcomes (employment rates of migrants, language-course take-up, discrimination, housing conditions) or on public attitudes to migrants. The polling search could not be run (search budget exhausted).
- The 2026 quota size (24,706 vs 24,830), its legal definition, and the claimed exhaustion date of 7 Sep 2026 are unconfirmed.
- The details of the 2026 Lithuanian-only service-language requirement (which law, which sectors, enforcement) are known from the headline only.

## 3. Rural and small-town depopulation; which municipalities are emptying; what happens to services

### Takeaway
Depopulation is concentrated in the east (Ignalina, Zarasai), the north (Akmenė) and the southwest periphery (Kalvarija). In 2025, 52 of 60 municipalities lost population. Ignalina lost about 3.4–4.2% in a single year and recorded about 8 deaths for every birth. Over 1996–2025, 55 of 60 municipalities shrank. Growth is limited to the Vilnius/Kaunas metro belts: in 2025 Kauno rajonas was the only municipality with more births than deaths. The service consequences (school mergers, nurse scarcity, fewer GPs) are well documented for schools and health but poorly documented for post offices and transport.

### Cited Findings
- In 2025, 52 municipalities lost population, and Ignalina district had the largest drop at −3.42%. A different source gives −577 people (−4.19%), so the methodologies differ. — [Duomenų galia – Gyventojų skaičius 2025](https://www.duomenugalia.lt/lietuvoje/lietuvos-gyventoju-skaicius-2025-m); [mapijoziai.lt – Gyventojų skaičius savivaldybėse 2026](https://mapijoziai.lt/gyventoju-skaicius-savivaldybese-2020-m/)
- Over 1996–2025 the population fell in 55 of 60 municipalities. — [mapijoziai.lt – Savivaldybės 1996–2025](https://mapijoziai.lt/lietuvos-savivaldybes-gyventoju-skaicius-1996-2020-m/)
- In 2025 some remote municipalities recorded about 7–9 times more deaths than births. Ignalina: 39 births, 319 deaths. Akmenė district: 99 births, 345 deaths. Birštonas: 16 births, 69 deaths. Only Kauno rajonas had more births than deaths. — [15min – Demografinė duobė (2026)](https://www.15min.lt/gyvenimas/naujiena/ar-zinai/demografine-duobe-kai-kuriose-lietuvos-savivaldybese-mirsta-7-kartus-daugiau-zmoniu-negu-gimsta-1634-2620972); [15min – full municipal statistics](https://www.15min.lt/gyvenimas/naujiena/ar-zinai/pilna-statistika-kiek-kiekvienoje-lietuvos-savivaldybeje-pernai-zmoniu-gime-kiek-mire-ir-kiek-gyvena-dabar-1634-2649182); [15min – 10 best-placed municipalities](https://www.15min.lt/gyvenimas/naujiena/ar-zinai/i-demografine-duobe-grimzta-ne-visi-10-lietuvos-savivaldybiu-kuriose-situacija-geriausia-1634-2625128)
- Kalvarija municipality: about 9,913 residents (−233, about −2% in a year). Other sources give 9,595 or 9,490, depending on date and source. Zarasai district: 14,027 residents at the start of 2025. — [lt.wikipedia – Kalvarijos savivaldybė](https://lt.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kalvarijos_savivaldyb%C4%97); [mapijoziai.lt](https://mapijoziai.lt/lietuvos-savivaldybes-gyventoju-skaicius-1996-2020-m/)
- Šilutė / Pamarys region (west): deaths continue to exceed births (local media headline). — [Šilutės naujienos](https://www.silutesnaujienos.lt/lt/pamaryje-ir-toliau-mirsta-daugiau-nei-gimsta-o-naujagimiu-vardus-sunku-istarti/)
- Health services: the density of nurses in municipal-level institutions varied 10-fold across municipalities in 2023. Rural access to care is particularly weak. — [OECD/European Observatory – State of Health in the EU: Lithuania Country Health Profile 2025](https://www.oecd.org/content/dam/oecd/en/publications/reports/2025/12/country-health-profile-2025-country-notes_7e72146d/lithuania_48218446/25bc0724-en.pdf)
- Schools: the EU Education and Training Monitor 2025 says many schools, "particularly in rural areas", are having trouble adapting to the network rationalisation. Teacher shortages are concentrated in rural schools. — [EC Education and Training Monitor 2025 – Lithuania](https://op.europa.eu/webpub/eac/education-and-training-monitor/en/country-reports/lithuania.html)
- Some municipalities are choosing to keep their networks intact. Lazdijai's 2026–2030 school-network plan foresees no school liquidations in the next five years, applying a general minimum of 60 pupils per school. — [Dzūkų žinios, Mar 2026](https://dzukuzinios.lt/2026/03/asta-zablackiene-per-artimiausia-penkmeti-nenumatytas-ne-vienos-mokyklos-likvidavimas/)
- A Seimas Research Department analytical review on regional policy (25/30, 12 Mar 2025) exists; content not retrieved. — [lrs.lt](https://www.lrs.lt/sip/getFile3?p_fid=94718)

### Inferences
- In places like Ignalina, Akmenė and Zarasai, natural decrease alone is about 2% of the population a year (Ignalina: about 280 net deaths on roughly 13k residents). Even zero emigration would not stabilise these areas, so service planning there has to assume continued shrinkage.
- For ordinary residents of these municipalities, the result is an older, more isolated population. Fewer children means schools merge and pupils travel further. Nurse scarcity, 10-fold variation across municipalities, means uneven access to home care and primary care for the elderly.
- **Solution gaps:** (a) mobile and shared-service models: multi-function service hubs combining post, pharmacy, telemedicine and municipal services; on-demand rural transport; (b) home care and remote-monitoring services for older people living alone; (c) municipality-level demographic forecasting tools to plan school and clinic networks; (d) incentives or remote-work hubs to draw returnees and remote workers to small towns.

### Gaps
- No sourced data was retrieved on closures of Lietuvos paštas post offices, cuts to rural bus routes, or closures and mergers of local outpatient clinics (ambulatorijos) and hospitals. These searches could not be run after the search budget was exhausted.
- No authoritative VDA ranking of the fastest-shrinking municipalities over 2015–2025 was retrieved. A 15min "10 municipalities with the largest 10-year decline" article exists but its content was not obtained: [15min](https://www.15min.lt/gyvenimas/naujiena/ar-zinai/kuriose-lietuvos-savivaldybese-gyventoju-skaicius-labiausiai-mazeja-o-kur-auga-1634-2416476).
- The urban/rural population split and the internal-migration flows to Vilnius were not obtained.

## 4. Labour shortages by sector; impact on wages and the economy

### Takeaway
The labour market remains tight with high vacancies even after the 2022–2024 migrant inflow. The OECD calls labour shortages a drag on growth and exports. Documented shortages are sharpest in transport (truck drivers), healthcare (especially nurses and regional GPs), engineering/IT, construction, social work and teaching (maths and Lithuanian). Health has the best quantitative evidence: 7.5 nurses per 1,000 (2023), a 10-year low, and projected shortfalls of 269 family doctors, 207 internists and 146 paediatricians by 2032. The government response is controversial, for example tying new residency places to five years of service in a region.

### Cited Findings
- Immigration "has helped alleviate some labour shortages, but the labour market remains tight with high vacancies". — [OECD Economic Outlook 2025 Issue 1 – Lithuania](https://www.oecd.org/en/publications/2025/06/oecd-economic-outlook-volume-2025-issue-1_1fd979a8/full-report/lithuania_7b382467.html)
- OECD: sustaining positive net migration would partly ease labour shortages "and their drag on growth and exports". It also recommends employing older workers, reforming VET for youth skill shortages, private pension schemes, rationalising the hospital network and promoting healthier lifestyles. — [OECD Survey 2025](https://www.oecd.org/en/publications/oecd-economic-surveys-lithuania-2025_4abf1ea5-en.html); [OECD ECOSCOPE blog, 28 Mar 2025](https://oecdecoscope.blog/2025/03/28/lithuania-addressing-demographic-challenges/?noamp=mobile&print=print); [OECD press release](https://www.oecd.org/en/about/news/press-releases/2025/03/lithuania-will-need-to-strengthen-fiscal-sustainability-tackle-labour-shortages-and-foster-productivity-to-address-ageing-challenges.html)
- Shortage occupations (third-party recruitment site, not the official list): truck and passenger-transport drivers rank first and doctors second, followed by engineers, couriers, programmers, construction workers and social workers. — [WageCentre – Shortage occupations in Lithuania 2026](https://wagecentre.com/work/work-in-europe/shortage-occupations-in-lithuania)
- Health workforce: 7.5 practising nurses per 1,000 in 2023, the lowest since 2010 and below the EU average. The National Health Strategy's 2:1 nurse-to-doctor target, moved from 2020 to 2025, is unlikely to be met. Physician density is 4.6 per 1,000, above the EU's 4.3, yet the OECD expects "a severe shortage of health professionals" in future. — [OECD Country Health Profile 2025](https://www.oecd.org/content/dam/oecd/en/publications/reports/2025/12/country-health-profile-2025-country-notes_7e72146d/lithuania_48218446/25bc0724-en.pdf)
- Projected shortages by 2032: 269 family doctors, 207 internal medicine doctors, 146 paediatricians. The Seimas approved extra state-funded residency places on condition that junior doctors work five years in a region lacking health professionals. The Junior Doctors Association opposes this and plans a Constitutional Court challenge, arguing that regions need conditions that make doctors want to stay. — [Euronews Health, 18 Jun 2026](https://www.euronews.com/health/2026/06/18/lithuanias-plan-to-send-newly-trained-doctors-to-rural-areas-sparks-backlash)
- Teaching (see section 5): about 300–458 unfilled teacher posts around September 2026, and the ministry projects up to 6,000 teachers could be lacking by 2031. — [tv3.lt – švietimo](https://www.tv3.lt/gaires/svietimo); [LRT](https://www.lrt.lt/naujienos/svietimas/45/2975229/mokytoju-truksta-ne-tik-lietuvoje-ka-daro-kitos-salys)
- Schools also lack psychologists: Education Ministry data show over 300 schools without one (LRT, 2026). — [LRT mediateka](https://www.lrt.lt/mediateka/irasas/2000806030/mokyklose-truksta-psichologu-smsm-duomenimis-specialisto-neturejo-per-300-mokyklu)

### Inferences
- Shortages are structural: the working-age population shrinks 27–30% by 2050. They hit sectors that cannot easily be offshored or automated, such as care, transport, teaching and construction. Those sectors are also the ones most dependent on Ukrainian, Belarusian and Central Asian workers, so quota and security constraints bind there first.
- The health workforce problem is mainly about nurses and geography, not total doctor numbers. Coercive allocation of doctors is legally contested, which suggests room for voluntary incentive packages: housing, spouse employment, career tracks.
- **Solution gaps:** (a) nurse recruitment, retention and upskilling (including foreign-trained nurses with fast credential recognition); (b) task-shifting and telemedicine for regional primary care; (c) training and retention programmes for older workers; (d) automation and productivity tools for logistics and construction SMEs.

### Gaps
- **Not retrieved because the search budget was exhausted:** Bank of Lithuania analyses of labour shortages, wage growth and potential growth; average gross and net wage figures for 2025–2026 and wage growth rates; unemployment and vacancy rates; shortages in IT/ICT; employer survey data on hiring difficulty. The report writer should treat the wage and economic-impact part of this question as unanswered beyond the OECD/IMF qualitative statements.
- The official 2026 Shortage Occupations List from the Employment Service (Užimtumo tarnyba) was not retrieved.

## 5. Education: school network consolidation, teacher shortages and ageing teachers, learning outcomes, university enrolment and study abroad

### Takeaway
Lithuania has the EU's oldest teaching workforce: 41.2% aged 55+ in 2023 and 58.4% aged 50+ per Eurostat (Oct 2026), with an average age of 51 vs the OECD's 45. Roughly 300–460 teacher vacancies go unfilled each September, mostly in maths and Lithuanian and in regional schools. The ministry projects a shortfall of up to 6,000 teachers by 2031. Network consolidation is under way through minimum class-size rules backed by EU recovery funds. The rules have been softened repeatedly after municipal pushback, and some municipalities, such as Lazdijai, plan no closures to 2030. PISA 2022 results are around the OECD average and stable since 2018.

### Cited Findings
**Teacher age and shortages**
- 41.2% of teachers were aged 55+ in 2023, the highest in the EU (EU average 25.1%). - Eurostat (released for World Teachers' Day, Oct 2026): Lithuania has the highest share of teachers aged 50+ in the EU at 58.4%, ahead of Portugal (55.6%) and Italy (54.3%). — [LSM (Latvian public media), 5 Oct 2026](https://eng.lsm.lv/article/society/education/05.10.2026-more-than-half-of-teachers-are-over-50-years-old-in-the-baltics.a666244/); [Eunews, 5 Oct 2026](https://www.eunews.it/en/2026/10/05/europes-teaching-staff-is-ageing-4-in-10-are-over-50/)

**School network consolidation**

- PISA 2022 means: maths 475 (OECD 472), reading 472 (OECD 476), science about 484 (aggregator). — [ŠMSM release, Dec 2023](https://smsm.lrv.lt/en/news-2/news-1/lithuanian-pupils-achievements-in-line-with-average-of-most-advanced-oecd-countries); [TheGlobalEconomy – science](https://www.theglobaleconomy.com/Lithuania/pisa_science_scores/)
- Results in all three domains were about the same as in 2018. **Higher education**
- 2026 general admission (LAMA BPO): main-round applications ran 1 June–23 July 2026, with two supplementary rounds in August. This is the procedure only; no applicant numbers were obtained. — [LAMA BPO – 2026 dates](https://lamabpo.lt/patvirtintos-2026-m-bendrojo-priemimo-datos/)

### Inferences
- About 58% of teachers are over 50, so roughly half the workforce reaches retirement age within 10–15 years. Pupil numbers are falling too: the 2025 birth cohort is about 17.5k, against about 30k in the late 2000s. The result is a paradox of shortage amid surplus, with simultaneous teacher oversupply in shrinking rural schools and shortages in maths/STEM and in growing suburbs. The EAG 2026 chapter title and the Employment Service's "deficit and surplus" framing ([paninfo.lt](https://paninfo.lt/uzimtumo-tarnyba-mokytoju-situacija-pries-rugseji-ir-deficitas-ir-perteklius/?pdf=79272)) point the same way.
- Repeated softening of class-size minimums (exceptions, municipal co-funding, a possible return to 12–15) shows that school closure is politically toxic locally. Consolidation is proceeding slowly, keeping small, costly schools open while teacher supply erodes.
- **Solution gaps:** (a) shared or remote teaching of maths, physics and IT across small rural schools (live online classes, regional teacher pools); (b) fast-track teacher certification for career-changers and returnees; (c) school transport and dorm solutions where gymnasiums close; (d) a mental-health support service for schools that cannot hire psychologists (over 300 schools); (e) Lithuanian-language support for returnee and migrant children.

- No count was obtained of schools closed or merged in 2024–2026, and no figure for national pupil numbers or their projected decline.
- No teacher salary data for 2025–2026 was obtained.
- PISA 2025 results are due in December 2026 and are not yet available.

## 6. Policies: diaspora and return programmes, family benefits, housing support, migration policy, regional development, and evidence of effectiveness

### Takeaway
Lithuania has many instruments: universal child benefit (€129.5/month in 2026), a birth grant rising to €1,036 from June 2026, return-migration coordination, a single foreign-worker quota, and doctor-to-region residency obligations. Independent reviews call these fragmented, project-based and weakly evaluated. The Seimas Research Department notes the 2018–2030 Demography, Migration and Integration Policy Strategy implementation plan has not been updated since 2022. OSW concluded no policy specifically aimed at reversing depopulation has been implemented. Return migration has been net-positive for six years, but evidence linking this to government programmes is thin.

### Cited Findings
**Family and child benefits (2026)**
- Universal child benefit (vaiko pinigai): €129.5 per child per month from 1 Jan 2026, which is 1.75 × the basic social benefit. The basic social benefit itself was raised to €74 from €70. The benefit for large or low-income families and for children with disabilities, including the additional part, rose from €194.6 to €205.7 a month. — [Ministry of Social Security and Labour – FAQ](https://socmin.lrv.lt/lt/duk/?q=&cats=15&pv=12)
- One-off birth grant: raised from €770 to €814, then to €1,036 from 1 June 2026. From 1 June 2026, parents without social-insurance records can receive a childcare benefit of €444 a month. (Secondary site; confirm against the legal act.) — [tevu-darzelis.lt – Vaiko pinigai 2026](https://www.tevu-darzelis.lt/vaiko-pinigai-ir-kitos-ismokos-2026-m-kas-keisis/); [tevu-darzelis.lt](https://www.tevu-darzelis.lt/vaiko-pinigai/)

**Return migration and diaspora**
- The Seimas Research Department review "Grįžtamosios migracijos skatinimo politika ir jos perspektyvos Lietuvoje" (26/20, 2026) found an inter-institutional coordination system and "strong dynamics" of initiatives. However, measures are often project-based, small in scale and limited in duration and results. The implementation plan of the Demography, Migration and Integration Policy Strategy 2018–2030 has not been updated since 2022, raising questions about consistency. Poland, Hungary and Romania have less consolidated approaches. — [Seimas review 26/20](https://www.lrs.lt/sip/getFile3?p_fid=120220)
- Earlier government migration-policy guidelines acknowledged that Lithuania's return-promotion policy "has so far not been sufficiently effective". — [Government resolution (e-seimas)](https://e-seimas.lrs.lt/rs/legalact/TAD/fdd7ab00899c11e39d2dc0b0e08d5f21/format/ISO_PDF/); strategy text: [Demography, Migration and Integration Policy Strategy 2018–2030 (Seimas resolution)](https://e-seimas.lrs.lt/rs/legalact/TAD/fbb35e02c21811e883c7a8f929bfc500/format/ISO_PDF/)
- The Seimas Speaker (J. Olekas) called for bringing back over 100,000 emigrants. Reporting noted that tools exist but policy consistency is questioned. — [ve.lt](https://ve.lt/aktualijos/lietuva/olekas-ragina-susigrazinti-virs-100-tukst-emigrantu-priemoniu-yra-bet-kyla); [respublika.lt](https://www.respublika.lt/lt/naujienos/lietuva/kitos-lietuvos-zinios/populiacijos-nykimas-seimo-pirmininkas-ragina-susigrazinti-virs-100-tukst-emigrantu/)
- A Seimas analytical review on diaspora groups and their needs (26/56, 2026) exists; content not retrieved. — [lrs.lt](https://www.lrs.lt/sip/getFile3?p_fid=122167)
- Foreign Ministry: 2025 was the sixth consecutive year in which more citizens returned than left (18,155 vs 9,669). — [URM](https://www.urm.lt/naujienos/141/seserius-metus-is-eiles-i-lietuva-grizta-daugiau-pilieciu-nei-isvyksta:45511)
- OSW (Dec 2024): officials see depopulation as a serious threat, yet "no policy focused on counteracting these negative trends has been implemented". Families cite weak financial incentives, education, medical care and housing support. Efforts to curb emigration "have proven ineffective, partly due to insufficient support provided to returnees". — [OSW](https://www.osw.waw.pl/en/publikacje/osw-commentary/2024-12-03/lithuania-losing-people-without-a-fight)
- Lithuania 2050 (Kurk LT) foresight work includes a situation analysis of migration and integration policy (A. Alekna, 18 Apr 2025); content not retrieved. — [kurklt.lt PDF](https://data.kurklt.lt/wp-content/uploads/2025/05/Alekna-Augustas-Esamos-situacijos-analize-18.04.2025_LT2050-Migracija-ir-integracija.pdf)

**Migration policy**
- Since 2025: one national annual quota for third-country workers, a Shortage Occupations List and a high-value-added list with lower salary thresholds; Lithuanian-only customer service from 2026 (see section 2). — [LRT](https://www.lrt.lt/en/news-in-english/19/3006058/lithuania-nears-annual-foreign-worker-quota); [micenter.lt](https://micenter.lt/en/work-permit); [tv3.lt](https://www.tv3.lt/naujiena/lietuva/nuo-2026-uju-su-klientais-tik-lietuviskai-kas-laukia-uzsienieciu-lietuvoje-n1460100)

- Extra state-funded residency places with a five-year regional work obligation were approved, and the Junior Doctors Association is planning a Constitutional Court challenge. — [Euronews, Jun 2026](https://www.euronews.com/health/2026/06/18/lithuanias-plan-to-send-newly-trained-doctors-to-rural-areas-sparks-backlash)

**Education**
- RRF-funded school network rationalisation and the Millennium Schools programme (see section 5). — [EC ETM 2025](https://op.europa.eu/webpub/eac/education-and-training-monitor/en/country-reports/lithuania.html)

**International recommendations**
- OECD 2025: keep net migration positive; encourage return migration; improve foreign-worker attraction; employment of older workers; VET reform; private pensions; hospital network rationalisation. — [OECD Survey 2025](https://www.oecd.org/en/publications/oecd-economic-surveys-lithuania-2025_4abf1ea5-en.html)
- China-CEE (Mar 2026) reviews three policy levers: raising births, return migration and third-country immigration. — [China-CEE briefing](https://china-cee.eu/2026/03/05/lithuania-monthly-briefing-demographic-decline-in-lithuania-causes-consequences-and-policy-responses/)

### Inferences
- Cash family support is rising (child benefit up about 5.7% in 2026, birth grant up about 35% in 2026). Yet births kept falling to about 17.5k in 2025 and the TFR is about 1.0. Cash transfers alone are not reversing fertility decline. OSW's interviewees point to housing, childcare, health care and job security as the binding constraints.
- Return migration is the clearest policy-adjacent success: six net-positive years, about 18k returnees a year. The Seimas review's critique (project-based, no updated implementation plan since 2022) means causality cannot be credited to programmes. More likely drivers are Brexit, wage convergence and the post-2022 security environment (inference, not sourced).
- The coercive turn (mandatory regional service for doctors, Lithuanian-only service rule, foreign-worker caps) contrasts with OECD/IMF advice to attract and retain people. This is a policy-design gap that incentive-based products and services could fill.
- **Solution gaps:** (a) an independent evaluation and monitoring unit, or open-data dashboard, for demographic programmes, since none exists; (b) young-family housing and childcare solutions in regional towns (rental, cooperative or employer-provided housing); (c) integrated "one-stop" support for returnees and diaspora families; (d) voluntary regional-placement packages for doctors, nurses and teachers (housing, spouse jobs, loan forgiveness) as alternatives to compulsion.

### Gaps
- **Not retrieved (search budget exhausted):** the current parameters of the young-family housing support programme (Law on Support for the Acquisition or Rental of Housing, state-subsidised mortgage share for families buying outside the big cities), and its 2025–2026 budget and take-up; details of the "Global Lithuania" programme and the Lithuanian diaspora/return portals and their budgets; regional development programmes (e.g., Regional Development Programme, "Kurk Lietuvai"); and any parental leave changes in 2025–2026. These programmes exist (known from background, not verified this session), and their current design and evaluations need checking.
- No quantitative evaluation (e.g., a counterfactual impact study) of any return-migration or family-benefit measure was found.
- No information was gathered on the current (Ruginienė-led) government programme's demographic commitments for 2025–2026.

No polling data could be gathered. The search budget ran out before the polling searches (Vilmorus, Spinter, Baltijos tyrimai, Eurobarometer, attitudes to migrants) could run. This question is unanswered in these notes.

### Cited Findings
- Indirect signals only. Public and elite discourse in 2026 frames the issue as a "demographic pit" (demografinė duobė) and asks how many and which migrants the country needs, which suggests salience. The headlines carry no poll numbers. — [15min](https://www.15min.lt/gyvenimas/naujiena/ar-zinai/demografine-duobe-kai-kuriose-lietuvos-savivaldybese-mirsta-7-kartus-daugiau-zmoniu-negu-gimsta-1634-2620972); [alkas.lt, Sep 2026](https://alkas.lt/2026/09/16/kiek-ir-kokiu-migrantu-reikia-lietuvai-valstybes-kryptis/)
- A demographer quoted in Kauno diena argues "we must dare to say it: we will shrink" (headline; date not confirmed). — [Kauno diena](https://kauno.diena.lt/naujienos/lietuva/salies-pulsas/demografas-reikia-isdristi-pasakyti-mazesime-1743056)
- OSW (2024) reports families citing insufficient financial incentives, education, medical care and housing support as reasons for not having (more) children. This is qualitative, not a representative poll. — [OSW](https://www.osw.waw.pl/en/publikacje/osw-commentary/2024-12-03/lithuania-losing-people-without-a-fight)

### Inferences
- With no poll data, the report writer should not make claims about the share of Lithuanians worried about emigration or depopulation, or about attitudes toward Central Asian or Indian workers.

### Gaps
- Needed but not retrieved: representative polls on (a) emigration intentions among young people; (b) perceived top national problems (where demography usually ranks); (c) attitudes toward labour migrants by origin, e.g., the Lithuanian Social Research Centre / Ethnic Studies Institute "social distance" surveys and Eurobarometer; (d) reasons for low fertility (Generations and Gender Survey or similar); (e) returnees' satisfaction surveys.
