# KVK Faculty of Business writing methodology (2024)

The user is a student at **Klaipėdos valstybinė kolegija | Higher Education Institution (KVK)**, Faculty of Business, in Klaipėda, Lithuania. The faculty's methodology is **mandatory** for all written student work. Apply it to every written task.

- Full text: `references/methodology-full-text.md` (grep it for anything not covered here)
- Original PDF: `references/KVK_Business_Faculty_Writing_Methodology_2024.pdf`

## How to apply it

1. **Identify the type of work** (table below). It sets the structure, length and minimum number of sources.
2. **If the lecturer gave a template or worksheet** (e.g. a practical assignment with tables to fill in), keep the template's layout and fill it in. Still apply the rules on language, reasoning, citations and sources: cite sources in the text as (Author, year) and add a **Literature** list at the end.
3. **For a full paper** (report, essay, project, course paper, graduation paper), use the full structure, formatting and checklist below.
4. **Before delivering**, run the checklist at the end and tell the user what they still need to add: real data, names, signatures, the adviser, or page numbers once the text is final.

### Academic integrity (never break these)
- Follow the KVK Code of Academic Ethics. Plagiarism (using someone's text or ideas without a correct reference) is strictly forbidden.
- **Never invent sources.** Every cited work must be real and verifiable (author, year, title, publisher/journal, and a DOI/URL where available). If unsure about a detail, say so and ask the user to verify it.
- **Never fabricate empirical data** (survey results, interview answers, observations, company figures). If the user has no data, write the instrument (questionnaire, interview questions, observation protocol) and mark the results as placeholders for the user to fill in. Say this clearly.
- Turnitin limits for graduation papers: total overlap ≤ 20 %, ≤ 7 % with a single source. More than 50 % overlap (cited or not) makes a paper unoriginal. Paraphrase instead of quoting at length.

## Types of papers

| Type | Purpose / required parts | Length | Sources |
|---|---|---|---|
| Summary report | Detailed look at a theoretical issue based on literature. Objective, purposeful, informative. Group reports show each student's contribution. | 10–20 pages (individual) | 4–10, also in foreign languages |
| Essay | Short argumentative paper with a reasoned critical view: advantages and disadvantages, solutions, future directions. Parts: introduction (problem, intrigue), development (argumentation, quotes, images), conclusions, literature, annexes. | Set by the lecturer (smaller) | Set by the lecturer |
| Report (internship) | Describe the tasks done with examples (documents, calculations, interviews, observations). Present results visually (graphs, tables), without repeating the same data in both. | 10–15 pages | – |
| Reflection | Describe the experience (tasks, what and how you learned, feelings) and reflect on it (what went well or badly, difficulties, how you solved them, what to improve). Answer the lecturer's questions. | 1–5 pages | – |
| Project / integrated project | Applied research starting from a specific problem, like a course paper | Set by the lecturer | – |
| Article | Structured academic paper, in Lithuanian or English | 5–7 pages | – |
| Course paper | Theoretical or practical independent research | 25–30 pages without annexes | Theoretical: ≥ 20. Practical: ≥ 15 analysed. List ≥ 15 |
| Graduation paper | Original, publicly defended | 40–50 pages (60–80 for 2 students) | ≥ 25 |

Every written work has these parts: title page, summary, table of contents, list of tables and figures, introduction, development of the topic, conclusions, literature, annexes. All papers follow the formatting in Section 3 (below).

## Structure of course papers and graduation papers (in this order)

1. **Title page.** Course paper (Annex 6): FACULTY OF BUSINESS / DEPARTMENT OF … / TITLE OF THE COURSE PAPER / "Course paper" / Author (name, surname), signature, date / Adviser (position, academic degree, name, surname), signature, date / Klaipėda, year. A graduation paper (Annex 7) also gives "Professional bachelor's graduation paper", the study programme title, its state code and the field of studies. Use 12 pt. Faculty, department and paper titles in **bold capitals**. No page number.
2. **Table of contents.** Generated automatically in Times New Roman 12. Main parts (theoretical analysis, research methodology overview, practical part) in CAPITALS, subsections in lowercase. The list of annexes is in 10 pt. The ToC does not list itself.
3. **List of tables** and **List of figures.** Each on a separate page, generated automatically, e.g. "Table 1. Title ……… 11" and "Fig. 1 Title ……… 15". Leave out if there are none.
4. **Concept vocabulary** (optional, decided with the adviser). At most 2 pages. Each concept is defined in 1–2 sentences with a source. Use scientific sources only (articles, monographs, research reports), never textbooks or newspapers. For a definition you composed yourself, write "Source: compiled by the author based on …".
5. **Summary in Lithuanian** ("Santrauka") and **Summary in English.** Written last. Each is on its own page and is at most 1 page. Start with a continuous **bold** line: author's name and surname, paper title, study programme, Faculty of Business, Klaipėdos valstybinė kolegija. Then give the research problem, object, aim, methods, conclusions and suggestions, and **3–5 keywords**. Leave out citations, tables, figures and numbers.
6. **Introduction.** Contains:
   - **Relevance**: move from the world to Lithuania to the organisation. Name Lithuanian and foreign authors who studied the topic and the relevant legislation, say what is already known and what is new, and explain the practical relevance for the organisation.
   - **Research problem**: a gap or a practical problem, which may be phrased as problem questions.
   - **Research object**: a phenomenon or process, **never an organisation**. The organisation is the research context or location.
   - **Aim**: linked to the title and starting with an infinitive ("to analyse …", "to prepare …").
   - **Tasks**: 3–5, numbered, starting with active verbs (to analyse, determine, assess, compare, identify, prepare…). Methods are not tasks, so never write "to conduct a survey" or "to provide conclusions". Each practical subsection matches a task.
   - **Methods** of data collection and analysis, listed in one line without numbering.
   - For two authors, state each author's contribution.
7. **1. THEORETICAL PART** (analytical title in capitals). See the writing rules below.
8. **2. OVERVIEW OF THE RESEARCH METHODOLOGY** (at least 2–3 pages). See below.
9. **3. PRACTICAL PART** (analytical title in capitals). See below.
10. **Conclusions and recommendations.** 1–2 pages, numbered by task. Tasks about theory share a single conclusion. Each conclusion that reveals a problem is followed by a proposal to solve it, and every proposal must be justified in the practical part. Don't copy the subsection summaries.
11. **Literature.** See below.
12. **Annexes.** See below.

The list of tables and figures, vocabulary, summaries, introduction, conclusions, literature and annexes are **not numbered**. Sections 1, 2 and 3 each start on a new page.

### Table of contents and headings
- Each section has **at least 2 subsections** (2.1., 2.2.). Deeper levels are allowed (3.1.1.).
- Every numbered subsection is **at least 1 page** and has three parts: introductory sentences, the main part, and a summary.
- Titles are original, written by the author and never copied from sources. Practical subsection titles match the tasks and focus on what the author did: "… analysis", "… assessment", "… improvement", "… opportunities".
- The theory and the practical part must cover the same aspects.
- If the company allowed its name, use it in the title, contents and text. Otherwise describe it by field ("a transport company", "a hotel", "a trading company"). **Never write "Company X" or "Institution Y".** Permissions go in the annexes (Annex 3 for use of the name, Annex 5 for permission to research). Publicly available data (e.g. Statistics Lithuania, the state budget) needs no permission.

### Theoretical part: writing rules
- Analyse relevant, up-to-date scientific, regulatory, professional and statistical information that supports the practical part.
- **Compare and summarise sources** and point out similarities and differences. Don't just list them.
- **Cite at least three authors on every page.** Every quote, paraphrase or borrowed piece of data needs a reference.
- Avoid long quotations and paraphrase instead.
- Keep the author's own opinion separate from the sources, e.g. "After analysing the information provided in scientific and professional sources, it can be stated that …".
- Each subsection (2.1., 2.2.) ends with a **2–3 sentence summary** tied to its title, optionally with a sentence linking it to the next part. Smaller parts (2.1.1.) need no summary.
- The theoretical part must be about the same length as the practical part and **not longer**. Cut theory that has no link to the research.
- Tables or figures that fill a whole A4 page go to the annexes.
- Practice-oriented sources are fine: opinions of experts and managers in the professional press or analytical programmes. Discuss their reliability with the adviser.

### Research methodology overview (Section 2)
- **The organisation**: nature and scale of its activities, type, management structure and achievements, limited to what relates to the object. Don't copy its advertising and don't write "we are…" or "our credo". Without written consent, give no identifying details (address, unique features).
- **The object**: justify it, describe the current situation, and state the limitations.
- **Methods**: give exact method names, most important first, and justify each choice. Say whether the questionnaire or test was made by the author or by others, and describe the instrument's content and structure.
- **Process**: the stages, with exact start and end dates, the problems that came up, and how they were solved.
- **Population and sample.** Sampling is probability-based (simple random, systematic, stratified, cluster) or non-probability (expert, quota, convenience, snowball). A standard error of 5 % at probability 0.95 is normal. Use the **Paniotto formula** n = 1 / (Δ² + 1/N) (formula (1)). Examples at ±5 %, ±7 %, ±10 %: N=100 gives 81/67/51, N=200 gives 134/101/67, N=300 gives 172/121/76, N=500 gives 222/145/83, N=1 000 gives 286/169/91, N=2 000 gives 333/185/95, N=5 000 gives 370/196/98, N=10 000 gives 385/200/99 (Kasnauskienė, 2010). An online calculator is at factus.lt.
- **Minimum samples for non-probability sampling**: observation 5; interview 4–6 informants; written survey at least 60 respondents (60–80 for two groups); sociometric study one group of 5–11; experiment at least 5.
- **Ethics**: informed consent, explanation of any risks, confidentiality, anonymity and privacy. Informants are coded D1, D2 and so on.
- **Response rate** (questionnaires handed out and returned), the software used (MS Excel, SPSS, NVivo…), and finally the respondents' demographics.
- Method notes:
  - **Written or online survey**: the introduction says who is running it, why, the aim, how results will be used, and guarantees anonymity. Keep it short with easy questions first and demographics at the end. Use open, closed and semi-closed questions. Suggested platforms: Google Forms, apklausa.lt, SurveyMonkey…
  - **Structured or semi-structured interview**: open questions that cover every theory subsection. Plan for 0.5–1.5 h per interview, recorded with consent. Word-for-word transcripts become interview protocols in the annexes (Annex 10). Analyse with a table of **categories, subcategories and supporting statements** (Annex 11).
  - **Focus group**: 6–12 people who share a trait, led by a moderator, 1.5–2 h. Analyse it like an interview.
  - **Case study**: data from several sources. Analysis may be interpretive, structural or reflective.
  - **Document content analysis**: explain how the documents were selected and why the sample size fits. Use classical or content analysis, and name any software used.
  - **Observation**: give the goal, plan, criteria and indicators (observation matrix) and the protocol (Annex 12). Observe more than once.
  - **Action research**: a cycle of identifying the problem, planning, acting and evaluating, with reflection.

### Practical part (Section 3)
- It must be research, not description: a critical, evaluative analysis of the problems and data that ends in conclusions and proposals with practical value.
- Use the neutral, impersonal form: "it was determined", "observed", "proposed", "recommended". Write "the results of the research show …", never "my research". **Abstract suggestions like "to strive", "to aim", "to improve", "better" or "more" are meaningless.** Proposals must be concrete: who does what, how, and when.
- Comment on and analyse **every table and figure** without repeating its numbers in the text. Text and visuals complement each other. Don't overload the text with visuals.
- Subsection summaries highlight the empirical results. Linking sentences between parts keep the text continuous.

### Literature list
- Heading **Literature**, on a new page at the end.
- Arrange entries **alphabetically**, **without numbering**. Sources in Latin script (Lithuanian, English, German…) come first, then Cyrillic ones. Keep every source in its original language.
- Format entries by **APA** (7th ed.) or **LST ISO 690:2010** and use the same style throughout. Several works by one author go newest first (2024, 2019…).
- Minimums: **at least 15 sources** (course paper) or **at least 25** (graduation paper). **At least ⅓ by foreign authors**, half of them in a foreign language. **At least ⅔ younger than 10 years.**
- List only what you cited or discussed. Leave out things you read but didn't use.
- Not allowed: other students' theses (bachelor's or master's), **Wikipedia, Investopedia** and similar online encyclopedias, and online sources missing the author, year or title. Avoid news portals (delfi.lt, 15min.lt). Use textbooks only in exceptional, justified cases. Base the work on official statistics and scientific publications.

Entry templates:
- Book, 1 author: `Vijeikis, J. (2014). Demokratija ir ugdymas. Klaipėda: UAB Baltic Printing House.`
- Book, 2 authors: `Kriščiūnas, B. and Dėdelė, A. (2014). Aplinkos inžinerija. Kaunas: Versus aureus.`
- Book, 3 or more authors: `Surname, N., Surname, N. and Surname, N. (year). Title: Subtitle. Place: Publisher.`
- Revised edition: `Aron, E. (2020). Title (3rd rev. ed.). Vilnius: Sofoklis.`
- Collective author: `Ministry of Education and Science of the Republic of Lithuania. (2016). Title. Place: Publisher.`
- No author: `Tarptautinių žodžių žodynas (2013). Vilnius: Alma Littera.`
- Chapter: `Surname, N. (year). Chapter title. In N. Surname (Ed.), Book Title (p. 53–71). Place: Publisher.`
- E-book with DOI: `… Place: Publisher. https://doi.org/10.xxxx`. Without DOI: `… Access on the internet: https://…`
- Journal article: `Surname, N. and Surname, N. (year). Article title. Journal Title, Vol. X, No. Y, p. 23–42.` Add `https://doi.org/…` or `Access on the internet: URL` when online.
- Newspaper online: `Surname, N. (18 July 2017). Title. Daily. [accessed on 10 December 2017]. Access on the internet: URL`
- Website: `Surname, N. / Institution. (year). Page title. [accessed on DD Month YYYY]. Access on the internet: URL`
- Legal act: `Parliament of the Republic of Lithuania. Law on Higher Education and Research (30 April 2009, No. XI-242). [accessed on …]. Access on the internet: https://www.e-tar.lt/…`
- Dissertation: `Surname, N. (year). Title: doctoral dissertation (manuscript). University. [accessed on …]. Access on the internet: URL`
- Secondary source: list the secondary source you actually read.
- For anything else, use the APA reference examples: https://apastyle.apa.org/instructional-aids/reference-examples.pdf

### Annexes
- Annexes hold material the student prepared: statistics, visuals, primary research data, questionnaires, transcripts and permissions.
- Number them in the order they are referred to in the text, and refer to every one of them.
- Each annex starts on a new page. If an annex runs over several pages, mark the later pages as a continuation.
- Put the paper title and annex number in the upper right corner in 10 pt. Annexes have **no page numbers**.

## In-text citations (author–year)
- `(Dewey, 2014)` or `Dewey (2014) argues that …`. If the name is already in the sentence, give only the year.
- Exact quote: in italics **or** in 'quotation marks' (pick one and use it throughout), with the page: `(Adler, 2023, p. 229)`, `Kavaliauskaitė (2023, p. 109) states that '…'`. Mark omissions with `[...]`. A quote must match the original exactly.
- Two authors: `(Paužuolienė and Pikturnaitė, 2022)`.
- 3–5 authors: list all of them the first time, `(Žvirelienė, Žostautienė and Kuvykaitė, 2014)`, and after that `(Žvirelienė et al., 2014)`.
- 6 or more authors: always `(Anužienė et al., 2024)`.
- Several sources: alphabetical, separated by semicolons: `(Aksamitaitė, 2008; Banelienė, 2015; Išoraitė, 2016)`.
- Collective author: the first time give the full name with the abbreviation in square brackets, `(Lithuanian Marketing Association [LiMA], 2024)`, and after that `(LiMA, 2024)`.
- No author: the title in *italics*, `(Žinių enciklopedija, 2019)`.
- Same author and year: `(Little, 2023a)`, `(Little, 2023b)`. Same author, different years: `(Burvytė, 2017, 2018)`. Same surname, different authors: add initials, `(M. Jurevičienė, 2016; D. Jurevičienė, 2017)`.
- Secondary source (only when the original is unavailable): `(Ah Fook and McNeill, 2020, as cited from Sidorčukaitė and Šakytė-Statnickė, 2021)`.
- Legislation: cite the title, `(Law on … of the Republic of Lithuania, 2018)`.
- Websites: cite like printed works, `(Eurostat, 2024)`, `(Official Statistics Portal, 2024)`. Full details go in the Literature list.
- In Lithuanian text, foreign male surnames are grammatically adapted (see the VLKK recommendations). References keep the original spelling.

## Formatting (Section 3)
- Language: Lithuanian, or a foreign language when studying in one. Use correct, coherent academic language.
- A4 paper, printed on one side. **Margins: top 2 cm, bottom 2 cm, left 3 cm, right 1 cm.**
- **Times New Roman 12 pt**, **1.5 line spacing** (tables and figures single-spaced), **justified**, **first-line indent 1.5 cm**.
- **Header**: the author's name and surname on the left and the paper title on the right. **Footer**: the college name and year on the left. Both are 10 pt Times New Roman in **grey**, separated from the text by a thin ½ pt grey solid line.
- **Page numbers**: Arabic numerals, centred in the footer, 12 pt, without dots. The title page is not numbered, and neither are the annexes.
- **Chapter titles in BOLD UPPERCASE; subsection titles in bold sentence case**, 12 pt. Leave one blank line between a title and the text. Number sections consecutively in Arabic numerals (1., 1.1., 1.1.1.).
- **Tables**: the title goes **above**, centred, 12 pt, single spacing, e.g. `Table 1. Title`. Table text is 10 pt single-spaced. **Figures**: the title goes **below**, centred, 12 pt, e.g. `Fig. 1 Title`. Figure text may be 10–12 pt. Tables and figures are numbered separately, each throughout the whole paper.
- **Source line** under every table and figure, 10 pt, centred:
  - `Source: compiled by the author according to the data of Statistics Lithuania 2021.`
  - `Source: compiled by the author according to research data, 2024.`
  - `Source: Author, N. (year). Title. Place, p. 25.` for a table or figure copied from another work.
- **Dates**: `2022-05-10`. In Word, use Ctrl+Shift+hyphen for a non-breaking hyphen. Or write them as `5 June 2022`.
- **Units**: abbreviated in the text (18 pct.) and as symbols in tables and graphs. Use a non-breaking space (Ctrl+Shift+Space) between a number and its unit.
- **Formulas**: use Word's Equation editor and number them at the right edge, e.g. `(2)`. After the formula put a semicolon, then "where:" and an explanation of each symbol, and give the source.
- Refer to the college as **Klaipėdos valstybinė kolegija | Higher Education Institution (KVK)**.

### .docx implementation values (docx-js / OOXML)
- Page A4 is 11906 × 16838 twips. Margins: top and bottom 1134, left 1701, right 567.
- Body: Times New Roman, size 24 half-points, `line: 360` (1.5), justified, first-line indent 851.
- Table text: size 20, single spacing. Captions: size 24. Source lines: size 20, centred.
- Header and footer: size 20, grey (e.g. `808080`). The header has a ½ pt grey bottom border (`sz: 4`) and the footer a ½ pt grey top border.
- Page number: a centred PAGE field, size 24. Use a different first page for the title page.
- Headings use the built-in Heading 1 and Heading 2 styles (bold, 12 pt, Heading 1 uppercase) so the automatic ToC works. Sections 1, 2 and 3 start with a page break.

## Defence presentation (course and graduation papers)
- The talk takes 7–10 minutes with **10–12 slides**, in this order:
  1. Institution, faculty, programme, author, title
  2. Research problem and object
  3. Aim and tasks
  4. Methodology
  5. Results for each task, at most 2 slides per task, shown in tables or graphs
  6. Final slides: summary, conclusions, suggestions
- Every slide has a title. Use the same design on all slides, with at most 7–8 bullet lines per slide. Text 24–28 pt, headings 32 pt. Keep animations modest and use no sounds. The KVK template is available on moodle.kvk.lt.
- At the defence: business attire, greet the audience and introduce yourself, speak clearly and calmly to the audience, and answer criticism respectfully, basing your answers on the research.

## Checklist before delivering any KVK work
- [ ] Correct type, structure and length for this kind of work. A lecturer's template is kept as it is.
- [ ] Introduction: relevance, problem, object (not an organisation), aim (infinitive), 3–5 numbered tasks with active verbs, methods in one line.
- [ ] Each numbered subsection is at least 1 page, with an introduction, main part and 2–3 sentence summary. Theory is not longer than the practical part.
- [ ] At least 3 authors cited per page of theory. Sources are compared, not just listed. The author's own opinion is marked.
- [ ] Every citation is a real, verifiable source in the correct author–year format. No Wikipedia, news portals or student theses.
- [ ] Literature list: alphabetical, not numbered, APA / LST ISO 690, the minimum number of sources, ⅓ foreign, ⅔ under 10 years old.
- [ ] Tables and figures are numbered, titled (tables above, figures below), have a source line, and are commented on in the text.
- [ ] Conclusions are numbered by task, each with a concrete proposal (never "to strive" or "to improve"), 1–2 pages.
- [ ] Summaries in Lithuanian and English, each at most 1 page, with a bold first line and 3–5 keywords (full papers).
- [ ] Formatting: margins 2/2/3/1 cm, TNR 12, 1.5 spacing, justified, 1.5 cm indent, header and footer, page numbers.
- [ ] No invented data. Placeholders and things the user must add or verify are listed clearly.
